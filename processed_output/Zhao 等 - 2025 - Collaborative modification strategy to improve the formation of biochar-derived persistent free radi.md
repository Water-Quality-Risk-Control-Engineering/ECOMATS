# Collaborative modifcation strategy to improve the formation of biochar-derived persistent free radicals for aniline removal via peroxymonosulfate activation

Zilong Zhao1,2\*, Shuting Zhu1 , Shuyu Qi1 , Ting Zhou1 , Yang Yang1 , Feng Wang1 , Qi Han1 , Wenyi Dong1,3,4,5, Hongjie Wang1,3,4,5 and Feiyun Sun1

# Abstract

This study explores a novel approach to biochar modifcation aimed at increasing persistent free radical (PFR) formation on biochar surfaces, thereby enhancing aniline removal via peroxymonosulfate (PMS) activation. By adjusting pyrolysis temperatures and doping ratios, optimal conditions were established. Spearman’s analysis highlighted the importance of $\mathsf { C } { = } \mathsf { C }$ bonds, the $1 _ { \mathrm { D } } / 1 _ { \mathrm { G } }$ ratio, and pyridinic N in generating PFRs. The modifed biochar derived at $5 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ (MB500), in conjunction with the PMS system demonstrated impressive efciency, achieving $9 2 \%$ aniline removal within 30 min. Detailed adsorption tests and active species detection indicated that aniline degradation occurred through both direct oxidation by PFRs and indirect oxidation by reactive species, particularly superoxide radicals $( \mathsf { O } _ { 2 } { \cdot } ^ { - } )$ . Furthermore, the synergistic efects of heteroatom nitrogen and ${ \sf N a } _ { 2 } { \sf C O } _ { 3 }$ modifcations signifcantly impacted PFR formation and stability. These fndings provide valuable insights into the mechanisms of PFR-mediated catalytic oxidation, highlighting the key roles of pyridinic rings, with or without oxygenated groups, in enhancing catalytic performance of biochar. This research advances the understanding of biochar surface chemistry and presents an efective strategy for developing high-performance biochar-based catalysts for environmental remediation, addressing the limitations of unmodifed biochar through targeted surface modifcations.

# 1 Introduction

In recent decades, biochar produced through hydrothermal carbonization or pyrolysis of biomass under anaerobic conditions has gained considerable global attention for its potential in environmental remediation applications (Chen et al. 2024; Wen et al. 2024). Its high specifc surface area, well-developed pore structure, and unique chemical properties make biochar an efective catalyst for activating peroxymonosulfate (PMS) to treat recalcitrant organic pollutants in water, including pharmaceuticals, personal care products, endocrine disruptors, volatile organic compounds, and disinfection by-products (Qi et al. 2020; Wu et al. 2021). Research studies indicate that biochar can activate persulfate through multiple active sites, including defective structures, graphitic confgurations, oxygenated functional groups, metal species, and persistent free radicals (PFRs) (Luo et  al. 2019; Qi et  al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2023; Zhao et al. 2021). Among these, PFRs, which could be formed from the partially pyrolyzed organic components of the biomass feedstocks, and/or the stabilized complexation of organic free radicals with transition metals, are one of the most common redox-active sites on biochar. Unlike transient radicals such as ⋅OH and $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \cdot - }$ , these solid-phase radicals display higher stability, persisting from hours to years (Ruan et al. 2019). Due to their delocalized unpaired electrons and excellent electron conductivity, PFRs on the biochar surface can transfer single electrons to oxidants, such as ${ \mathrm { H S O } } _ { 5 } { } ^ { - }$ , $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ,$ and molecular $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ . Tis process generates reactive oxygen species (ROSs) that can efectively oxidize a range of contaminants, including 2-chlorobiphenyl, diethyl phthalate, 1,3-dichloropropene, sulfamethoxazole, and As(III) (Yuan et al. 2022; Liu et al. 2024a, b; Liu et al. 2020; Luo et al. 2021; Zhu et al. 2023). PFRs can also directly induce the breakdown of organic pollutants like nitrophenol, and mediate the direct reduction of $\mathrm { C r } ( \mathrm { V I } )$ in aqueous solutions (Hu et al. 2024). It is clear that PFRs play a crucial role in pollutant removal, and in some cases, they even dominate the mechanism (Zhang et al. 2024). However, the importance of PFRs in these processes was often overlooked and has only begun to receive more attention over the past fve years (Luo et al. 2021).

Due to the inherent limitations of unmodifed biochar in catalytically removing organic pollutants (Zhao et  al. 2021), numerous studies have employed various surface modifcation techniques, including physical/chemical activation and hetero-atom doping (Luo et  al. 2021), to enhance the catalytic activity of biochar and broaden its environmental applications. Tese strategies aim to create specifc structures, increase the number of active sites and surface defects, and enhance the specifc surface area, pore size distribution, surface charge, functional groups, and electrical properties of biochar catalysts (Luo et al. 2019; Zhao et al. 2021; Zhu et al. 2018). Tis signifcantly enhances PMS activation through radical and nonradical mechanisms, leading to more efective pollutant removal. However, most studies rarely address the impact of these modifcation methods on PFRs formation and their contribution to the reaction activity of the modifed biochar. Understanding these aspects is crucial, as it provides valuable theoretical and practical insights into regulating the properties of biochar, improving its efciency, and gaining a deeper understanding of the underlying reaction mechanisms.

It is well established that the presence of heterogeneous materials in biomass can signifcantly afect the behaviour of biomass during thermochemical conversion processes (Huang et  al. 2022), leading to variations in the generation and characteristics of PFRs on diferent biochar surfaces. Since the factors infuencing the concentrations and types of PFRs difer across studies, mechanisms by which individual physical/chemical activation or hetero-atom doping infuences PFR formation on biochar surfaces remain controversial. For example, $\mathrm { H N O } _ { 3 }$ activation alters the surface characteristics of pyrolyzed biochar and increases the number of biochar-PFRs, particularly the oxygen-centered radicals (Luo et  al. 2019). However, it does not substantially increase the concentration of PFRs in hydrochar (Yan et al. 2018). Moreover, heteroatom doping is believed to alter the distribution and content of C and O atoms within the biochar structure, thereby infucencing the concentration and type of PFRs (Yu et al. 2020). Some researchers suggest that heterocycles in biomass could regulate unpaired electrons on adjacent $\mathsf { s p } ^ { 2 }$ -hybridized carbon substrates, facilitating PFRs formation within biochar (Yu et al. 2020). However, other studies suggest that nitrogen from urea can replace oxygen in $- \mathrm { O H }$ groups within biochar to form $\mathrm { - N H } _ { 2 } ,$ which may consume PFRs. Tis is supported by observations of negative correlation between PFR intensity and nitrogen content (Bi et  al. 2022; Huang et  al. 2022; Wu et  al. 2021). With the combined modifcations, a synergistic activation efect is often observed. For example, the combined activation of an alkali chemical like KOH and N-dopants such as urea, can promote the formation of additional microporous structures and enhance the selectivity of catalytic reactions (Lin et  al. 2023). Tese interactions undoubtly add complexity to studying PFRs. Given the intricate, non-linear relationship between biomass carbonization conditions and PFR characteristics, it is essential to understand how PFR occurrence relates to elemental composition, microstructure, and surface properties during carbonization. Establishing these connections enhances the current understanding of biochar-PFR formation and reaction characteristics under various modifcation conditions (Chen et  al. 2024). To our knowledge, any similar study has not been reported as yet.

In this study, corncob, sodium carbonate $\left( \mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { C O } _ { 3 } \right)$ as an alkali chemical, and various nitrogen precursors were ball-milled and co-pyrolyzed to synthesize modifed biochars. Te efects of pyrolysis temperature and modifcation methods on PFR formation in biochar were systematically investigated. Te Spearman analysis was used to assess correlations between elemental composition, structural characteristics, functional group composition, and PFR formation during co-pyrolysis, clarifying the key factors infuencing PFR formation under combined modifcation with heteroatoms and ${ \mathrm { N a } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { C O } } _ { 3 } ,$ as well as possible synergistic efects. Te catalytic and adsorption performance of the modifed biochar was then evaluated, using aniline as the target contaminant due to its widespread industrial use and substantial threat to ecosystems and human health upon enetring water. Te potential mechanism by which biochar-PFRs activate PMS for aniline degradation was further examined by indentifying reactive species and analyzing their correlation analysis with aniline removal. Te fndings from this study provide fundamental insights into PFR generation on biochar surfaces during biomass co-pyrolysis with diferent modifers and highlight the predominant roles of these PFRs in the removal of recalcitrant organic pollutants, supporting the development of biochar tailored for specifc environmental applications.

# 3 Results and discussion

3.1 Impact evaluation of PFRs formation during pyrolysis Te formation and evolution of PFRs during pyrolysis are signifcantly infuenced by the pyrolysis temperature (Chen et  al. 2024; Zhang et  al. 2023). As illustrated in Fig. 1, the concentration of PFRs progressively increased with rising pyrolysis temperatures, reaching a maximum of $5 . 9 1 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 9 }$ spins $\mathbf { g } ^ { - 1 }$ at $5 0 0 ~ \mathrm { { ^ \circ C } }$ . Tis peak concentration is consistent with previously reported values, which typically range from $1 0 ^ { 1 6 }$ to $1 0 ^ { 1 9 }$ spins $\mathbf { g } ^ { - 1 }$ (Luo et al. 2021). Beyond this temperature, the concentration of PFRs dropped sharply or even vanished, likely due to the breakdown and reorganization of the organic structure, as well as a decrease in the availability of phenolic compound precursors (Luo et  al. 2021; Yang et al. 2016; Zhang et al. 2023). Tis pattern difers from the typical changes observed in the pore structure of biochar during pyrolysis (see Fig. S2). With increasing pyrolysis temperature, biochar undergoes thermoplastic transformations, forming macropores and micropores. Tis process resulted in a steady rise in specifc surface area and pore volume, reaching $1 2 2 4 . 5 8 \ \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 } \ \mathrm { g } ^ { - 1 }$ and $0 . 9 9 \ \mathrm { c m } ^ { 3 } \ \mathrm { g } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively. In contrast, the average pore size consistently exhibited mesoporous characteristics (ranging from 3.22 to $4 1 . 5 2 \ \mathrm { n m } )$ and gradually decreased (see Table  S2). Regarding the $\mathbf { g }$ -value, pyrolysis temperatures between 300 and $4 0 0 \ \mathrm { ^ \circ C }$ , yielded values above 2.0040, indicating a predominance of oxygen-centered semiquinone-type PFRs. However, at temperatures between $5 0 0 ~ \mathrm { ~ \textdegree C }$ and $7 0 0 ~ \mathrm { ~ } ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ , the $\mathbf { g }$ -value fell within the range of 2.0030 to 2.0040, suggesting a co-prevalence of both carbon and oxygen-centered phenoxy PFRs (Bi et al. 2022). Previous studies indicate that with increasing temperature and pyrolysis duration, the types of PFRs on the biochar surface transition from semiquinone-type radicals to phenoxy-type radicals and eventually to cyclopentadienyl radicals (Huang et al. 2022; Luo et al. 2021; Ruan et al. 2019). Tis transformation coincides with the gradual reduction of oxygenated functional groups, including phenols, alcohols, lactones, aldehydes, ketones, and carboxylic acids, as confrmed by FTIR analyses and Boehm titration results (Fig. S3). Given that the highest concentration of PFRs was observed at $5 0 0 ~ \mathrm { ^ { \circ } C }$ , the MB500 sample was chosen as the representative sample for subsequent analysis.

Further investigation examined the impact of biochar modifcation methods on PFRs formation. As shown in Fig.  2a, after pyrolysis at $5 0 0 ~ \mathrm { ^ { \circ } C } ,$ , the concentration of PFRs on unmodifed corncob biochar was $4 . 0 8 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 9 }$ spins $\mathbf { g } ^ { - 1 }$ , primarily consisting of carbon- and oxygencentered free radicals. Biochar modifcation notably afected the concentration of PFRs, while having little impact on the types of radicals present (Fig.  2b). Under modifcation conditions, the $\mathbf { g }$ -value typically showed a slight increase, indicating a higher proportion of oxygen-centered PFRs. Previous studies have reported that oxygen-centered PFRs are more reactive than carboncentered PFRs, particularly in generating reactive species such as $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \cdot - }$ radicals (Yu et al. 2020). Unlike the adverse efects induced by $\mathrm { N a _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ activation, urea addition alone tended to facilitate the production of more PFRs. Interestingly, the combined use of $\mathrm { N a _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ and urea resulted in a signifcant synergistic enhancement of PFRs concentration. As the mass ratio of urea to $\mathrm { N a _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ increased from 1:4 to 2:1, the PFRs concentration frst rose and then decreased, peaking at a mass ratio of 3:4. According to literature, $\mathrm { N a _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ lowers the activation energy during pyrolysis, promoting the condensation of small organic molecules into conjugated aromatic rings. Tis process also generates numerous defective edges along the biochar boundaries, introducing hydrogen and oxygen functionalities (Huang et  al. 2022; Qi et  al. 2020; Tao et  al. 2020). A high proportion of $\mathrm { N a _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ in the mixture may lead to sodium ions occupying active sites on the benzene ring, thereby reducing the potential for alkyl or aliphatic side chains to participate in cyclization reactions.

Tis interference inhibits the condensation of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), which in turn limits the availability of precursor compounds essential for the formation of PFRs. Consequently, an excess of $\mathrm { N a _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ may reduce the overall concentration of PFRs by disrupting the molecular processes that facilitate their generation (Huang et  al. 2022). Te inclusion of urea tends to disrupt carbon layers, forming heterocycles and generating more defects in MB500 (Wu et al. 2021; Zhu et al. 2018). Tese defects could regulate unpaired electrons onto the adjacent carbon structures, generating more lone pair electrons within PFRs (Liu et  al. 2024a, b). Excessive nitrogen doping can release substantial amounts of nitrogen-containing small molecules, such as $\mathrm { N H } _ { 3 } ,$ , which may react with hydrocarbon radicals (e.g., ⋅CH). Tis reaction can quench certain PFRs, leading to a reduction in their overall concentration. Consequently, maintaining an optimal ratio of urea to $\mathrm { N a _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ is essential to enhance the synergistic efects between these components, thereby promoting PFR generation. Furthermore, the molecular structure of nitrogen precursors plays a signifcant role in infuencing the condensation behavior of biochar and surface property changes during pyrolysis, afecting both the concentration and types of PFRs formed (Fig. S4a and Fig. S4b). For instance, the PFR concentration in MB500 increased when using urea, dicyandiamide, or melamine as nitrogen precursors. A related study noted that, despite dicyandiamide and melamine having higher nitrogen content than urea, they tend to form more condensed ${ \mathrm { C } } _ { \mathrm { X } } { \mathrm { N } } _ { \mathrm { y } }$ structures (such as CN heterocycles and tri-s-triazine units) during pyrolysis, where nitrogen is not fully incorporated into the carbon lattice. In contrast, the thermal decomposition of urea produces $\mathrm { N H } _ { 3 }$ and $\mathrm { C O } _ { 2 } ,$ which facilitates N-doping, tailors the surface chemistry and pore structure of the biochar, and modifes the acidity and alkalinity of the graphene basal plane (Li et  al. 2017). As a result, urea achieved a high level of N-doping in the biochar, establishing a more favorable microenvironment for PFR generation (see Sect.  3.2). In contrast, using thiourea inhibited PFR formation in MB500. Te expected synergistic efect of N,S co-doping did not occur; instead, N,S co-doping appears to substitute more oxygen atoms, resulting in fewer defect structures in the biochar compared to the single N-doped system (Ding et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2022a, b). In summary, compared to dicyandiamide, melamine, and thiourea, urea-modifed biochar produced the highest concentration of PFRs on its surface under the same dosage conditions. Meanwhile, based on the results of the preliminary experiments, it was also found that a higher concentration of PFRs correlates with a higher removal efciency of aniline (Fig. S4c). Tus, urea can be chosen as the $_ \mathrm { N }$ precursor for biochar modifcation in future experiments to further explore PFR generation and its relationship with pollutant removal.

# 3.2 Correlation between infuence factors and PFRs formation

During biomass pyrolysis, the formation of PFRs with unpaired electrons is driven by chemical bond cleavage and carbon structure reconstruction, involving a series of reactions (Chen et  al. 2024; Tao et  al. 2020) which may include dehydration, decarboxylation, decarbonylation, aromatization, and intra-molecular condensation (Huang et al. 2022; Ruan et al. 2019; Tao et al. 2020; Wu et al. 2021). Due to the complexity and interdependence of these fundamental reactions, the modifed biochars produced at pyrolysis temperatures from 300 to $6 0 0 ~ \mathrm { ^ { \circ } C }$ were quantitatively characterized for their elemental composition, structural features, and types of functional groups. Since biochar modifcation afects PFR concentration more than PFR type, Spearman analysis was conducted to explore correlations between these quantitative indicators and PFR levels. Tis analysis aimed to deepen the understanding of PFR formation in biochar modifed with both urea and $\mathrm { N a _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ .

As shown in Fig.  3 and Fig. S5, there was no signifcant positive or negative correlation between PFRs concentration and most quantitative indicators except for ${ \mathrm { C } } { = } { \mathrm { C } }$ $( P \le 0 . 0 5 )$ , $\mathrm { I _ { D } / I _ { G } }$ $( P { \le } 0 . 0 1 )$ , and pyridinic N $( P { \le } 0 . 0 1 )$ . As pyrolysis temperature rises, covalent bonds between substituent groups (such as methylene and methyl) and functional groups (like $C { - } C$ and $\mathrm { C - O - C }$ bonds) in biomass progressively break, generating numerous free radical fragments that recombine to form aromatic rings. Tese rings then condense to form larger, fused aromatic structures, stabilizing PFRs due to steric hindrance (Chen et  al. 2024; Ruan et  al. 2019). Similar fndings have been reported, suggesting that phenolic $\mathrm { O - H }$ groups may convert to phenoxy, with aromatization leading to the formation of aromatic radicals, as indicated by an increase in ${ \mathrm { C } } { = } { \mathrm { C } }$ bonds in aromatic and heterocyclic rings (Zhu et  al. 2023). Tis implies that PFR formation is highly dependent on aromatic ${ \mathrm { C } } { = } { \mathrm { C } }$ structures. Te structural indicator $\mathrm { I _ { D } / I _ { G } }$ shows a signifcant positive correlation with PFR concentration $( P { \le } 0 . 0 1 )$ , underscoring the role of defect structures in PFR generation. Tese abundant structural defects generally enhance the transport of unpaired electrons, resulting in increased free radical production. (Zhu et al 2018). Te signifcant correlation between the relative content of pyridinic $_ \mathrm { N }$ and PFRs concentration further indicates that among various defect structures, pyridinic $_ \mathrm { N }$ signifcantly plays a particularly crucial role in PFRs generation. Due to the specifc electron confguration, pyridinic $_ \mathrm { N }$ with $\mathsf { s p } ^ { 2 }$ hybridization at the edges of graphene planes could raise the density of p states near the Fermi level and lower the work function, thus serving as active sites (Yu et al. 2020) promoting PFRs formation by providing provide $p$ -electrons to the $\pi$ system (Wu et  al. 2021). XPS analysis (Fig. S6) showed that addition of $\mathrm { N a _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ alone increased the pyrrolic $_ \mathrm { N }$ content in biochar from 34.6 to $5 9 . 6 9 \%$ , while urea alone promoted an increase in both pyridinic $_ \mathrm { N }$ and graphitic N, particularly in graphitic N, which rose from 10.96 to $2 4 . 2 \%$ . However, combining urea and $\mathrm { N a _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ signifcantly enhanced the pyridinic and pyrrolic $_ \mathrm { N }$ content in biochar while reducing graphitic $_ \mathrm { N }$ and oxidized N levels. Tis suggests that the combined modifcation with urea and $\mathrm { N a _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ may help slow the conversion of pyridinic and pyrrolic N into graphitic N during pyrolysis (Zhao et al. 2021), favoring the creation of nitrogen heterocyclic structures in biochar.

Although no signifcant correlation was found between other factors and PFRs formation, they still have a combined efect on the generation and stabilization of PFRs. Biochar produced at moderate temperatures primarily forms oxygen-centered or carbon- and oxygen-dentered PFRs, indicating that the presence of oxygen or other elements is essential for enhancing PFRs generation, even though carbon is the primary element involved in PFRs formation (Bi et al. 2022). For example, due to their small size and high reactivity, H radicals can easily enter the macromolecular carbon skeleton structure and participate in the pyrolysis reaction. A higher number of H radicals may lead to a reduction in carbon-centered PFRs in biochar (Chen et al. 2024), while a higher presence of oxygen-containing structures, particularly quinones, can promote the formation of more PFRs (Wu et  al. 2021). Te positive correlation between PFRs concentration and the content of residual $_ \mathrm { N }$ and $\mathrm { N a }$ in biochar suggests that the synergistic efect of urea and $\mathrm { N a _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ promotes the formation of PFRs. Tis aligns with the observed correlations between PFRs concentration and the relative content of pyridinic N. Similar infuence was also refected in other structural indicators, including the H/C, O/C, and $\mathrm { ( O + N ) / C }$ atomic ratios, which represent the degree of aromatization, the quantity of oxygenated functional groups, and the polarity of biochar, respectively.

In terms of inorganic elements, it is generally accepted that trace concentrations of transition metals act as electron acceptors, extracting delocalized π-electrons from substituted aromatics in biochars (e.g., hydroquinone or catechol) and controlling the types and intensities of PFRs (Liu et al. 2024a, b; Ruan et al. 2019). However, excessive metal contents in biomass may consume the unpaired electrons for nucleation and reduction of metals (Wu et  al. 2021; Liu et  al. 2024a, b) due to the electron-shuttling efect of PFRs (Ruan et al. 2019). Unlike Fe, Zn in corncob biomass may suppress PFRs formation by hindering the electron transfer during biomass pyrolysis due to its poor ability to accept electrons (Liu et  al. 2024a, b). Tis complex relationship negatively impacts PFRs formation.

Te intensity of PFRs shows a positive correlation with ${ \mathrm { C } } { = } \mathrm { O }$ and pyrrolic N, and a negative correlation with $C { \mathrm { - } } \mathrm { O }$ , ${ \mathrm { O - C = O } } _ { \mathrm { { } } }$ graphitic $\mathrm { N } _ { i }$ and oxidized N. It has been suggested that $\mathrm { K B H _ { 4 } }$ -reducible carbonyl groups, likely present in aldehydes, aromatic ketones, and quinone structures (Liu et  al. 2024a, b), are important indicators of PFRs in biochars. Tese structures are associated with PFR formation, particularly in the reversible reaction between semiquinone radicals and quinones (Liu et  al. 2024a, b; Tao et al. 2022). Te homolytic cleavage of $_ \mathrm { C - O }$ or $_ { \mathrm { O - C = O } }$ linkages is a prerequisite for PFRs formation during biomass pyrolysis (Chen et al. 2024), so their contents are negatively correlated with PFR concentration. For pyrrolic N, it has been stated that PFRs formed within the pyrrolic $_ \mathrm { N }$ structure are more delocalized and stable compared to those in the graphitic-N structure. Tis is due to the lower energies of singly occupied molecular orbitals (SOMOs) and the distinct charge distribution patterns in the pyrrolic N structure (Zhu et al. 2020). Terefore, the presence of pyrrolic $_ \mathrm { N }$ is more conducive to PFRs formation. Based on the above discussion, it can be concluded that ${ \mathrm { C } } { = } { \mathrm { C } }$ $( P { \le } 0 . 0 5 )$ , $\mathrm { I _ { D } / I _ { G } }$ $( P { \le } 0 . 0 1 )$ and pyridinic $\mathrm { ~ N ~ } ( P { \leq } 0 . 0 1 )$ are the key factors in regulating PFRs formation in MB500 catalysts. Te pyridinic ring, whether or not it contains oxygenated functional groups, is likely the primary source of PFRs. However, due to the limited scope of research conducted, the complex relationships between the elemental composition, structural characteristics, functional group types, and PFR properties of biochar remain inadequately understood. A more detailed investigation is needed in future studies to fully elucidate these connections.

Aniline, a widely used organic compound in various industrial applications, was selected as a target pollutant to evaluate the catalytic performance of the MB500 catalyst towards PMS in aqueous solutions under diferent conditions. Figure 4a and b illustrate the infuence of initial $\mathrm { p H }$ on aniline removal. Te reusability of the modifed biochar catalyst is a critical metric for assessing its long-term efectiveness. As illustrated in Fig. S8, after fve consecutive reuse cycles, the aniline removal efciency of the MB500/PMS catalytic system gradually decreased from an initial $9 7 . 0 2 \%$ to $7 8 . 9 1 \%$ . Te positive correlation between aniline removal and PFR signals on biochar particles after multiple reuse cycles (Fig. 5b) indicates that the reduction in PFRs signifcantly contributes to the decline in aniline removal efciency. Since PFRs primarily originate from the pyridine rings in biochar, the relative content of ${ \mathrm { C } } { = } { \mathrm { C } }$ , C–O/C–N functional groups, and pyridinic N decreased after catalytic reactions (Fig.  6a and b). Te reduction in other active sites on the biochar surface further impacts its catalytic efciency. Oxygen containing functional groups such as ${ \mathrm { C } } { = } \mathrm { O }$ (ketone carbonyl) and $_ { \mathrm { O - C = O } }$ (ester or carboxyl groups), along with nitrogen species like graphitic N, underwent varying degrees of consumption during the reaction (Fig.  6a–c), which in turn infuenced the generation of reactive species in the system. Importantly, $_ { \mathrm { O - C = O } }$ bonds herein mainly originate from lactone groups in biochar rather than carboxyl groups (Fig.  6d). Previous studies have suggested that the lone pair of electrons in the Lewis basic sites of biochar-based catalysts (such as the oxygen atom in ${ \mathrm { C } } { = } \mathrm { O }$ and pyridinic N), as well as the free-fowing π-electrons in sp2-hybridized carbon, can transfer from the biochar to PMS, thereby inducing the production of ⋅OH and $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \cdot - }$ (Zhang et al. 2023; Zhao et al. 2021). Te graphitic N structure enhances the formation of dangling σ bonds and facilitates electron transfer by increasing the charge density of carbon atoms. Tis, in turn, promotes PMS adsorption and activation activity via the transfer of electron from biochar to PMS (Luo et al. 2021; Qi et al. 2020; Zhao et  al. 2021). As these active sites were consumed, additional $_ \mathrm { C - O }$ functional groups and pyrrolic N were formed on the biochar surface (Fig. 6a–c). Tese changes in the physicochemical properties of the biochar surface can negatively impact its catalytic performance. It is often overlooked that interactions between biochar and pollutants—driven by factors such as metal cations, reactive oxygen species (ROS), and ultraviolet radiation—may facilitate the regeneration of biochar-PFRs during the reaction (Xie et al. 2024). Terefore, future studies should focus on improving the regeneration of biochar-PFRs.

# 3.4 Adsorption behavior of MB500 catalyst

In the biochar/PMS catalytic system, the removal of organic pollutants generally involves both adsorption and degradation mechanisms (Luo et  al. 2021). Fig. S9 illustrates the adsorption hebavior of MB500 towards aniline at diferent initial $\mathrm { p H }$ levels. Te results indicated that within the initial $\mathrm { p H }$ range of 3 to 10, aniline adsorption tended to equilibrium in approximately $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ , with removal efciencies ranging from 19.89 to $3 0 . 2 7 \%$ . As the initial $\mathsf { p H }$ increased, the removal efciency decreased, suggesting that the moderate adsorption capacity of MB500 did not play a signifcant role in aniline removal in the MB500/PMS catalytic system. Te adsorption of aniline on MB500 followed pseudo-second-order kinetics, with a high correlation coefcient of $\mathrm { R } ^ { 2 } { = } 0 . 9 9 6$ (Fig. S10 and Table S3), indicating the predominance of chemical adsorption. In this case, the physical adsorption facilitated by the porous structure, characterized by a specifc surface area of $4 8 . 7 4 ~ \mathrm { m } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { g } ^ { - 1 }$ , a pore volume of $0 . 1 0 \ { \mathrm { c m } } ^ { 3 } \ { \mathrm { g } } ^ { - 1 }$ , and an average pore size of $8 . 4 6 \ \mathrm { n m }$ contributed minimally to the overall activity of the optimal catalyst. Interestingly, in an extended adsorption study, the removal rate of aniline increased steadily over time, ultimately reaching $4 0 \%$ after 40  days (Fig.  7). Given the reaction characteristics of PFRs, it is reasonable to hypothesize that PFRs in biochar can directly react with aniline, leading to its oxidative degradation. Previous studies have also reported that PFRs, with their delocalized unpaired electrons, can remediate pollutants by regulating electron transfer independently (Liu et al. 2024a, b; Yang et al. 2016). Due to the slow nature of the direct degradation process, no systematic or in-depth research was conducted on it in this study.

Te $\mathrm { p H }$ of a solution signifcantly infuences the types of pollutants, the surface charge of biochar, and other physicochemical properties, thereby interfering with the adsorption behaviour of biochar. According to the Zeta potential distribution graph (Fig. S11), the isoelectric point of MB500 was 1.90. Within the pH range of 3 to 10, the surface of MB500 consistently carried a net negative charge, with the degree of deprotonation increasing as the $\mathrm { p H }$ rose. Aniline, a weak base, predominantly exists in its molecular and positively charged ionic forms when the $\mathrm { p H }$ is between 3.0 and 7.0. Te ionic form of aniline interacts with MB500 through electrostatic efects. However, as the pH increases, the proportion of ionic aniline decreases, weakening its electrostatic interaction with MB500. Even within the $\mathsf { p H }$ range of 7.0 to 10.0, some removal of aniline in its molecular form was observed, suggesting that additional adsorption mechanisms, beyond electrostatic interactions, contribute to the adsorption of aniline by MB500.

Other mechanisms, such as hydrogen bonds, $\pi - \pi$ interactions, and hydrophobic interactions, also contribute to the adsorption of aniline, as confrmed by the FTIR and XPS spectra analyses. Figure 8a displays the FTIR spectra of MB500 before and after aniline adsorption. Te spectra revealed that the peak at $3 3 8 5 ~ \mathrm { c m ^ { - 1 } }$ , corresponding to the stretching vibrations of $- \mathrm { O H }$ groups and $_ \mathrm { N - H }$ bonds, became broader and more intense following adsorption. Tis alteration suggests that aniline was adsorbed onto MB500 through hydrogen bonding between the $\mathrm { - N H _ { 2 } }$ groups of aniline and the $- \mathrm { O H }$ groups on the MB500 surface (Yu et al. 2017). Small shifts and increased intensities of the peak at $1 5 9 0 ~ \mathrm { { c m } ^ { - 1 } }$ , corresponding to stretching vibration of aromatic ring skeleton or $_ \mathrm { N - H }$ bending vibration in aniline, suggest $\pi - \pi$ interactions between the benzene ring of aniline and the electron-depleted areas of MB500 (Yu et al. 2016, 2024). Te increased intensity of the peak at $1 2 2 2 ~ \mathrm { { c m } ^ { - 1 } }$ , which is associated with both ${ \mathrm { C - N } }$ stretching and ring stretching vibrations (Suresh et al. 2013; Yu et al. 2017), confrms the binding of aniline to the surface of MB500 during the interaction process. Further, due to $\pi - \pi$ interactions (Zhang et al. 2022a, b), the peaks between 600 and $9 0 0 \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ , associated with the out-of-plan bending of aromatic carbon (Yu et al. 2018), became more pronounced following aniline adsorption. Tese observations collectively suggest that both hydrogen bonding and $\pi { - } \pi$ interactions play signifcant roles in the adsorption of aniline onto MB500.

# 3.5 Reactive species formation in MB500/PMS system

To identify the primary reactive species generated during PMS activation, chemical quenching experiments were conducted. Figure  9 illustrates the efects of various scavengers, namely MeOH, TBA, $p { \mathrm { - } } \mathrm { B Q } ,$ FFA, and KI, on aniline removal by the MB500/PMS catalytic system. Te introduction of TBA and MeOH at concentrations of 5 and $1 0 \ \mathrm { m m o l \ L ^ { - 1 } }$ did not signifcantly suppress aniline removal, particularly in the case of TBA, indicating that $\cdot \mathrm { O H }$ and $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \cdot - }$ were not the dominant reactive species in this system. However, the presence of $p$ -BQ and FFA at $5 ~ \mathrm { m m o l ~ L ^ { - 1 } }$ signifcantly inhibited aniline degradation, reducing the removal efciency by $2 4 \%$ and $1 7 \%$ respectively. Tis inhibition was further enhanced at a concentration of $1 0 \ \mathrm { m m o l \ L ^ { - 1 } }$ . Tese results suggest that $\mathrm { O _ { 2 } } ^ { . - }$ generated in the MB500/PMS catalytic system plays a crucial role in aniline degradation, followed by $^ { 1 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ,$ while $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \cdot - }$ and ⋅OH have lesser contributions. Te addition of KI, which primarily detects surface-bound reactive species, resulted in a moderate inhibition in aniline removal, decreasing the removal efciency by $2 1 \%$ at a concentration of $1 0 \ \mathrm { m m o l \ L ^ { - 1 } }$ . Tis indicates that the catalytic oxidation reaction occurred both in the bulk solution and at the catalyst interface, with respective contributions of approximately $7 0 \%$ and $3 0 \%$ . Based on these results, it can be concluded that radical reactions, along with non-radical pathways occurring mainly in the bulk solution, were responsible for the aniline removal in the MB500/PMS catalytic system.

Te reactive species generated by the MB500/PMS system were further investigated using the EPR analysis, employing DMPO and TEMP as spin-trapping reagents. As shown in Fig. S12, no signifcant EPR signals were detected with MB500 alone. However, in MB500/ PMS catalytic system, characteristic signals for DMPO and TEMP spin trappig adducts were observed, confrming the generation of ⋅OH, $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \cdot - }$ , $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { \cdot - }$ , and ${ } ^ { 1 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ during the reaction. Te intensities of these EPR signals increased with the extension of reaction time (Fig. S13), with the concentration of each reactive species reaching $4 . 2 1 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 1 }$ spins $\mathrm { m m } ^ { - 3 }$ , $5 . 9 3 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 1 }$ spins $\mathrm { m m } ^ { - 3 }$ , $1 . 7 2 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 3 }$ spins $\mathrm { m m } ^ { - 3 }$ , $7 . 1 3 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 1 }$ spins $\mathrm { m m } ^ { - 3 }$ at $3 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ , respectively. To determine the correlation among the amount of PFRs, formation of reactive species, and efciency of aniline removal, linear ftting was performed. As shown in Fig. 10a and Table 1, there was a highly negative correlation between the generation of $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \cdot - }$ and ${ \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } } ^ { \cdot - }$ and the concentration of biochar-PFRs $\mathrm { ~  ~ \cal ~ R ~ } ^ { 2 } = 0 . 9 0 8$ and 0.897, respectively). However, the linear correlation coefcient between PFRs concentration and the generation of ⋅OH and $^ 1 \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ was weak, indicating that the PFRs were the main contributors to the formation of $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \cdot - }$ and $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { \cdot - }$ . Tis fnding difers from previous studies, where $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \cdot - }$ and ⋅OH were typically considered the main reactive radicals in catalytic PMS activation ( $\mathrm { Y u }$ et  al. 2020). Terefore, it is entirely feasible to control the formation of reactive species in biochar-catalyzed advanced oxidation processes through surface modifcation via $\mathrm { N a _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ activation and hetero-atom $_ \mathrm { N }$ doping. According to previous research, biochar-PFRs can directly transfer a single electron to PMS molecules and adsorbed molecular oxygen, resulting in the formation of $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \cdot - }$ and $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } \mathrm { ^ { - } }$ (Liu et al. 2024a, b; Luo et al. 2021; Zhang et al. 2023; Zhao et al. 2021), respectively (Eqs.  1 and 2). $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { \cdot - }$ can react with PMS molecules or disproportionate to $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ,$ further generating $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \cdot - }$ and ⋅OH (Yu et  al. 2020). Partially generated $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \cdot - }$ from these pathways then reacts with $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }$ or $\mathrm { O H ^ { - } }$ to form ⋅OH (Wu et  al. 2021; Yu et  al. 2020) (Eqs. 3 and 4). To confrm the role of adsorbed molecular oxygen in aniline removal, dissolved oxygen (DO) in the reaction was removed by $\Nu _ { 2 }$ pre-sparging. Unexpectedly, no signifcant diference in aniline removal efciency was observed with or without DO (Fig. S14). Tis indicates that the adsorbed molecular oxygen did not contribute as a source of $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { \cdot - }$ in the presence of biochar-PFRs. In fact, various PFRs could react with $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }$ to generate $\mathrm { H O } _ { 2 }$ ⋅ by one-electron transfer (Eq. (5)) (Luo et al. 2021), which can be subsequently converted to $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { \cdot - }$ under alkaline conditions. Previous studies have confrmed that $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { \cdot - }$ is easily converted into ${ } ^ { 1 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ within the degradation system (Eqs. 6–8). However, due to the poor correlation between PFRs and ${ } ^ { 1 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ generation in this study, it can be inferred that other pathways for ${ } ^ { 1 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ generation might exist that are not afected by $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { \cdot - }$ . Te electrophilic addition and electron abstraction on the surface of biochar-based catalysts are the key chemical processes taking place in the ${ } ^ { 1 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ dominated non-radical pathways (Liu et al. 2023). In this study, the ketone structure in biochar at the edge of the carbon matrix (Luo et  al. 2021) may serve as an electron acceptor to induce PMS to produce ${ } ^ { 1 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ through a series of reactions, such as additive reactions, dehydration, and ringopening reactions (Hu et al. 2020; Qi et al. 2020). Due to its higher electronegativity compared to carbon, the graphitic $_ \mathrm { N }$ doped into the $\mathsf { s p } ^ { 2 }$ -hybridized carbon ring withdraws electrons from neighboring carbon atoms, resulting in a positive charge on the adjacent carbon atoms. Tis electron-defcient carbon atom then attracts electrons from PMS, facilitating the formation of $\mathrm { S O } _ { 5 } { ^ { - } }$ through a nucleophilic addition reaction. Tis species can further react with water to generate ${ } ^ { 1 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ (Eqs.  9 and 10) (Liu et  al. 2023; Zhao et  al. 2021). $^ 1 \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ can also be produced through PMS self-decomposition (Eq.  (11)) (Liu et  al. 2023; Zhao et  al. 2021), and the coexistence of ⋅OH, $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \cdot - }$ , and $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } \mathrm { ^ { - } }$ in the reaction system can also lead to the production of ${ } ^ { 1 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ (Eqs. 12–14) (Huang et al. 2021).

# 3.6 Reaction mechanism analysis

Based on the above discussion, the possible mechanism of PMS activation by biochar-PFRs for aniline removal was proposed and is illustrated in Fig.  11. Te collaborative modifcation of urea and $\mathrm { N a _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ creates more pyridinic N heterocyclic defects in mesoporous biochar. Tese defects facilitate the formation of ${ \mathrm { C } } { = } \mathrm { O }$ functional groups and ${ \mathrm { C } } { = } { \mathrm { C } }$ bonds, redistributing excess electrons to the adjacent sp2-hybridized carbon atoms in the carbonbased framework via the delocalized $\pi$ -conjugated network (Zhang et al. 2022a, b). Tis electron transfer alters the original state of PFRs on the biochar surface, particularly their concentration, resulting in the formation of more oxygen- and carbon-centered PFRs at $5 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ .

Given the relatively small specifc surface area and pore volume of the modifed biochar at this temperature, during the catalytic oxidation reaction, aniline molecules predominantly interacted with active sites such as pyridinic $_ \mathrm { N }$ and graphitic $_ \mathrm { N }$ on the biochar surface. Tese interactions occurred through various chemical mechanisms, including electrostatic interactions, hydrogen bonding, $\pi - \pi$ interactions, and hydrophobic efects. Te bonded aniline molecules were then oxidized via a direct electron-transfer mechanism by PFRs, with the process potentially extending over a prolonged reaction time of up to 40 days (Zhao et al. 2021). However, this direct oxidation process is negligible within the $3 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ timeframe of this study. In the indirect oxidation process, the single-electron transfer from various active sites, such as $\mathrm { s p } ^ { 2 } ~ \mathrm { C } { = } \mathrm { C } ,$ , and pyridinic $_ \mathrm { N }$ of PFRs in biochar, reacts with $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }$ and PMS, leading to the generation of various reactive species, particularly $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { \cdot - }$ and $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \cdot - }$ . All of these produced reactive species contribute to aniline removal, with the $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { \cdot - }$ radical pathway being the most efective, outperforming the $^ 1 \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } , \ \mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { \bar { \ } } ^ { \cdot \cdot }$ and ⋅OH in the MB500/ PMS system. In this process, organic contaminants lose electrons, converting into intermediate products or being directly mineralized into $\mathrm { C O } _ { 2 }$ and $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }$ (Zhao et al. 2021). Te reasons behind the dominant role of $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { \cdot - }$ and the dynamic relationship with the species in biochar-based catalysts require further exploration in future studies. As active sites such as $C { = } C$ , $\mathrm { C } { = } \mathrm { O }$ , ${ \mathrm { O - C = O } } _ { \mathrm { i } }$ , pyridinic N, and graphitic $_ \mathrm { N }$ are lost, along with a reduction in catalytic activity, the efciency of aniline removal gradually decreases with each use.

# 4 Conclusions

In this study, biochar enriched with PFRs was synthesized through a combined modifcation process involving urea and $\mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { C O } _ { 3 }$ . Te factors infuencing PFR formation were investigated, demonstrating that ${ \mathrm { C } } { = } { \mathrm { C } }$ bonds, the $\mathrm { I _ { D } / I _ { G } }$ ratio, and pyridinic $_ \mathrm { N }$ are key determinants in the generation of PFRs. Batch experiments showed that, under neutral conditions, the MB500/PMS catalytic system achieved a $9 2 \%$ removal rate of aniline within $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ and demonstrated excellent tolerance to anions. It was found that aniline removal occurs through two primary mechanisms: direct oxidation by PFRs and indirect oxidation via active species generated by PFR activation, particularly $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { \cdot - }$ . Tis study provides new insights into the synergistic efects of combined modifcations on PFR formation in biochar, laying a solid theoretical foundation for the structural design and functional optimization of biochar. Furthermore, it presents an innovative approach for the efective removal of recalcitrant pollutants from water, positioning biochar as a highly promising candidate for large-scale wastewater treatment. Refecting on limitations of the study, future research should focus on the application of PFR-enriched biochar for treating diverse real-world wastewater, the full utilization of various active sites on the biochar surface to enhance its efcacy, and the development of efective regeneration techniques to enable efcient recycling. Tese advancements will support the broader application of biochar in environmental remediation.
