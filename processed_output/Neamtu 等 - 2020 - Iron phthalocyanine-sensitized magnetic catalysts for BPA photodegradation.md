# Iron phthalocyanine-sensitized magnetic catalysts for BPA photodegradation

Mariana Neamtu1\*, Claudia Nadejde1, Loredana Brinza1, Oana Dragos2, Daniela Gherghel 3 & Andrea Paul4

The catalytic behavior of iron phthalocyanine (FePc)-sensitized magnetic nanocatalysts was evaluated for their application in the oxidative treatment of Bisphenol A (BPA) under mild environmental conditions. Two types of FePc (Fe(II )Pc and $\mathsf { F e ( l l l ) P c ) }$ , which are highly photosensitive compounds, were immobilized on the surface of functionalized magnetite. The nanomaterials were characterized by high resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM), X-ray difraction (XRD), Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTI R) and thermogravimetric analyses (TGA). The generation of singlet oxygen by nanomaterials was also investigated. In the presence of UVA light exposure $( 3 6 5 \mathsf { n m } )$ ) and $\mathsf { 1 5 m M }$ ${ \sf H } _ { 2 } { \sf O } _ { 2 } ,$ the $M @ \mathsf { F e } ( \mathsf { I I I } ) \mathsf { P c }$ photocatalyst gave the best results; for a catalyst concentration of $2 . 0 { \mathfrak { g l } } ^ { - 1 } .$ , around $60 \%$ BPA was removed after 120min of reaction. These experimental conditions were further tested under natural solar light exposure, for which also M@Fe(III )Pc exhibited enhanced oxidative catalytic activity, being able to remove $8 3 \%$ of BPA in solution. The water samples were less cytotoxic after treatment, this being confrmed by the MCF-7 cell viability assay.

Emerging recalcitrant organic pollutants received extensive attention over the years, since they are difcult to remove using conventional technologies. Continuous eforts are made to fnd improved solutions for fghting against and for diminishing environmental pollution. Endocrine disruptive chemicals (EDC) are a class of hazardous persistent pollutants exhibiting numerous harmful efects, able to induce subtle and irreversible changes in living organisms, even in concentrations of few parts per billion1,2 . Bisphenol A (BPA) is an EDC that remains a major water contaminant, since its production in the 1950s. Since then, BPA become a well-established model compound for persistent micropollutants. Although found in low concentration in water, its removal proved to be unsuccessful using conventional water treatment approaches, such as chlorination and ozonation, processes that also exhibit several draw-backs3 . Promising alternatives, such as photocatalysis1,4–6 , were found to be very efcient and versatile. For instance, photocatalysis involving dye-sensitization represents an eco-friendly approach with great potential for very efcient removal of persistent hazardous pollutants from waters. Singlet oxygen, a reactive oxygen species (ROS), has proved to be an extremely efective agent in the oxidation of emerging pollutants, due to its high oxidation ability.

Organic phthalocyanines (Pcs) are the synthetic analogues of natural porphyrine compounds7 . Discovered in 1928, Pcs, possessing a two-dimensional geometry and a ring system consisting of $1 8 \pi$ -electrons, are composed of four isindole units linked by nitrogen atoms. Due to the large, aromatic, macrocyclic structure of $\mathrm { P c }$ , a variety of metals (Me), such as $\mathrm { F e ^ { 8 - 1 0 } }$ , $\mathrm { \dot { Z } n ^ { 1 1 , 1 2 } }$ , $\mathrm { C u } ^ { 1 3 , 1 4 }$ , Mn, Co, $\mathrm { N i } ^ { 1 5 , 1 6 }$ , $\bar { \mathsf { S i } } ^ { 1 7 }$ , $\mathrm { R u } ^ { 1 8 }$ , can be bonded inside the cycle of Pc, substituting the two hydrogen atoms. Tese metals can tune the fne structure of the new functionalized compound as well as its physical and chemical properties. Pcs and their metalo-derivates exhibit numerous remarkable features such as excellent chemical and thermal stability, high response to light exposure in a large range from UV to near infrared region of the light spectrum, etc. Moreover, their ability to generate singlet oxygen, makes them ideal photosensitizers. Tus, they found applicability in various felds. Recent advances in phthalocyanine-based functional materials are presented in a very comprehensive review by Bian and Jiang19. A detailed review of Pc synthesis and application as catalysts is given by Sorokin20. Among application as catalysts, Pcs have been received special attention as their efciency can potentially be increased by the presence of Fe, which may play an important role due to the redox dynamic structure of the Fe(II)/Fe(III) in oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) and photocatalysis. In literature, a limited number of studies were found about the potential of single–atom catalysts of the iron phthalocyanine (FePc) mechanism for the hydrogen peroxide activated catalytic decomposition of recalcitrant organic pollutants.

In comparison with homogeneous photocatalysis, heterogeneous photocatalysis presents signifcant advantages; in the latter mentioned procedures, the FePcs can be fxed onto solid supports, such as magnetic substrates, which allow easy retrieval of the material from the environment without causing additional pollution; this also provides the possibility of material reuse in subsequent water treatment cycles. Magnetite $\left( \mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 } \right)$ is a popular choice among magnetic carriers, being recognized as an eco-friendly material with numerous important advantages and extensive applications. Te incorporation of $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 }$ particles in polymers provides material stability, protection against oxidation, nanoparticle dispersion and possibility for further functionalization. Based on the versatile reactivity of phthalocyanine with metal and outstanding physical properties of MePc, introducing the phthalocyanine to $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 }$ nanoparticles may result into a new hybrid material whose properties can be diferent not only from the single polymer but also from the $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 } ,$ due to the synergistic efects derived from the interaction between them. Te few reported data in the literature demonstrates that such interaction generally leads to improved photophysical and photochemical properties of MePc- $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 }$ systems8,10,21.

Hence, in this study, we report the synthesis and photocatalytic behavior of FePc-sensitized magnetic nanoparticles for the photodegradation of BPA in solution. Te combination of advanced oxidation processes with light-responsive FePc immobilized on magnetic nanoparticles was investigated for efcient BPA photodegradation at near-neutral pH, using diferent catalyst dosages and $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ concentrations, under diferent light sources. Te obtained magnetic nanocatalysts were easy recoverable and recyclable, possessing good photocatalytic activity, especially under visible light photoactivation.

At temperature above $2 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ the TG curves of the M and $\mathbf { M } @ \mathbf { F e } ( \mathrm { I I I } ) \mathbf { P c }$ shows a weight increase of about $0 . 6 5 \%$ , most probably due to the oxidation of the magnetite into maghemite as previously mentioned36. Kinetics of BPA photodegradation and cytotoxicity of the samples. Te efect of catalysts concentration, hydrogen peroxide concentration and the initial concentration of micropollutant, as well as the reutilization of the catalyst have been considered for the photodegradation of BPA in the present study. Additionally, the concentration of singlet oxygen has been determined.

Tree concentrations of catalyst (1.0, 2.0, $3 . 0 \mathrm { g L } ^ { - 1 } ,$ were used in our experiments. According to the results presented in Fig. 5a,b, the highest degradation has been achieved at catalyst concentration of $2 \mathrm { g } \mathrm { L } ^ { = _ { 1 } }$ for both catalysts, slightly more active being the $\mathbf { \bar { M } e } \mathbf { F e } ( \mathrm { I I } ) \mathbf { P c }$ catalyst. Te photodegradation of BPA in the presence of both catalysts is between 19 and $2 8 \%$ , depending on the concentration of catalysts used in the experiments.

At higher concentration of the catalyst $( > 2 \mathrm { g } \mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 } )$ no improvement in the degradation of the pollutant could be observed. Tis could be explained by the agglomeration of magnetic nanoparticles, at higher concentration. Tus, the concentration of $2 \mathrm { g } \mathrm { \bar { L } } ^ { - 1 }$ has been selected for the next experiments.

Under natural solar light the degradation of BPA was between 15 and $2 0 \%$ that is similar to degradation under UVA light (Fig. 6). In the presence of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ the degradation was enhanced. $8 3 \%$ of BPA in solution was removed by the ${ \mathrm { F e } } ( { \mathrm { I I I } } ) { \mathrm { P c } }$ photocatalyst during 120 minutes of irradiation.

Additionally, the property as a photosensitizer of the catalysts to generate singlet oxygen was investigated. Te results are presented in Fig. 7. and show that, afer 60 minutes of irradiation, in the presence of $\mathrm { M } @ \mathrm { F e } ( \mathrm { I I } ) \mathrm { P c }$ catalyst, $9 . 8 \mu \mathrm { m o l } \mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 }$ of singlet oxygen was generated, whereas in the presence of $\mathrm { M } @ \mathrm { F e } \overline { { ( \mathrm { I I I } ) } } \mathrm { P c }$ catalyst only $1 . 2 6 \mu \mathrm { m o l }$ $\mathrm { ~ L ~ } ^ { - 1 }$ were produced. Tis indicate that the $\mathrm { M @ F e ( I I ) P c }$ catalyst can promote more efciently the formation of $^ 1 \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } .$ which enhances the degradation of BPA.

Te proposed mechanism could be as follows: the BPA oxidation and degradation is triggered by the Reactive Oxygen Species (ROSs) generated by a molecular oxygen activation process which occurred in the presence of photosensitizer under the UV irradiation5,35,37–41. Detailing, a photon is adsorbed by an electronically excited singlet state which can further undergo intersystem crossing and generate longer lived excited triplet state, promoting a sensitizer. More singlet oxygen is then being produced by energy transfer towards dissolved molecular oxygen. Te magnetic core generates electrons and holes in the valence band which contribute to Fenton photocatalysis.

Te addition of hydrogen peroxide improved the degradation of BPA. Five concentrations of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ have been selected (Fig. 5c,d). Te $\mathrm { M } @ \mathrm { F e } ( \mathrm { I I I } ) \mathrm { P c }$ catalyst was more efcient. In $1 2 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ using $1 5 \mathrm { m m o l L ^ { - 1 } H _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } } , 6 7$ $6 7 \%$ of BPA has been decomposed. At the same concentration of hydrogen peroxide the degradation of BPA was increased to $8 2 \%$ and to $7 0 . 4 6 \%$ in the presence of $\mathbf { M } @ \operatorname { F e } ( \operatorname { I I I } ) \operatorname { P c }$ and $\begin{array} { r l } { \boldsymbol { \mathrm { M } } @ \boldsymbol { \mathrm { F e } } ( \mathrm { I I } ) \boldsymbol { \mathrm { P c } } . } \end{array}$ , respectively, afer $2 4 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ . By increasing of hydrogen peroxide concentration to $2 0 { - } 2 5 \mathrm { m m o l L ^ { - 1 } }$ no improvement of the decomposition of the micropollutant could be observed. Tis could be because the generated hydroxyl radicals produced hydroperoxyl radicals $\mathrm { ( H O } _ { 2 } { \bullet } )$ in the presence of a local excess of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ . Te hydroperoxyl radicals are much less reactive and do not contribute to the oxidative degradation of the organic substrate which occurs only by reaction with HO•. Similar behaviour shows the $\mathbf { M } \ @ \operatorname { F e } ( \operatorname { I I } ) \operatorname { P c }$ catalyst. Terefore, for our further experiments a concentration of $\mathrm { 1 5 m m o l L ^ { - 1 } H _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } }$ was chosen. No signifcant photodegradation of BPA occurred in the presence of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ alone (in the absence of the photocatalysts). High catalytic removals of $0 . 0 2 \mathrm { m m o l L ^ { - 1 } }$ of RB195 and RhB dyes (ca. $8 0 \%$ ) from aqueous solution were also achieved by Han et al. 42,43 in the presence of FePc and CuFePc immobilized on PAN, $2 . 5 0 \mathrm { m m o l L ^ { - 1 } H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ and $\mathrm { p H } 6$ . Tey indicated that the photocatalytic activity is improved by the $\pi - \pi$ stacking interaction when FePC is loaded on graphene, and also by the addition of the oxidant46. # Conclusion

In this study two types of iron phthalocyanine (FePc)-sensitized magnetic nanocatalysts were investigated for the photodegradation of BPA, an endocrine disruptive chemical (EDC), that is able to induce damages in living organisms. Te quasi-spherical shape of the nanoparticles, with diameters up to $1 5 \mathrm { n m }$ , were identifed by HR-TEM. Te ultrathin coating of phthalocyanine ring of ca $1 \mathrm { n m }$ surrounding the magnetic cores was also evidenced and confrmed by FTIR spectra and TG analysis. XRD patterns of the obtained samples confrmed that magnetite was the dominant crystalline phase in all samples. In the presence of $\mathbf { M } @ \operatorname { F e } ( \operatorname { I I } ) \operatorname { P c }$ catalyst, $9 . 8 \mu \mathrm { m o l } \mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 }$ of singlet oxygen was generated afer 60 minutes of irradiation, whereas in the presence of $\mathbf { M } @ \operatorname { F e } ( \operatorname { I I I } ) \operatorname { P c }$ catalyst only $\bar { 1 . 2 6 } \mu \mathrm { m o l } \mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 }$ were produced. Te optimum concentrations of catalyst and hydrogen peroxide were $2 . 0 { \bf g } \dot { \bf L } ^ { - 1 }$ and $\mathrm { 1 5 m m o l L ^ { - 1 } H _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } } ;$ , respectively. Te photodegradation of BPA in the presence of both catalysts was between 19 and $2 8 \%$ , whereas, with the addition of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ , the BPA removal have increased (between 49 and $8 3 \%$ ) and it was accelerated by exposure to natural solar light. In the presence of hydrogen peroxide, the $\mathbf { M } @ \operatorname { F e } ( \operatorname { I I I } ) \operatorname { P c }$ catalyst was more active. Te activity of both catalysts remains good over four consecutive runs. Te MCF-7 cell viability assay showed that, afer treatment, the water samples were less cytotoxic. Our results showed that both catalysts can be successfully applied in wastewater treatment.

# Methods

Materials and characterization methods. All aqueous solutions were prepared with fresh ultrapure water $( 0 . 0 5 5 \mu \mathrm { S } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 } )$ ) produced by an Evoqua LABOSTAR Pro TWF UV unit. High purity chemicals (SIGMAALDRICH) were used in all experimental procedures. Iron(II) phthalocyanine $( \mathrm { F e } ( \mathrm { I I } ) \mathrm { P c } )$ and iron(III) phthalocyanine- $4 , 4 ^ { \prime } , 4 ^ { \prime \prime } , 4 ^ { \prime \prime \prime }$ -tetrasulfonic acid with oxygen monosodium salt hydrate $( \mathrm { F e } ( \mathrm { I I I } ) \mathrm { P c } )$ were chosen as sensitizers and were immobilized on magnetic nanoparticles in order to catalyse the photooxidation of BPA in solution.

A BANDELINE SONOPULS homogenizing system, model HD4100, equipped with a HF generator GM 4100, ultrasonic converter UW100, standard horn $\tilde { \mathrm { S H 1 0 0 G } }$ and a $1 3 \mathrm { m m }$ titanium probe, was set-up at $2 0 \mathrm { W }$ for the sonochemical synthesis of the magnetic nanoparticles.

Te synthesis of the photocatalysts was followed by the characterization of their physico-chemical properties. Ultra-high resolution transmission electron microscopy (UHR-TEM) images were acquired by a LIBRA200MC/ CARL ZEISS GmbH instrument (Germany). X-ray difraction (XRD) patterns were recorded by a BRUKER AXS D8-Advance X-ray difractometer (Germany) using Bragg-Brentano confguration and the $\mathrm { C u - K } \alpha$ radiation ( $\lambda$ $= 0 . 1 5 4 \mathrm { n m }$ ); the DIFFRACplus Eva sofware was also used in order to estimate the average crystallite size in the synthesized nanomaterials based on Scherrer’s equation. Standard Si material and its spectra was used as a reference material for FWHM analyses and calculation of relative particle size. Te immobilization of phthalocyanines on the magnetic cores was evaluated by Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy (JASCO 6100 spectrometer) at room temperature; powder samples prepared in KBr discs were used to record the FT-IR spectra between 400 and $4 0 0 0 \mathrm { c m } ^ { - \mathrm { \bar { 1 } } }$ , with a resolution of $4 \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ . Termogravimetric analyses (TGA) were carried out in argon atmosphere using a NETZSCH STA 409 PC/PG device, in the temperature range from $2 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ to $8 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ with a heating rate of $5 \mathrm { K m i n ^ { - 1 } }$ . Electron spin resonance (ESR) spectra were recorded using a MINISCOPE MS300 device (Magnettech GmbH, Berlin, Germany) and the diamagnetic 2,2,6,6-tetramethyl-4-piperidinol (TMP-OH, $4 0 \mathrm { m m o l } \mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 }$ ) as trap for the molecular singlet oxygen $( ^ { 1 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } )$ generated in solution in the presence of the photocatalysts; the entire settings and method is described in detail in our previous work35.

Photocatalyst synthesis. Te preparation of the two photocatalysts consisted in a multi-step procedure, starting with the sonochemical synthesis of the magnetite $\left( \mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 } \right)$ cores and their subsequent stabilization with low molecular polyethylene glycol (PEG), immediately followed by the sensitizing of the obtained nanoparticles with photoactive compounds, namely iron phthalocyanines (FePc). Te detailed procedure was described in our recent study35. Briefy, in here, the conventional wet co-precipitation route, a very efcient method for $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 }$ nanoparticles synthesis, was carried out under ultrasound irradiation, maintaining a constant temperature of $6 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ and normal atmospheric conditions. Te as-synthesized bare magnetic nanoparticles were then rapidly functionalized with PEG in order to prevent their agglomeration and oxidation, yielding $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 } / \mathrm { P E G }$ nanoparticles, further denoted as M. Equal amounts from the fresh black slurry M were further functionalized with either Fe(II) Pc or ${ \mathrm { F e } } ( { \mathrm { I I I } } ) { \mathrm { P c } }$ . A volume of $2 0 \mathrm { m L }$ of $3 . 7 2 \mathrm { m m o l L ^ { - 1 } }$ solution of each iron phthalocyanine was gradually poured onto $0 . 5 { \mathrm { g } }$ of M and kept under rapid continuous mechanical stirring and heating $( 7 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ for 210 minutes. Te resulted magnetic photocatalysts, $\mathrm { M } @ \mathrm { F e } ( \mathrm { I I } ) \mathrm { P c }$ and $\mathbf { M } @ \operatorname { F e } ( \operatorname { I I I } ) \operatorname { P c }$ respectively, were fnally purifed (by repeated rinsing with ultrapure water and absolute ethanol alternated with magnetic decantation for removing the residual products) then dried in an oven under vacuum at $6 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ .

Catalytic activity. Kinetic experiments were carried out at laboratory-scale, in mild conditions (i.e. initial near-neutral $\mathrm { p H }$ of 6.6, room temperature $2 3 \pm 2 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ ) in order to assess the catalytic activity of the FePc-based photocatalysts for the BPA degradation in aqueous solution. A solution of $2 \mu \mathrm { m o l } \mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 }$ BPA was used as initial concentration. Before starting the kinetic experiments, the catalysts was tested in the dark in the presence of BPA; the solution was analyzed at regular time intervals indicating that no adsorption phenomena occurred. Next, the photooxidation tests were carried out in $5 0 \mathrm { - m L }$ Berzelius glasses, each one containing $5 \mathrm { m L }$ pollutant solution, placed on a cooled support and under a UVA light source (Analytik Jena bench lamp, $\lambda { = } 3 6 5 \mathrm { n m }$ , 15 W) consisting in two parallel self-fltering, low pressure ‘black light’ tubes; the diameter of the irradiated surface area was $1 1 . 3 \bar { 4 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 2 }$ , the irradiation path-length was $0 . 8 \mathrm { c m }$ , and photon fow of $9 . 2 8 \times 1 0 ^ { - 8 }$ Einstein $\mathsf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ (measured by polychromatic actinometry with phenylglyoxylic acid in $\mathrm { A c N } { : } \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } = 3 { : } 1 \mathrm { v } / \mathrm { v } ) ^ { 5 3 }$ . In the case of natural solar light exposure experiments, the same protocol was followed the diameter of the irradiated surface area was the same and photon fow was $9 . 6 7 \times 1 0 ^ { - 8 }$ Einstein $\mathsf { s } ^ { - 1 }$ . Te experiments were performed in July 2019, Iasi $( 4 7 ^ { \circ } 9 ^ { \prime } 6 . 2 1 3 6 ^ { \prime \prime }$ $\mathrm { N } , 2 7 ^ { \circ } 3 5 ^ { \prime } 1 6 . 4 9 0 4 ^ { \prime \prime } \mathrm { E } )$ , Romania.

Te catalytic performance of the synthesized photocatalysts was assessed by evaluating the efect of catalyst concentration, hydrogen peroxide dosage, under light exposure for a total reaction time of $1 2 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ . Catalyst concentration of 1.0, 2.0 and $3 . 0 \mathrm { g L } ^ { - 1 }$ were tested in the BPA degradation experiments at various time intervals with in the total reaction time of $1 2 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ . Five $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ concentrations, 5, 10, 15, 20 and $2 5 \mathrm { m m o l } \mathrm { L } ^ { - 1 }$ , freshly prepared from a $3 0 \%$ $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ standard solution, were studied in the next step. During kinetic experiments, aliquots of $3 0 0 \mu \mathrm { l }$ of BPA solution were withdrawn from the reaction vessels at diferent time intervals (15, 30, 60 and $1 2 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ ), immediately fltered through a $0 . 2 2 \mu \mathrm { m }$ Millipore nitro-cellulose membrane. Te concentration of BPA in the supernatant solutions was determined by a high performance liquid chromatograph (HPLC-UV-VIS/DAD Agilent Technologies 1260 Infnity series). Te description of experimental set-up and detailed method of analyses can be found in our previous study54. Te experiments were performed in duplicate, the calculated relative standard error being under $3 \%$ .

For determination of the dissolved iron, the Ferrozine method was used55.

Te detailed procedure of the MCF-7 cell viability assay, performed on the studied samples in order to test their cytotoxicity level, is described in our earlier publications37,56.
