Abstract: Heterogeneous Mg-Fe oxide/biochar (MgFeO@BC) nanocomposites were synthesized by a co-precipitation method and used as biochar-based catalysts to activate peroxymonosulfate (PMS) for sulfamethoxazole (SMX) removal. The optimal conditions for SMX degradation were examined as follows: pH 7.0, MgFeO@BC of $0 . 4 \ \mathrm { g / L } ,$ PMS concentration of $0 . 6 \mathrm { m M }$ and SMX concentration of $1 0 . 0 \mathrm { m g / L }$ at $2 5 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ . In the MgFeO@BC/PMS system, the removal efficiency of SMX was $9 9 . 0 \%$ in water within $4 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ under optimal conditions. In the MgFeO $@$ BC/PMS system, the removal efficiencies of tetracycline (TC), cephalexin (CEX), ciprofloxacin (CIP), 4-chloro-3-methyl phenol (CMP) and SMX within $4 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ are $9 5 . 3 \%$ , $9 8 . 4 \%$ , $9 8 . 2 \%$ , $9 7 . 5 \%$ and $9 9 . 0 \%$ , respectively. The radical quenching experiments and electron spin resonance (ESR) analysis suggested that both non-radical pathway and radical pathway advanced SMX degradation. SMX was oxidized by sulfate radicals $\left( \mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \bullet - } \right)$ , hydroxyl radicals $( \bullet \mathrm { O H } )$ and singlet oxygen $( ^ { 1 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } )$ , and $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } ^ { \bullet - } }$ acted as the main active species. MgFeO@BC exhibits a higher current density, and therefore, a higher electron migration rate and redox capacity. Due to the large number of available binding sites on the surface of MgFeO@BC and the low amount of ion leaching during the catalytic reaction, the system has good anti-interference ability and stability. Finally, the intermediates of SMX were detected.

Keywords: biochar-based catalysts; sulfate radicals; peroxymonosulfate; nonradical pathway

# 1. Introduction

Sulfamethoxazole (SMX) is widely used as a sulfonamide antibiotic and can efficiently treat infectious diseases of humans and animals [1,2]. The pathways of SMX into the ecosystem mainly include insufficiently treated wastewater discharge, pharmaceutical industry wastewater discharge, agricultural activities and leakages from septic systems [3]. Generally, SMX is released into the aquatic environment via effluent discharge from pharmaceutical manufacturing plants. Because of the potential carcinogenicity of SMX, it has serious effects on human health [4–6]. Therefore, the development of pollutant degradation technology has received more attention [7].

Persulfate-based advanced oxidation processes (PS-AOPs) were verified early to be efficient for in situ chemical oxidation of contaminant due to its unique superiority such as higher oxidation potential and wider applicability under the environments [8–10]. Unlike $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ,$ solid peroxymonosulfate (PMS) is easy to be stored, transported, and delivered [11]. The reactivity of peroxymonosulfate (PMS) is higher than peroxodisulfate (PDS) due to the structural asymmetry [12]. Peroxymonosulfate (PMS) can be activated by ultrasound, electrolysis, UV light, heating and transition metals [4,13]. Carbon-based materials have the advantages of low cost, abundant surface functionality and superior thermal stability [12]. Therefore, carbon-based materials could act as the well PMS activator and promote the removal of sulfamethoxazole (SMX).

Biochar (BC), as one of typical carbon-based materials, is derived from thermal pyrolysis of biomass feedstock, such as sludge, crop residues and animal litters [14]. BC is featured with abundant surface functional groups (e.g., C-OH, -COOH, $C { = } 0 )$ and a developed porous structure [11,15]. In addition, persistent free radicals (PFRs) on the surface of biochar (BC) have proved to be responsible for the catalytic capacity of catalysts [11]. Jung et al. reported that $\mathrm { M g O }$ -modified biochar composites derived from $\mathrm { M g }$ -enriched biomass show superior adsorption performance due to its increased available binding sites [16]. However, the difficulty of separating $\mathrm { M g O }$ -modified biochar from reaction solutions has yet to be resolved, which has limited large-scale application of $\mathrm { M g O }$ -modified biochar to wastewater remediation. Qin et al. reported that $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 }$ had been used in a Fenton-like process, which exhibited a high magnetic behavior [17]. Compared with $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 } .$ , Fe-Mg oxide loaded on biochar can reduce secondary pollution released by Fe [18]. Magnesium ferrite is a well-known cubic spinel ferrite nanoparticle, wherein the tetrahedral and octahedral cation sites are coordinated and occupied by divalent $\mathrm { M g ^ { 2 + } }$ and trivalent $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ , respectively. Therefore, Fe-Mg oxide loaded on biochar is more stable [19].

In this work, green, efficient, non-toxic and recyclable $\mathrm { F e - M g }$ oxide co-doped biochar $( { \mathrm { M g F e O @ B C } } )$ was synthetized by a co-precipitation method and used as a PMS activator for the removal of SMX. In the MgFeO@BC/PMS system, the removal efficiency of SMX was $9 9 . 0 \%$ in water within $4 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ under optimal conditions. Batch experiments were performed to investigate the catalytic performance of $\mathrm { M g F e O @ B C }$ . Moreover, the reactive oxygen species (ROS) in the MgFeO@BC/PMS system were identified by radical quenching tests and electron spin resonance (ESR) tests. The activation mechanism of PMS and degradation pathways of SMX were discussed. Finally, the practical application prospect of the MgFeO@BC/PMS system was evaluated through the interference experiment of anion and natural organic matter.

The loading of $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 }$ and $\mathrm { M g O }$ significantly changes the morphology of biochar. There is an obvious loading of oxide particles on the surface of $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 } @ \mathrm { B C }$ and $\mathrm { M g O @ B C } ,$ but the distribution of these oxide particles on the surface of carbon-based materials is not uniform (Figure $^ { \mathrm { 1 b , c } } .$ ). The pyrolysis samples were cooled to room temperature under the protection of $\Nu _ { 2 }$ $\mathrm { N } _ { 2 }$ flow rate: $4 0 \mathrm { m L } / \mathrm { m i n } )$ , washed in deionized water several times to neutral and then dried overnight $( 1 0 5 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ to obtain the samples. The samples were named MgFeO@BC. For comparison, $\mathrm { M g O @ B C } ,$ $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 } @ \mathrm { B C }$ 2⋅ 2 3⋅ 2 and BC were prepared in the same method.

# 3.2. Reaction Procedures

All tests were carried out in $5 0 \mathrm { m L }$ beakers containing $5 0 \mathrm { m L }$ of SMX solution (SMX concentration $= 1 0 . 0 ~ \mathrm { m g / L ) }$ to evaluate the catalytic performance of MgFeO@BC. An amount of $0 . 1 \mathrm { M N a O H }$ or $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S O } _ { 4 }$ was added into the reaction solution to adjust the initial $\mathrm { p H }$ values before reaction. All experiments were performed in triplicate. The range of $\mathrm { M g F e O @ B C }$ dosing was $0 . 2 \mathrm { - } 0 . 8 \mathrm { g / L } ,$ , the concentration of PMS was $0 . 2 \mathrm { - } 0 . 8 \mathrm { m M }$ , the $\mathrm { p H }$ was 3.0–11.0, SMX concentration was $0 . 5 { - } 1 0 . 0 \mathrm { m g / L }$ and the reaction temperature was set to $2 5 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ . All tests were carried out at $1 5 0 \mathrm { r p m } ,$ , and $1 . 0 \mathrm { m L }$ reaction solution was obtained periodically at intervals and filtered through a $0 . 4 5 ~ { \mu \mathrm { m } }$ filter. The excess reaction was terminated by adding 1 mL ${ \mathrm { N a } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { S } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { O } } _ { 3 }$ $( 0 . 2 \mathbf { M } )$ . The recycling experiment was performed for 5 cycles. After each cycle, MgFeO@BC was collected and dried in an oven at $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 2 \mathrm { { h } }$ for further use.

# 4. Conclusions

In this study, $\mathrm { M g F e O @ B C }$ bimetallic composite was successfully prepared by a coprecipitation method. The practical application potential of $\mathrm { M g F e O @ B C }$ was comprehensively investigated, and the mechanism of SMX degradation in the MgFeO $@$ BC/PMS system was revealed. (1) MgFeO@BC has a larger specific surface area $( \mathrm { S _ { B E T } } = 4 6 8 . 9 1 \ \mathrm { m ^ { 2 } { \cdot } g ^ { - 1 } ) }$ , a developed porous structure and more defective sites $( \mathrm { I _ { D } } / \mathrm { I _ { G } } = 1 . 3 8 )$ , and thus obtains excellent adsorption and catalytic performance, promotes the mass transfer and shortens the migration distance between the PMS and SMX. (2) The saturation magnetization value of MgFeO@BC $( 7 . 0 5 \mathrm { \ e m u / g ) }$ is low, and MgFeO@BC maintains good magnetic recovery performance while also reducing the accumulation of iron oxides. (3) The PMS adsorbed on the surface of MgFeO@BC first enters into the redox cycle between Fe (II) and Fe (III) for decomposition, forming $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } ^ { \bullet - } }$ , •OH and $^ 1 \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ,$ among which $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } ^ { \bullet - } }$ is the most active species in the system.
