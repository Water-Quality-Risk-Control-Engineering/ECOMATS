# . Introduction

As an important strategic material, the development and utilization of non-ferrous metals bring not only great economic benefits to the society, but also a series of environmental pollution problems [1,2]. Among them, the problem of heavy metal pollution in mining areas has attracted extensive attention [36], but less attention has been paid to the organic flotation reagent pollution generated in the process of mineral production and processing [710]. Flotation is currently the most widespread technology used to beneficiate non-ferrous metals [11]. According to the statistics, the global annual consumption of flotation reagents has exceeded 1 million tons [11,12]. Among them are some Substances of Very High Concern (SVHC) with known carcinogenic and teratogenic effects. $\alpha$ Nitroso $\boldsymbol { \cdot } \boldsymbol { \beta }$ naphthol $\mathrm { ( \alpha { \tt N } \beta N ) }$ , a typical organic flotation reagent, is widely used as a mine chelating reagent in the flotation processes of cobalt ores and tungsten ores. However, it is also recognized as a carcinogenic toxic compound due to its high chemical stability and environmental persistence [12]. Without effective treatment, it may seriously threaten the surrounding ecological security [13]. Therefore, it is very important and necessary to effectively eliminate these toxic compounds in the mining environment.

Recently, some physical, chemical and biological remediation technologies have been developed for dealing with the pollution of organic flotation reagents in mining areas, such as coagulation-flocculation [14,15], biodegradation [11], ozone oxidation [16,17], photocatalytic degradation [12], and so on. However, these technologies still have the disadvantages of complex operation process, high energy consumption, strict microbial culture conditions and long treatment cycle. In addition, heterogeneous advanced oxidation processes (AOPs) based on the activation of various catalytic materials have attracted extensive attention due to their relatively simple treatment process and short treatment period [1820]. Despite that, most of the existing catalytic materials usually have the disadvantages of a complex preparation process, high production cost and strict effective pH conditions [21]. As is known to all, most of the mining areas in China are located in remote areas with underdeveloped economy. Obviously, these limitations seriously hinder their practical engineering application, especially in the large-scale remediation of organic pollution in mining areas. Therefore, developing remediation technology with high performance and low cost has become an urgent need for the treatment of organic pollution in mining areas.

In recent years, the use of waste as raw material for pollution remediation has become an important breakthrough in reducing remediation costs [2225]. Steel converter slag (SCS) is the waste slag produced in the steelmaking process. According to the statistics, $1 5 0 { \sim } 2 0 0$ kg of steel slag is produced with each ton of steel [26]. As the world's largest steel producer, China produces a large amount of SCS every year, but its comprehensive utilization rate is lower than $3 0 ~ \%$ How to effectively manage these waste steel slags has become the key to the sustainable development of the steel industry. At present, SCS is mainly used as the raw material for cement, concrete and other building materials [27]. A small part of SCS is modified and used for production of adsorption materials [2831], particle electrodes [32], etc. It is worth noting that SCS is an attractive and low-cost catalytic material because it contains a large amount of metal oxides [22,33]. Cheng et al. [22,33,34] innovatively modified the SCS by using salicylic acidmethanol mixture and cobalt nitrate, and successfully removed organic pollutants such as alachlor and sulfamethazine. Vasiliki et al. [26] successfully applied SCS to the degradation of propyl p-hydroxybenzoate by oxidative digestion modification in an acidic medium. All of these studies have effectively optimized the performance of sCS and broadened its applications. However, most of them need to modify the SCS, which still requires acidic conditions. The catalytic degradation mainly depends on hydroxyl radicals, which also makes its anti-interference ability weaker. All of these have hindered its practical application in the remediation of mining areas. Recently, the non-radical oxidation pathway has attracted much attention due to its low sensitivity to environmental factors, wide operating pH range, and high selectivity for electron-rich organic pollutants [35]. It has been found that the incorporation of $\mathrm { M g O / C a O }$ onto catalytic materials can trigger non-radical pathways, which still have good catalytic efficiency in alkaline environments [36,37]. Combined with the main composition of SCS, which is rich in CaO and $\mathbf { M g O }$ ,we speculate that SCS may also possess the ability to produce non-radicals. However, little research has been reported on the degradation of organic pollutants by SCS via non-radical pathways. The use of SCS as a catalytic material to remove organic flotation reagents in mining areas has also not been reported. If SCS could be applied in the remediation of organic flotation reagents pollution in mining areas, that would undoubtedly not only effectively reduce the cost of pollution remediation, but also promote the sustainable development of the steel-mining industry.

In this study, SCS was introduced into the field of organic pollution remediation in mining areas for the first time. Based on the principle of "treating waste with waste", sCS was used as an efficient and low-cost catalytic material for catalytic degradation of $\alpha$ nitroso $\boldsymbol { \cdot } \boldsymbol { \beta }$ -naphthol (αNβN), a typical organic flotation reagent in mining areas. The aim of the present study is to: 1) evaluate the performance and the influencing factors of SCS catalytic degradation of αNβN; 2) clarify the degradation mechanism under the experimental conditions used in this study, investigate whether radical and non-radical pathways play a role, and reveal the possible role of SCS in the removal process of αNβN; 3) investigate the anti-interference ability and reusability of SCS in the proposed reaction; and 4) analyze degradation products of αNβN, and the toxicity changes in water solutions containing αNβN during the catalytic degradation of SCS.

# 2.3. αNβN catalytic degradation experiment

The degradation experiments of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ were all carried out in $6 0 ~ \mathrm { m L }$ amber glass bottles. Except for the influencing factor experiment, all experiments were carried out under the following conditions: the initial concentration of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ was $5 0 ~ \mathrm { m g / L }$ , the initial $\mathtt { p H }$ was $3 . 0 0 \pm 0 . 0 1$ (adjusted by 0.1 M $\mathrm { H N O _ { 3 } }$ or NaOH solution, and was not controlled during the reactions), the addition amount of SCS was $_ { 0 . 1 g }$ , the addition amount of $3 0 \ \% \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ was $5 0 0 \mu \mathrm { L } ,$ , the shaking speed was $1 0 0 \mathrm { r p m }$ , and the ambient temperature was room temperature $2 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ . The specific operation is as follows: added $0 . 1 \ g$ of SCS to $5 0 ~ \mathrm { m L }$ of αNβN solution, then added $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ to start the reaction. At the sampling time point, $1 ~ \mathrm { m L }$ of the reaction solution was extracted, and immediately added $2 0 ~ \mu \mathrm { L }$ of 1 M $\mathrm { H _ { 2 } S O _ { 4 } }$ and an excess of methanol to quench the reaction. Finally, the reaction mixture was filtered through a $0 . 2 2 ~ { \mu \mathrm { m } }$ membrane. After filtration, the $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ concentration in the samples was immediately determined.

In addition, quenching experiments were conducted to analyze ROS during the reaction. The experimental conditions were: the initial concentration of αNβN was $5 0 ~ \mathrm { m g / L }$ , the initial $\mathtt { p H }$ was $3 . 0 0 \pm 0 . 0 1$ , the additional amount of SCS was $0 . 1 \ : g$ , the additional volume of $3 0 \% \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ was $1 0 0 ~ \mu \mathrm { L }$ , the shaking speed was $1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { r p m }$ . # 3. Results and discussion

# 3.1. Catalytic removal of αNβN

# 3.1.1. Reaction activity

In order to investigate the catalytic performance of SCS, $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ removal experiments in different reaction systems were conducted. Four reaction systems including the control ( αNβN alone), SCS alone, $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ alone and ${ \mathrm { S C S } } + { \mathrm { H } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { O } } _ { 2 }$ were compared. Fig. 1 shows that either SCS or $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ alone were unable to remove $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ effectively. After $6 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ of reaction, the removal efficiency of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ in SCS alone and $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ alone were only $0 . 2 3 ~ \%$ and $2 . 9 2 ~ \%$ , respectively. These results indicate that the adsorption capacity of SCS for $\mathsf { \alpha { N } \beta { N } }$ was limited, and $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ alone could not effectively oxidize and degrade it because $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ was relatively stable. In contrast, when SCS was combined with $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ ,it exhibited excellent αNβN removal performance, and more than $9 8 . 8 ~ \%$ of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ could be removed within $6 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ of reaction. This may be due to the active sites on the surface of sCs, which can effectively activate $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ and produce active species to remove $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ Vasiliki et al. [26] also found that using modified sCS materials can effectively activate $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ to produce hydroxyl radicals. Overall, the ${ \mathsf { S C S } } + { \mathsf { H } } _ { 2 } { \mathsf { O } } _ { 2 }$ system had a strong $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ removal performance. Compared with the previous research results [12], the ${ \mathrm { S C S } } + { \mathrm { H } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { O } } _ { 2 }$ system showed obvious advantages in the removal efficiency and remediation cost of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ .

# 3.1.2. Influencing factors of αNβN degradation

3.1.2.1. Effect of SCS dosage. SCS in different dosages was used to evaluate its effect on αNβN removal. Fig. 2a shows that the removal efficiency of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ gradually increased with the increase of SCS dosage. Specifically, after $6 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ of reaction, the removal efficiency of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ were $3 . 1 7 \%$ , $8 5 . 6 1 \%$ , $9 2 . 4 0 \%$ , $9 6 . 0 8 \%$ and $9 8 . 8 \%$ when the dosage of SCS was $\mathrm { 0 . 8 / L , 0 . 2 5 \mathrm { g / L , 0 . 5 \mathrm { g / L , 1 \mathrm { g / L } } } }$ , and ${ 2 g / \mathrm { L } }$ , respectively. This was mainly due to the number of reactive sites in the system, which gradually increased with the increase of the amount of SCS added, thus enhancing the catalytic effect of SCS on $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ [22,38]. When the dosage of SCS was further increased to $\textrm { 3 g / L }$ , the removal efficiency of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ was $9 8 . 9 4 ~ \%$ , which was only $0 . 1 4 ~ \%$ higher than that of ${ } _ { 2 } \ g / \mathrm { L }$ .This observation might be due to the constant concentration of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ in the reaction system. Because of that, the activation effect of the excessive SCS on $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ was limited when the dosage of SCS reached saturation [22]. Therefore, considering the removal efficiency of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ and treatment cost, ${ 2 g / \mathrm { L } }$ was selected as the optimal SCS dosage in the following experiments.

3.1.2.2. Effect of $H _ { 2 } O _ { 2 }$ concentration. The effect of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ concentration on the degradation of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ is shown in Fig. 2b. Without the addition of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ , the removal of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ was very limited $( 5 . 0 7 \% )$ . On the contrary, when $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ was added to the reaction system, the removal efficiency increased obviously. The removal rate of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ increased from 0.071 $\operatorname* { m i n } ^ { - 1 }$ to 0. $1 9 4 ~ \mathrm { { m i n } ^ { - 1 } }$ with the increase of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ concentration from $0 . 2 5 \mathrm { m L }$ to $1 \mathrm { m L }$ , indicating that the dosage of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ was directly related to the removal of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ . It is speculated that with the increase of the concentration of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ,$ the amount of ROS produced in the process of activating $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ by SCS also increases, thus effectively accelerating the removal of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ [39,40]. Considering that $0 . 5 0 \mathrm { m L }$ of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ was able to remove more than $9 8 . 0 \%$ of $\mathsf { \alpha { N } \beta { N } }$ , this concentration was used for most of the batch experiments.

Effect of the initial $p H .$ Under different pH conditions, the decomposition of oxidants and the dissociation behaviors of pollutants will be different, so the initial pH is one of the most important factors in the catalytic degradation process. As shown in Fig. 2c, the removal efficiency of αNβN reached more than $9 5 \%$ after $6 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ of reaction in the pH range studied, indicating that SCS can be used in a wide pH range. (2) The real-time $\mathsf { p H }$ range at initial $\mathrm { p H } ~ 2$ was 5.58.3, while the real-time pH range of the other experimental groups was 9.810.5. The traditional Fenton reaction is more effective under acidic conditions because $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ was mainly replaced by $\mathrm { H O ^ { - } }$ as complexes $\mathrm { [ F e ( O H ) ( H _ { 2 } O ) _ { 5 } ] ^ { + } }$ and $\mathrm { F e ( O H ) } _ { 2 } ( \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } ) _ { 4 }$ in the pH range from 3 to 3.5, which can more effectively excite $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ to produce $\boldsymbol { \cdot } _ { \mathrm { O H } }$ and thus promoting the removal of pollutants [42]. However, in this study, the pH value of the system increased gradually after the addition of SCS, which exceeded the optimum pH range. In addition, it has been found that $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ can be effectively activated under alkaline conditions, producing ROS (Eqs. (3)(8)) to remove organic pollutants [43,44]. Therefore, compared with other experimental groups, the realtime $\mathtt { p H }$ range at initial $\mathrm { p H } ~ 2$ was close to neutral, and the alkaline activation was weakened, so the degradation efficiency was relatively low.

Traditional AOPs typically require strict control of the pH [45,46]. 3.1.2.4. Effects of interference ions. In previous studies, some common environmental interference ions have a great impact on the removal of organic pollutants. Since free radicals can react with some interfering ions, these reactions can reduce their efficiency of action [47]. Therefore, it was necessary to evaluate the influence of coexisting ions on the degradation of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ by the ${ \mathrm { S C S } } + { \mathrm { H } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { O } } _ { 2 }$ system.

We investigated the influence of common inorganic anions $\left( \mathbf { C l } ^ { - } \right)$ $S O _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - }$ , $\mathrm { N O } _ { 3 } ^ { - } ,$ and $\mathrm { H C O } _ { 3 } ^ { - } )$ and inorganic cations $( \mathrm { K } ^ { + }$ , $\mathrm { M g } ^ { 2 + }$ , $\mathrm { C a ^ { 2 + } }$ ,and $\mathrm { N a ^ { + } }$ on the degradation of αNβN. However, the results of this study (Fig. 3a) showed that the $\mathrm { S C S } + \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ system had a powerful anti-interference ability. The removal efficiency of $\mathsf { \alpha { N } \beta { N } }$ did not change noticeably after adding various interference ions. According to the analysis results of the system ROS in Section 3.2, it is speculated that this phenomenon is mainly due to the existence of non-radical oxidation pathways, including $^ 1 0 _ { 2 }$ and electron transfer during $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ degradation, which have a strong anti-interference ability.

In addition to the common inorganic anion and cations, various heavy metal ions also widely exist in the mining environment [5]. It has been reported that the coexistence of organic flotation reagents and heavy metal ions may change their migration and transformation behavior [13,48]. Therefore, the effects of several common heavy metal ions (Cd, Pb, Cr, and Sb) on the degradation of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ were investigated to evaluate the availability of the ${ \mathsf { S C S } } + { \mathrm { H } } _ { 2 } { \mathsf { O } } _ { 2 }$ system in the real mining environment. Fig. 3a showed that heavy metal ions had no obvious inhibitory effect on the degradation of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ . The removal efficiency of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ after adding $1 0 \mathrm { p p m }$ of Cd, Pb, Cr, and Sb ions to the system were 98. $2 0 \%$ $9 8 . 9 3 \%$ . $9 8 . 7 5 \%$ and 98. $6 9 \%$ , respectively. Previous studies have shown that SCS has strong adsorption capacity and chemical precipitation ability, which can be used as an adsorbent to effectively remove heavy metal ions such as $\mathbf { P b }$ , Zn, Cr, Ni, and Cd [28,29]. Therefore, in the ${ \sf S C S } + { \sf H } _ { 2 } { \sf O } _ { 2 }$ system, the reason why the addition of heavy metal ions has no obvious effect may be ascribed to the adsorption and chemical precipitation removal of heavy metal ions by SCS. In addition, the generation of $^ 1 0 _ { 2 }$ also enhanced the resistance of the reaction system in a complex aqueous matrix [18]. In summary, the ${ \mathsf { S C S + } }$ $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ system has good anti-interference ability for heavy metal ions, showing strong adaptability to the mining environment.

# 3.1.3. Removal of aNβN in real waters

In order to further evaluate the practical application performance of the $\mathrm { S C S \ + \ H _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } }$ system, the degradation effect of αNβN in different water matrices was investigated, including mine water collected from southwest China and tap water. The main parameters of the real water samples are listed in Table S1. It could be seen from Fig. 3b that, compared with ultrapure water, there was no obvious difference in the removal efficiency of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ in tap water and mine water, which reached $9 8 . 7 ~ \%$ and $9 8 . 4 ~ \%$ , respectively. Similar phenomena have also been observed in other catalytic degradation systems with the participation of non-radical pathways mediated by $^ 1 0 _ { 2 }$ [36,49]. Normally, the degradation efficiency of traditional radical-based AOPs reaction systems in real water samples will be weakened, because radicals are easily interfered by coexisting substances such as $\mathrm { C l ^ { - } }$ , $S O _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - }$ in real water [50]. However, the non-radical pathway shows a strong anti-interference ability. Combined with the interference ions influence experiments, the ${ \mathsf { S C S } } + { \mathsf { H } } _ { 2 } { \mathsf { O } } _ { 2 }$ system shows strong anti-interference performance and the potential for simultaneous removal of heavy metals. In summary, the excellent degradation efficiency of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ in real water indicated that the ${ \sf S C S } + { \sf H } _ { 2 } { \sf O } _ { 2 }$ system had great potential for future practical treatment.

# 3.1.4. Reusability and stability of SCS

A cyclic degradation experiment was performed to evaluate the reusability and stability of SCs. The results of the recycling experiment (Fig. S1) showed that, after SCS was reused several times, there was no obvious decrease in its degradation performance. After 4 times of continuous operation, the removal rate of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ was $8 3 . 5 \%$ . In addition, the XRD (Fig. S2), SEM (Fig. S3), and other results also showed that SCS had good stability. At the same time, SCS only needs to stand for a few minutes to achieve natural sedimentation separation, so the subsequent recovery and reuse are also very convenient.

# 3.2. Mechanism of αNβN degradation and $H _ { 2 } O _ { 2 }$ activation

# 3.2.1. Identification of radical pathways in $S C S + H _ { 2 } O _ { 2 }$ system

In general, the removal of organic pollutants by catalytic activation of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ is mainly due to the radicals produced in the reaction system, which is essential because of its strong oxidizing ability [39,51]. In order to explore the possible radicals in the $\mathrm { S C S \ + \ H _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } }$ system and their contribution to the degradation of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ , a series of experiments was conducted.

Firstly, we carried out quenching experiments. Related studies have confirmed that hydroxyl radical (•OH) is the primary radical of the $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ activation system [22,33]. According to the fast reaction rate constant of tert-butanol (TBA) with •OH $( k ( \mathrm { T B A } , \mathbf { \bullet } \mathrm { O H } ) = 6 \times 1 0 ^ { 8 } \mathrm { M } ^ { - 1 } s ^ { - 1 } )$ , TBA was selected as the scavenger for •OH [52,53]. Fig. 4a shows that the removal efficiency of αNβN was reduced by $4 4 . 3 5 ~ \%$ after adding excess TBA (TBA: $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } = 2 0 0 { : } 1 )$ , indicating that •OH played a role in the degradation process. However, unlike the traditional $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ activation system, the addition of excessive TBA in this study could not completely inhibit the removal of $\mathsf { \alpha { N } \beta { N } }$ , indicating that besides the ·OH radical, there might have been other ROS involved in the reaction. Furthermore, $p$ . benzoquinone (BQ) was used as scavenger for $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { - } ( k ( \mathrm { B Q } , \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { - } ) = 9 . 6 \times$ $1 0 ^ { 8 } \mathrm { M } ^ { - 1 } \thinspace s ^ { - 1 } )$ [54]. The results showed (Fig. 4a) that the addition of BQ could not effectively inhibit the removal of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ The removal efficiency was reduced by $3 . 6 7 \%$ only, indicating that $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ^ { \bullet - }$ did not play any prominent roles in the process of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ removal. Moreover, in the early stage of the reaction, the presence of BQ slightly accelerated the removal rate of αNβN, which may be due to the acceleration of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ activation by BQ under alkaline conditions. It has been demonstrated that benzoquinone could activate PMS to produce $^ 1 0 _ { 2 }$ via a dioxane intermediate under alkaline conditions, resulting in the effective removal of organic pollutants such as sulfamethoxazole through a non-radical pathway [55].

HPLC-MS was used to detect and analyze the intermediate products in the system after the degradation reaction. Subsequently, the nitro group $\left( - \mathbf { N O } _ { 2 } \right)$ was removed to produce 1,2-naphthalenediol, which was further oxidized, to produce 1,2- naphthalenedione. Then, the benzene ring was opened to produce 1,2- benzenedicarboxylic acid. Followed by decarboxylation and hydroxylation, the benzoic acid and salicylic acid were produced respectively. Finally, it is further degraded to fumaric acid, carbon oxides (COx) and $_ \mathrm { H _ { 2 } O }$ .

# 4. Conclusion

In this study, SCS was used for the first time as a low-cost catalyst to activate $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ and efficiently remove $\mathsf { \alpha { N } \beta { N } _ { : } }$ , a typical organic flotation reagent in the mining area. In the ${ \sf S C S } + { \sf H } _ { 2 } { \sf O } _ { 2 }$ system, more than $9 8 . 8 \%$ of $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ can be removed within $6 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ , and the system can be applied in a wide $\mathsf { p H }$ range. The results of the quenching experiment, EPR and electrochemical analysis indicated that both radical and non-radical pathways play an important role in the removal of $\mathsf { \alpha { N } \beta { N } }$ ,with OH and $^ 1 0 _ { 2 }$ as the main reactive oxygen species. Fe, bridging OH and terminal OH on the surface of SCS were the active sites for $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ activation. The intermediate products were identified by HPLC-MS analysis, and the microcalorimetric analysis results showed that the ${ \mathrm { S C S } } + { \mathrm { H } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { O } } _ { 2 }$ system could effectively reduce the ecotoxicity of a water solution containing $\mathsf { \alpha { N \beta N } }$ , Due to the participation of non-radical pathways, the system exhibits strong anti-interference ability and good reusability. Compared with the previous research results, the $\mathrm { S C S \ + \ H _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } }$ system showed obvious advantages in the removal efficiency of αNβN, the operability of the reaction system and the remediation cost. This study provides not only a new strategy for the remediation of organic pollution in mining areas, but also a new idea for the sustainable development of the steel industry.
