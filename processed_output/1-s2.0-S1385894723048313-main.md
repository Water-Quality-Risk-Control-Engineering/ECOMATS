# . Introduction

Antibiotics have been considerably utilized for a couple of decades in various human activities including chemotherapy, surgery, transplantation and CovID-19, etc. [1,2]. These drugs are also used for medical and non-medical reasons in animal husbandry, crop production improvement and aquaculture [37]. People have become progressively dependent on antibiotics and have overused them. Analytically, the use of antibiotics systematically increased worldwide after the COvID-19 pandemic [812]. Tragically, incomplete metabolism and the uses and overuses of antibiotics inevitably increased the antibiotic residues in aquatic habitats, including groundwater, municipal wastewater, and the surface water [1315].

Antibiotic residues in aquatic ecosystems can pose a threat to human health [16,17]. First, antibiotic vulnerability over a long duration can alter the spreading of microbial populations in the aqueous medium, thereby affecting the other innovative organisms through the food chain transmission, and compromising ecosystem safety. Second, antibiotics at low concentrations can promote the preference of antibiotic-resistant bacteria and antibiotic-resistance genes [1824]. Third, antibiotic residues pose a significant risk of serious and chronic toxicity and can cause significant damage to human health. For example, sulfonamides may easily cause allergic reactions in sensitive individuals [25]; accretion of tetracycline can prevent the growth of bones in children [22]. Countless diseases and millions of deaths are the result of polluted water worldwide. Therefore, it is most important to come across effective and inexpensive technology to completely remove such pollutants [2629].

At present various treatment technologies have been extensively used and developed to remove the antibiotics from wastewater such as physicochemical, biological and electrochemical, etc. These treatment methods have particular limitations that hindered their widespread application. Thus, it is crucial to find a highly effective and environmentally friendly method that converts organic contaminants into harmless and non-toxic by-products [22,30,31]. In a comparison of the above procedures, advanced oxidation processes (AOPs) are considered a promising treatment technology due to their excellent ability to produce a variety of radical and non-radical ROS such as hydroxyl radicals (OH) $[ \mathrm { E } ^ { 0 } = 1 . 8 \substack { - 2 . 7 4 } \mathrm { V } ]$ , sulfate radicals $\left( S 0 _ { 4 } ^ { * - } \right)$ $[ \mathbf { E } ^ { 0 } = 2 . 5 - 3 . 1 $ V], superoxide radicals $( 0 _ { 2 } ^ { * - } )$ $[ \mathbf { E } ^ { 0 } = 1 . 7 ~ \mathrm { V } ]$ , and non-radical singlet oxygen $( ^ { 1 } 0 _ { 2 } )$ $[ \boldsymbol { \mathrm { E } } ^ { 0 } = 0 . 8 1$ V], etc., which have the ability to degrade the antibiotics into easily degradable intermediates with lesser toxicity [32,33]. Among AOPs, the activation approaches have attained extensive consideration, which will produce strong ROS that have higher redox potential, good reaction stability, longer half-life and wider pH range [34,35]. Various studies have observed the positive synergistic effects of activation with persulfate (PS) and photo-catalytic (PC) on antibiotic removal. PC activation can increase the use of solar energy with an excellent efficiency of solar energy absorption [25,32,33]. As a result, the traditional semiconductor photocatalysts such as $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ and $\mathrm { Z n O }$ showed poor PC abilities owing to their wide band gap and are activated only by UV light which is only $4 \%$ of the sunlight spectrum [22]. To find out the solution of these challenges, various researchers have been worked on the formation of visible light driven photocatalysts and collaborating with activation systems to synergistically mineralize recalcitrant pollutants such as antibiotics [35,36].

Bismuth-based semiconductor materials with outstanding carrier separation capacity of visible or near-visible light absorption, various chemical compositions, resistance to photo-corrosion, low production cost and rich crystal chemistry have been considered as promising photocatalysts to solve the environmental challenges. A variety of bismuth-based photocatalyst (BPC), like bismuth oxide $\mathbf { ( B i _ { 2 } O _ { 3 } ) }$ , bismuth oxy-halides (BiOX where $\mathrm { ~ X ~ } = \mathrm { I } ,$ , Br, Cl,), bismuth tungstate $\left( \mathrm { B i } _ { 2 } \mathrm { W O } _ { 6 } \right)$ , bismuth molybdate $\left( { \bf B i } _ { 2 } { \bf M o O } _ { 6 } \right)$ , bismuth titanate $\mathbf { ( B i _ { 4 } T i _ { 3 } O _ { 1 2 } ) }$ , bismuth vanadate $\mathrm { ( B i V O _ { 4 } ) }$ , bismuth subcarbonate $\mathrm { ( B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } ) }$ , bismuth phosphate $\mathrm { ( B i P O _ { 4 } ) }$ , etc., have shown excellent PC performance when exposed to solar energy or visible light [Fig. 1]. However, their actual application in the industry has not been fully clarified due to some problems with their structure. In addition, various approaches have been used to improve their catalytic activity. These include optimization of crystal facets, doping with metals or nonmetals, high bismuth content strategies, hierarchical microstructures and heterojunction design, etc [31,37].

Recently, several reports have been published on the recent progress of bismuth-based composite oxides and their PC application [3638]. Unfortunately, few literatures have systematically summarized the synthesis techniques of bismuth-based semiconductor materials and their applications in the degradation of antibiotics, which is a big challenge [37,3941]. However, synthesis techniques have a remarkable impression on physicochemical properties and PC performance. The present review proposed a systematic literature on the current developments of antibiotic degradation via photocatalysis. The basics of photocatalysis mechanism and PC activation with peroxydisulfate (PDS) or persulfate (PS), peroxymonosulfate (PMS), hydrogen peroxide $\left( \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathbf { O } _ { 2 } \right)$ and ozone $\left( \mathbf { O } _ { 3 } \right)$ are discussed and summarized. The effect of types and their structural modification of different bismuth-based photocatalysts (BPCs) on antibiotic degradation are evaluated and summarized. The role of fabrication techniques with their advantages, and disadvantages on crystal facets, metals or nonmetals doping, high bismuth content strategies, hierarchical microstructures and heterojunction designs are discussed. In particular, we precisely discuss the development in process performance and mineralization pathways of common antibiotics.

# 2. Classification and photocatalytic mechanism

# 2.1.Classification of bismuth-based photocatalysts

The metallic element bismuth with electron configuration $6 s ^ { 2 } 6 \mathrm { p } ^ { 3 }$ belongs to the sixth period and fifth group of the periodic table and generally occurs in the $\mathbf { B } \mathbf { i } ^ { 3 + }$ oxidation state [4244]. However, there is also the $\mathrm { B i } ^ { 5 + }$ state in which the 6 s orbital is vacant and which exhibits excellent visible light absorption [4547]. The deformation of bismuth 6 s orbital in bismuth-based oxides can lead to an overlap in the valence band of $\mathtt { B i } 6 s$ and O 2p orbitals, resulting in improvement in the visible light response by the reduction of band gap and an increase in mobility of the photoinduced charges. In past years, a variety of novel BPCs having various crystalline structures are mentioned in the literature. Based on their chemical composition, they can be broadly categorized into different categories such as bismuth sulfides, bismuth oxides, bismuth metal oxides and bismuth oxyhalides, etc. [13,48].

In addition, the band gap is an important feature of semiconductorbased photocatalysts. As mentioned in Table 1, many BPCs are visible light irradiation active with an energy gap of $< 3 . 0 \ \mathrm { e V }$ ,while some catalysts such as $\mathrm { L i B i O _ { 3 } }$ ,BiOI, and ${ \mathrm { B i } } _ { 2 } { \bf S } _ { 3 }$ exhibit excellent visible light photocatalysis performance with a band gap $< 2 . 0 \ \mathrm { e V }$ [49]. Table 1 listed the typical bismuth-based photocatalysts with their corresponding band gaps. In addition to a small band gap, strong redox capability, suitable band edge positions, long-term stability, and excellent chargeseparation efficiency are also required for the photocatalysts to enhance the degradation of antibiotics present in wastewater [49]. Due to the characteristic limitations of single-component photocatalysts, many controllable BPCs have been designed by structure mediation, morphology control, element doping and heterojunction construction [7]. In the following sections, specific details of the characteristics of various BPCs and their PC efficiency in the degradation of antibiotics are discussed in detail.

# 2.3. Role of activators in photocatalysis mechanism

Despite the increasing confidence about practical application of PC, comparatively limited real water treatment systems apply PC in place of commercial AOPs [65]. In recent years, PS activation has been fast investigated and developed as the best technology. Persulfate is separated into PMS $\left( \mathrm { H S O } _ { 5 } ^ { - } \right)$ and PDS $( S _ { 2 } O _ { 8 } ^ { 2 - } )$ . The bond dissociation energy of the double oxygen bond (O-O) in PMS and PDS is $3 7 7 \mathrm { k J / m o l }$ and 140 $\mathbf { k J } / \mathbf { m o l }$ , respectively, which is broken by the addition of a catalyst or external energy [66,67]. Different approaches have been used to activate the PS such as utilization of heat, ultraviolet light, ultrasonics, carbon materials, and transition metals [22,65]. During the photoactivation, a wide range of UV radiation $( 1 9 0 - 2 5 3 . 7 \ \mathrm { n m } )$ has been studied [68,69]. During the free radical and ROS formation, the synergistic effect of light radiation and catalysts facilitates the reaction positively [70,71]. # 3.3.3. Bismuth subcarbonate

$\mathrm { B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ is a well-studied solid carbonate that has a layered structure consisting of $\mathrm { B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } ^ { 2 + } }$ and $\mathrm { C O } _ { 3 } ^ { 2 - }$ layers, a major part of the trivalent $\mathbf { B i ^ { 3 + } }$ with a coordination number of 8 and associated $\mathrm { C O } _ { 3 } ^ { 2 - }$ group planes are orthogonal to the $\mathrm { B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } ^ { 2 + } }$ layers. The alteration character of the Bi-O polyhedron in $\mathrm { B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ can offer a large surface area for PC activity (Fig. 4g) [7,14,125]. It has a high band gap $( \sim 3 . 4 \mathrm { e V } )$ and shows only UV light activity, therefore the PC activity needs to be enhanced by coupling them with lower bandgap containing semiconductors to increase the light absorption capacity. For example, Lai et al. [126] synthesized a $\mathrm { C e O _ { 2 } / B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ heterojunction that showed performance in about $8 0 \%$ of organic pollutants degradation after $^ { 1 . 5 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ when $3 5 ~ \mathrm { m g }$ $\mathrm { C e O _ { 2 } / B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ photocatalyst was mixed with $2 0 \ \mathrm { \ m g L } ^ { - 1 }$ organic contaminant in $1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { m L }$ , while Wang et al. [5] prepared the PMS activated $\mathrm { Z n F e _ { 2 } O _ { 4 } / B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } / B i O B r }$ heterojunction in different morphology by using the hydrothermal route of synthesis. Higher than $9 3 \%$ TC, $9 1 \%$ oxytetracycline and $9 2 \%$ doxycycline were degraded within $2 0 ~ \mathrm { { m i n } }$ under sunlight irradiation and PMS addition.

# 3.3.4. Bismuth molybdate

${ \bf B i _ { 2 } M o O _ { 6 } }$ is an aurivillius oxide perovskite with $\mathrm { \ B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } }$ 2p layers surrounded by corner sharing $\scriptstyle { \mathrm { M o O } } _ { 6 }$ octahedra, forming a moderately narrow band-gap $( 2 . 5 \mathrm { - } 2 . 8 \mathrm { e V } )$ . In ${ \bf B i _ { 2 } M o O _ { 6 } }$ , the alternating of $\mathrm { ( B i _ { 2 } O _ { 3 } ) ^ { 2 + } }$ and $( \mathrm { M o O } _ { 6 } ) ^ { 2 - }$ layers facilitates electron transfer. It arises in various forms such as $\scriptstyle \gamma - \mathbf { B i } _ { 2 } \mathbf { M o O } _ { 6 }$ $\mathbf { \beta } \mathbf { \mathrm { { \beta } \mathrm { { - } } \mathbf { { B i } } _ { 2 } M o O _ { 6 } } }$ ,and $\mathbf { \alpha } _ { \mathrm { { ( - } } } \mathbf { B i _ { 2 } } \mathbf { M o O _ { 6 } }$ . It shows the response to visible-light around $5 0 0 \mathrm { n m }$ In ${ \bf B i _ { 2 } M o O _ { 6 } }$ , the CB contains Bi 6p and Mo $\mathfrak { 4 p }$ orbitals, while VB is made up of O 2p orbitals (Fig. 4g) [8,51,114,127,128]. Various treatment methods have been effectively used for the synthesis of ${ \bf B i _ { 2 } M o O _ { 6 } }$ . For example, Zhang et al. [129] used a hydrothermal anion exchange approach to generate the porous $\mathbf { B i M O _ { x } }$ nanoflake $[ \mathbf { M } = \mathbf { V } $ , W, and Mo) by replacing the $\mathsf { W O } _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - }$ in ${ \bf B i _ { 2 } M o O _ { 6 } }$ with $\mathrm { M o O } _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - }$ [129], while Shen et al. [130] used the simple solvothermal approach to synthesize the novel $\mathrm { B i _ { 2 } M o O _ { 6 } / B i _ { 2 } M o O _ { 6 - x } }$ homojunction, which showed 18 times better PC performance than pure ${ \bf B i _ { 2 } M o O _ { 6 } }$ Feng et al. [114] reported the PS activated ${ \bf B i _ { 2 } M o O _ { 6 } }$ system, which had superb about $8 3 \%$ TC-HCl degradation within $6 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ during the visible light irradiation because it decreased the $\mathrm { e ^ { - } \mathrm { h ^ { + } } }$ pairs recombination rate and constructed the heterojunctions. Similarly, Guo et al. [127] observed that the $8 7 . 6 8 \%$ of norfloxacin was degraded within $3 0 \ \mathrm { m i n }$ because oxidation capabilities increased via the formation of ROS.

# 4. Effect of fabrication techniques on photocatalytic performance

# 4.1. Solid-state reaction

The photocatalyst synthesis via solid-state reaction approach is referred to as mechanochemical synthesis because chemical reactions take place during the milling process due to direct mechanical energy absorption [131]. During the solid-state reaction, high temperatures are used to solid starting materials. Heating the materials to extremely high temperatures creates a straightforward reaction pathway. Grinding or comminution occurs when the grinding medium collides and is often used to reduce particle size or crush, resulting change in the physical/ chemical properties. In addition, grinding can increase surface energy and defects, as well as cause chemical interactions and/or structural transformations [40].

Fig. 5(a) shows that solid-state reaction involves heating solid reagents at extremely high temperatures to obtain polycrystalline crystalline and/or single materials. Aspects affecting the solid-state reaction include chemical and morphological properties of the reagents like reactivity, free energy change, and surface area during the solid-state reaction. For example, Long et al. [132] developed a rapid solventfree grinding process for the flower-like BiOx synthesis $( \mathbf { X } = \mathbf { B } \mathbf { r }$ ,I and Cl) nanostructures. Tetsuya and Ye [133] synthesized the $\mathrm { R b B i _ { 2 } N b _ { 5 } O _ { 1 6 } }$ and $\mathrm { R b B i N b } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 7 }$ in different morphologies. Goti et al. [134] synthesized the monoclinic ${ \bf B i V O _ { 4 } }$ by choosing the solid state and melting reactions at high temperatures. Du et al. [135] developed the $\mathrm { B i _ { 1 2 } T i O _ { 2 0 } / B i _ { 4 } T i _ { 3 } O _ { 1 2 } }$ nanocomposite by choosing the same molar ratio of $\mathbf { B i } _ { 2 } \mathbf { O } _ { 3 }$ and $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ . Zhou et al. [136] developed a cubic $\mathrm { M g _ { 3 } S c _ { 2 } G e _ { 3 } O _ { 1 2 } { : } B i ^ { 3 + } }$ with an inverted garnet shape. Gan et al. [61] developed a solid-state process to prepare a hybrid bismuth titanate and so on. The advantages of the solid-state reaction process are its simplicity and large-scale fabrication [137].

# 4.2. Hydrothermal and solvothermal route

The hydrothermal route is a very suitable approach in the formation, development, and coupling of bismuth halide nanocomposites [112]. This approach has many benefits such as low-temperature synthesis, no complication in application, and morphological control [118]. In this method, the pH of the solution, the temperature, the duration, and the presence of a capping agent show a significant character in the hydrothermal synthesis [116]. The shape and crystalline nature of the material can be varied by adjusting the various reaction parameters, such as the amount of bismuth source used [55].

Moreover, variations in the Bi source concentration, the hydrothermal method offers energy savings and cost-effective advantages in synthesizing bismuth-containing nanoparticles by controlling particle size, degree of crystallinity, and shape [115]. The conditions of solvothermal synthesis are similar to those of hydrothermal synthesis except those organic solvents such as toluene, octadecene, and decalin are used in place of aqueous solutions. The shape and size of the nanocrystals can be adjusted by limiting the reaction temperature and concentration of the bismuth precursors, similar to hydrothermal synthesis seen in (Fig. 5(b)) [31,92,138,139]. Tables 4 and 5 show the detailed use of the hydrothermal synthesis method, which has been widely used for several bismuth-containing compounds.

# 4.3. Precipitation method

The precipitation approach is one of the most intriguing to synthesize bismuth nano-composite. It entails the synthesis of substances in a supersaturated state. Nucleation is a critical stage in the production of nanoparticles. The accumulation rate in the precipitation procedure influences the morphology, size, and physico-chemical characteristics of the nano-composite particles; however, it is less effective than the hydrothermal approach in influencing particle shape and size. Fig. 5(c) illustrates the necessary steps for precipitation synthesis. This approach is popular due to its quick synthesis, ease of use, regulated composition, overall uniformity, and low temperature [112]. This technique was used to synthesize the $\mathrm { { B i _ { 1 2 } T i O _ { 2 0 } / B i F e O _ { 3 } } }$ [140], while Benina and Valant, [141] successfully synthesized the ${ \mathrm { B i } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { T i } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { O } } _ { 7 }$ and $\mathrm { B i _ { 2 } T i _ { 2 } O _ { 7 } / B i _ { 4 } T i _ { 3 } O _ { 1 2 } }$ with a $9 0 \mathrm { n m }$ and $3 0 \mathrm { n m }$ typical crystallite size, respectively. Similarly, a two-step precipitation process was used to synthesize the Z-scheme $\mathrm { B i _ { 2 } M o O _ { 6 } / B i O B r }$ nanocomposites because in Z-scheme nanocomposites ions present in the solution contribute to the migration of charge carriers, which was linked to the participation of redox ion pairs, the electrons transfer and holes between the heterojunction composites [142], whereas Yu et al. [143] synthesized the $\mathrm { B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ with oxygen vacancies at room temperature using glyoxal as the reductant that has highest reduction ability to produce the oxygen vacancies.

# 4.4. Sol-gel approach

The solgel approach is a combination of different processes i.e., hydrolysis, gelation, polycondensation, crystallization, and aging as shown in Fig. 5(d). Various bismuth-based oxides and composites have been synthesized using this approach. For example, Li et al. [2016] synthesized $\mathrm { S i O _ { 2 } / B i _ { 4 } T i _ { 3 } O _ { 1 2 } }$ photocatalysts; Casados et al. [144] developed different $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 } – \mathrm { B i } _ { 2 } 0 _ { 3 }$ nanomaterials with different compositions; Fang et al [145] developed $\mathrm { B i _ { 2 } O _ { 2 . 7 } / B i _ { 2 } T i _ { 2 } O _ { 7 } }$ nanocomposites.

A variety of combinations were created by varying the bismuth and titanium precursors. They found that the $\mathbf { B i } / \mathrm { T i }$ ratio played a crucial role. Yilmaz et al [146] described the use of a simple solgel process to prepare the perovskite material ${ \mathrm { B i F e O } } _ { 3 }$ . The composite thus synthesized exhibited a p-type behavior and band potential of $\sim 1 . 1 5 \mathrm { V }$ Adding Ag ${ \sim } 2 \%$ Ag loading) boosted the photocurrent for $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ generation. The improvement in photoelectrochemical efficiency is due to light scattering by the integrated Ag nanoparticles, which increases the charge transfer rate at the ${ \bf A g / B i F e O _ { 3 } }$ interface, leading to a reduction in overpotential losses as shown in Table 3.

Nowadays, the combined synthesis approach is very challenging. For example, the authors developed a combined dip-coating and solgel technique for the synthesis of the bismuth titanate. Gu et al [147] developed a sol-gel and hydrothermal approach to prepare the vanadium-doped $\mathrm { { B i } _ { 4 } T i _ { 3 } O _ { 1 2 } }$ . The main advantages of the solgel process are the ease of handling, the use of low temperatures, and the production of a regular morphology. The advantages and disadvantages of various fabrication techniques are defined in Table 3.

# 5. Remediation of antibiotics

# 5.1. Bond breakage of antibiotics with typical bismuth-based photocatalysts

The destruction of antibiotics starts with the heterolytic and homolytic cleavage of multiple and single bonds of C-N, C-S, C-O and C-C breaking. Fig. 6 proposed the information about the bond breaking and destruction of the different antibiotics including cephalosporins, penicillin, quinolones, sulfonamides, tetracyclines, etc., during the photolysis with BPCs.

Generally, antibiotic by-products are formed by a series of exchange, hydration and oxidation processes with the attack of ROS. A ring structure of antibiotics can be again oxidized and ruptured. The active species formed via the BPCs reactions are very efficient and effective in destroying the majority of antibiotics.

# 5.Identification of the challenges and future research

(1) BPCs have been investigated for several years for various photocatalytic degradation applications. The ability to produce these photocatalysts in a controlled manner offers excellent opportunities. As concern about antibiotics growth, it is an essential need of wastewater treatment plants to reduce the antibiotics from the effluents. Therefore, it is critical to research a new approach for the simultaneous degradation of antibiotics with BPCs. For this purpose, photocatalysis could be combined with other techniques such as biodegradation, Fenton process and electrocatalysis. In addition, a heavy metal such as Ag or ZnO can be lost from bismuth-based composites by photocorrosion and released in the solution, which poses a greater risk to human health than antibiotics. Therefore, it is important to focus on their toxicity.

(2) Industrial wastewater can contain various organic acids and bases, and the solution pH may be either acidic or basic due to the existence of organic pollutants. This can consequently affect the kinetic rate of organic degradation. Therefore, it is important to focus on the development of new photocatalysts that can efficiently degrade organic contaminants over a broad pH range. To achieve this, it is important to understand the mechanisms of photochemical degradation of different antibiotics at different pH values and to develop a comprehensive understanding of how these processes work. It is important to ensure that the intermediates or by-products formed during the photocatalysisbased decomposition process are not more harmful than the original contaminant.

(3) The stability of photocatalysts can be problematic, especially when used in aqueous environments. Therefore, the photocatalyst composites must be easily set apart from the reaction mixture to facilitate the recycling and reuse of the photocatalysts.

(4) Overcoming these challenges is important for improving the performance of bismuth-based composites, and considerable efforts have been made to achieve this goal. The use of BPCs has shown great potential for the photodegradation of antibiotics. However, pure bismuth-based photocatalysts have limited light absorption and redox capability. To address these issues, researchers have investigated the modification of bismuth-based compounds through changes in morphology/structure, heterojunction construction, and element doping. The high photocatalytic efficiency is due to the high rate of photogenerated charge carrier separation. Mostly, lab-made solutions with antibiotic concentrations below $\scriptstyle { \mathrm { m g / L } }$ were used to study the photodegradation by BPCs. In fact, the concentration of antibiotics in effluents of contaminated water treatment plants and surface water is typically between $\scriptstyle { \mathrm { m g / L } }$ and ng/L. Therefore, the use of BPCs for actual antibiotic degradation applications of antibiotic breakdown, especially for situations involving trace antibiotics.

(5) Although promising results have been obtained in the laboratory, there are still significant challenges to overcome in the practical application of BPCs. These involve the multiple sources of contaminants in wastewater, the complex properties of antibiotics, and the difficulties in removing intermediates and antibioticresistance genes.

# 7. Conclusions

Antibiotic contamination in water is highly dangerous. Combining PC of visible light-driven PS activated AOP is a highly efficient approach for antibiotic degradation. In comparison of homogeneous catalysis, the heterogeneous catalysts are studied to be more favorable due to their excellent reusability, recyclability and safety. Among them, BPCs are likely to be applied in real-world sewage treatment projects. In antibiotics degradation, all types of BPCs have been extensively used due to their safety, high stability and cost-effective synthesis process. In comparison of other systems, PC activated PS illustrates benefits in antibiotics degradation. It solves the challenges of small efficiency of single and mixed PC, producing an extensive variety of ROS. The crystal structure and electronic configuration makes the BPCs an ideal material for its use in photocatalysis application of wastewater treatment. The combination of bismuth with other heterojunction materials shows as a promise candidate in the modification of its properties. However, previous literature shows most of BPCs studies have attention on the surface modification of materials and their application in persistent pollutants remediation.

There are many practical limitations on using the heterogeneous BPC to activate with PS. Most of the research are only performed on the laboratory scale and batch mode experiments Practically, the cost of PS activated PC system is conclusively the biggest challenge affecting its application. Additionally, the use of visible light or sunlight will also be influenced via various environmental issues. In the future, a depth investigation of the theoretical and experimental research for antibiotics mineralization via the PS activated BPCs system, a wide variety of application setups can be appreciated.
