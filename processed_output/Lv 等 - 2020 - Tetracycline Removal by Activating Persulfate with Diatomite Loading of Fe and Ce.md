# Article Tetracycline Removal by Activating Persulfate with Diatomite Loading of Fe and Ce

School of Traditional Chinese Materia Medica, Shenyang Pharmaceutical University, Shenyang 110016, China; lcnmi@syphu.edu.cn 2 School of Pharmaceutical Engineering, Shenyang Pharmaceutical University, Shenyang 110016, China; shijindou1996@foxmail.com (J.S.); tangqiuju96@foxmail.com (Q.T.) \* Correspondence: huqi@syphu.edu.cn; Tel.: +86-24-43520205

Received: 28 October 2020; Accepted: 24 November 2020; Published: 25 November 2020

Abstract: Persulfate (PS)-based oxidation technology is efficient in removing refractory organics from water. A novel diatomite (DIA) support Fe and Ce composite (Fe-Ce/DIA) was prepared for activating persulfate to degrade tetracycline in water. The Fe and Ce were uniformly loaded on DIA, and the total pore size of Fe-Ce/DIA was $6 . 9 9 \times 1 0 ^ { - 2 } \mathrm { c m } ^ { 3 } / \mathrm { g } ,$ and the average pore size was $1 2 . 0 6 \mathrm { n m }$ . Fe-Ce/DIA presented a good catalytic activity and $8 0 \%$ tetracycline was removed under the persulfate system. The Fe-Ce/DIA also had photocatalytic activity, and the corresponding tetracycline removal efficiency was $8 6 \%$ under UV irradiation. Fe-Ce/DIA exhibited less iron dissolution rate compared with Fe-DIA. The tetracycline degradation rate was enhanced when the temperature increased. The optimal tetracycline removal efficiency was obtained when the conditions were of persulfate $1 0 \mathrm { m M }$ , Fe-Ce/DIA dosage $0 . 0 2 { \mathrm { g / L } } ,$ and tetracycline concentration $5 0 \mathrm { m g / L }$ . In addition, Fe-Ce/DIA showed a wide $\mathrm { p H }$ application and good reusability and stability.

Keywords: tetracycline; persulfate; advance oxidation; diatomite; wastewater

# 1. Introduction

Nowadays, the global pharmaceutical industry develops rapidly, and hundreds of antibiotics have been discovered and applied in human and animal medicine. China consumed $1 . 6 2 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ tonnes of antibiotics in 2013, and approximately $5 2 \%$ of the antibiotics were used in livestock breeding and aquaculture [1], and the total production increased to $1 . 9 6 \times 1 0 ^ { 5 }$ tonnes in 2019. Whenever antibiotics are used, wastes with residual antibiotics are inevitable and eventually discharged into the water environment. The concentration of antibiotics in surface water was 10, 15, 50, and $4 5 0 ~ \mu \mathrm { g / L }$ in Europe, the United States, Africa, and the Asia Pacific [2], which may endanger the quality of the aquatic ecological environment [3,4]. Tetracycline (Figure S1), as a representative antibiotic, is widely used in animal production worldwide [5]. China is the biggest supplier of the Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient (API) of tetracycline. The abuse of tetracycline had caused the universal presence of its residuals and metabolites in environments, in particular, these antibiotics are detected by the Wastewater Treatment Plants (WWTPs) [6–9]. Antibiotics, even in little concentrations, could become a serious threat to ecological security. Thus, effective antibiotic removal approaches should be explored.

Numerous approaches have been employed to remove antibiotics from wastewaters, including biodegradation [10], coagulation/flocculation [11], adsorption [12,13], membrane separation [14], and advanced oxidation processes (AOP) [15–18]. However, biodegradation methods have difficulty in obtaining suitable microorganisms from those that survive in antibiotics [19,20]; adsorption and membrane methods only accumulate and transfer antibiotics from one to another [21–24]. Compared with the above, advanced oxidation processes (AOP) can degrade antibiotics by the production of reactive free radicals, such as hydroxyl radical $\mathrm { ( H O ^ { \bullet } ) }$ or sulfate radical $( \mathrm { S O _ { 4 } } ^ { \bullet - } )$ [25,26]. Fenton is a common AOP process, and $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ is catalyzed by $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + }$ to produce $_ \mathrm { H O ^ { \bullet } }$ for degrading organic pollutants into small molecules such as $\mathrm { C O } _ { 2 } , \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }$ and inorganic salts in the process. However, Fenton is limited by the narrow $\mathrm { p H }$ -scale [27,28]. As a Fenton-like reagent, persulfate can be used as the oxidizing agent to produce $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } ^ { \bullet - } }$ to degrade organics in water [29]. $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } ^ { \bullet - } }$ has get increasing attention because of its wider $\mathrm { p H }$ range [30], longer half-time, and stronger oxidation selectivity in wastewater treatment [31]. The cost for chemicals and reactors was approximately $0 . 1 5 \mathrm { - } 0 . 7 5 \ : \mathfrak { H } /$ tonne. $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } ^ { \bullet - } }$ can be formed by persulfate and several activators, such as UV-light, heat, base, and electrode transition metal ions [32,33].

Fe is a cheap and abundant crust mineral that is commonly used as a catalyst [34]. The advantages of Fe-based catalysts are as follows: Rich in the earth, easy to produce, and nearly non-toxic [35]. Xu et al. [36] used ferrous ion activating PS to degrade dye orange $G ,$ and the decolorization rate of orange G reached $9 9 \%$ . However, the $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ , formed by the oxidation of $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + }$ , was easily precipitated into iron mud. The serious iron ion dissolution condition restricts the large-scale application of iron-based catalysts. Therefore, some studies attempted to improve the activity and stability of the catalysts, such as, (1) introduce other metal to improve the catalytic activity; (2) provide a supporter for the catalyst to change the physical structure of the catalyst. Among many metal elements, rare earth is widely used in heterogeneous catalysis for its special redox properties. Cerium (Ce) is one of the most abundant rare earth elements. As a dispersant and stabilizer, it was used to strengthen adsorption and catalytic oxidation effects. The cycle from $\mathrm { C e } ^ { 3 + }$ to $\mathrm { C e ^ { 4 + } }$ makes $\mathrm { C e O } _ { 2 }$ have good electron transfer characteristics and catalytic oxidation activity. The doping of $\mathrm { C e O } _ { 2 }$ can improve the storage and activity of lattice oxygen. Perez-Alonso and coworkers reported that the cerium link with iron could increase the performance of catalysts and reduce the dissolution rate of iron [37].

Metal catalysts tend to agglomerate [38,39], and a carrier can help prevent agglomeration [40]. In terms of Fe-based catalysts carrier selection, the current research focuses on new functional materials, such as metal-organic frameworks, carbon nanotubes, graphene oxide, etc., but the complex preparation process and the high price of these materials cause a cost inefficiency. Diatomite (DIA) is porous and cheap, and has strong adsorption capacity [41] and stable chemical properties [42,43]. Some studies used DIA as a carrier: V-doped $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 } / \mathrm { D I A }$ showed excellent performance as a photocatalyst for degrading rhodamine [44]; $\mathrm { C u } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } { \cdot } \mathrm { Z n O } / \mathrm { D I A }$ showed good performance in degrading red water [45]; Ce-doped $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 } / \mathrm { D I A }$ granular composite could enhance photocatalytic activity [46]. As is seen from the above, DIA is a potential Fe-based catalysts carrier.

To combine the advantages of $\mathrm { F e / C e }$ and DIA, a series of DIA matrix composites (Fe-DIA, Ce-DIA, Fe-Ce/DIA) were fabricated to activate persulfate to degrade tetracycline in water. The composites were characterized to show the combination of Fe and Ce. The adsorptive and catalytic ability of DIA containing Fe and Ce was compared through tetracycline removal. The iron dissolution and reusability of the Fe-Ce/DIA were measured. Finally, the parameters for tetracycline removal were given through investigating the Fe-Ce/DIA dosage, tetracycline initial concentration, $\mathrm { p H } ,$ and temperature in the reaction.

# 2.2. The Adsorptive and Catalytic Properties

The tetracycline adsorption by DIA, Fe-Ce/DIA, Fe-DIA, and Ce-DIA was compared under the same conditions (Figure 5). The tetracycline removal by DIA adsorption was about $2 0 \%$ under the dark. The combination of Fe, Ce and DIA could increase the adsorption capacity, and the best efficiency was $3 0 \%$ for Fe-Ce/DIA. The doping of Fe and Ce provided a rough surface, which probably improved the adsorption capacity of Fe-Ce/DIA.

The tetracycline degradation by DIA, Fe-Ce/DIA, Fe-DIA, and Ce-DIA was also investigated through adding persulfate. The load of Fe or Ce could improve the catalytic activity, the removal efficiency by Ce-DIA was about $7 0 \%$ , while that of Fe-DIA and $\mathrm { F e - C e / D I A }$ were $8 0 \%$ (Figure 5b). The result indicated that Fe-Ce/DIA and Fe-DIA effectively activated persulfate to degrade tetracycline. The Fe-Ce/DIA and Fe-DIA had the same catalytic efficiency in the same dosage, but Fe content in Fe-Ce/DIA was less, which means less iron dissolution.

# 2.3. Photocatalytic Property

The photocatalytic activity of Fe-Ce/DIA was investigated under visible and ultraviolet light. The tetracycline removal by Fe-DIA was slightly better than Fe-Ce/DIA, as Fe-DIA contained more Fe in the same dosage (Figure 6a). The final removal rates were $8 6 \%$ $\mathrm { F e - C e / D I A ) }$ and $9 1 \%$ (Fe-DIA). Besides, $\mathrm { C e O } _ { 2 }$ had a strong absorption in the ultraviolet region, and some photogenerated electron pair holes were produced and reacted with water to produce hydroxyl radicals [48,49]. Thus, Fe-Ce/DIA showed better photocatalytic properties than Fe-DIA during the first $2 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ (Figure $^ \mathrm { 6 b }$ ). $\mathrm { C e O } _ { 2 }$ exhibited poor photocatalytic activity under visible light, but showed good activity under ultraviolet light (Figure 6). $\mathrm { C e O } _ { 2 }$ had a wide band gap of $3 . 2 \mathrm { e V }$ and only absorbed light with a wavelength less than $4 4 4 \mathrm { n m }$ , which led to its little absorption under visible light and thus poor catalytic activity [48].

2.5. Parameters on Tetracycline Removal by Fe-Ce/DIA

The effect of Fe-Ce/DIA dosage on the tetracycline removal was investigated at five different dosages. $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + }$ was a necessary catalyst for sodium persulfate to generate free radicals. At the same reaction time, higher removal efficiencies were obtained with the dosage from 0 to $0 . 0 1 \mathrm { g / L } ,$ the reaction rate increased from $2 . 4 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \operatorname* { m i n } ^ { - 1 }$ to $8 . 6 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \operatorname* { m i n } ^ { - 1 }$ . When the dosage of $\mathrm { F e - C e / D I A }$ was $0 . 0 2 { \mathrm { g / L } } ,$ the reaction rate decreased to $6 . 1 \times 1 0 ^ { - 3 } \operatorname* { m i n } ^ { - 1 }$ (Figure 9, Table 1). The similar result had been obtained in organic pollutants degradation by activated persulfate [50]. The decline can be explained as follows: Due to the aggregation of excessive catalyst, reactants cannot enter the active center; Fe is consumed by $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } ^ { - }$ if it exceeds a certain amount as Equation (1) [50]. The optimum dosage of Fe-Ce/DIA was $0 . 0 1 \mathrm { g / L }$ in this study. Therefore, considering the cost and efficiency of tetracycline removal, a dosage of $0 . 0 1 \mathrm { g / L }$ Fe-Ce/DIA was selected for the successive tetracycline removal study.

# 2.6. Degradation Mechanism

Fe-Ce/DIA showed good performance on tetracycline removal, and a potential removal mechanism was proposed as shown in Figure 10. Firstly, Tetracycline and persulfate were absorbed on the surface of $\mathrm { F e - C e / D I A }$ . Tetracycline antibiotics were positively charged in acidic solutions and anionic under alkaline conditions [52]. DIA was negatively charged at most $\mathrm { p H }$ conditions. In acid condition, tetracycline adsorption by diatomite depended on positive and negative charge; in alkaline condition, tetracycline was adsorbed by cation on diatomite. $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + }$ could activate persulfate to produce $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } ^ { \bullet - } }$ . $\mathrm { C e } ^ { 3 + }$ can also activate persulfate to produce $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } ^ { - }$ [28]. The persulfate could be activated by ultraviolet light [8,53]: $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } ^ { \bullet - } }$ reacted with $_ { \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } }$ to form HO• radicals, and $\mathrm { C e } ^ { 3 + }$ activated $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ to produce $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } { } ^ { \bullet - }$ . Finally, tetracycline was degraded into $_ { \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } }$ and $\mathrm { C O } _ { 2 }$ by all active radicals $\mathbf { ( H O ^ { \bullet } }$ , $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } ^ { \bullet - } }$ and $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } { } ^ { \bullet - }$ ). Eight main products were found in the degradation process, and the degradation products included $\mathrm { m } / z$ 475, 459, 443, 430, 441, 414, 386, and 342. The detail results were shown in Table S1 and Figure S2.

# 3.2. Synthesis of Composites

DIA $( 3 \mathrm { g } )$ was calcined at $3 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 2 \mathrm { h } , }$ , mixed with $\mathrm { N a C l }$ solution, stirred in a water bath at $6 5 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 2 \mathrm { h } , }$ washed until no $\mathrm { C l ^ { - } }$ detection, and dried at $1 1 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ . The solid-liquid ratio of DIA and NaCl $\mathrm { ( 6 0 \ m L 1 . 0 \ m o l / L ) }$ ) was 1:20.

$\mathrm { C e ( N O _ { 3 } ) _ { 2 } } { \cdot } 6 \mathrm { H _ { 2 } O } \left( 0 . 0 4 3 \mathrm { g } \right)$ was dissolved in water $( 5 0 \mathrm { m L } )$ and mixed with $\mathrm { F e S O } _ { 4 } \left( 1 . 0 4 3 \ : \mathrm { g } \right)$ . The total concentration of $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + }$ and $\mathrm { C e } ^ { 2 + }$ was $0 . 1 \mathrm { m o l / L }$ . The solution was mixed with dried DIA, added with citric acid $( 1 . 0 5 1 \mathrm { g } )$ ) and stirred in a water bath $6 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 2 \mathrm { h } , }$ . $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 1 \mathrm { h } }$ ). Ammonia water was added during the reaction to maintain the precursor $\mathrm { p H } = 8 . 0$ . The material was dried at $1 1 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ calcined at $4 0 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 2 \mathrm { h } , }$ and ground to obtain Fe-Ce/DIA. The Fe-DIA and $\mathrm { C e }$ -DIA were also synthesized by the same method as Fe-Ce/DIA.

# 3.3. Characterization of Composites

X-ray diffractometer (XRD, Bruker D8 Advance, Karlsruhe, Germany) was used to test the crystal properties. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JEM1002EX) was used to analyze microscopic morphology and element distribution. The pore structure and specific surface area characteristics of the composites were tested using a physical adsorption instrument (BET, ASAP 2460, Micrometrics, Norcross, GA, USA).

The adsorption capacity of Fe-Ce/DIA, Fe-DIA, Ce-DIA, and DIA to tetracycline was investigated before a degradation study. Each sample $( 0 . 0 2 0 \ : \mathrm { g } )$ was dissolved in tetracycline $2 0 0 ~ \mathrm { m L }$ , $5 0 \mathrm { m g / L } ,$ ) separately, then reacted in dark for $2 . 5 \mathrm { h }$ $3 7 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ , $1 5 0 \mathrm { r p m }$ ). The sample $3 { \mathrm { m L } } ,$ reacted for 0, 30, 60, 90, 120, and $1 8 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ ) was filtered using a $0 . 2 2 \mu \mathrm { m }$ filter, extracted in EP tube for $2 \mathrm { m L }$ , mixed with $\mathrm { C H } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O H }$ $( 3 \mathrm { m L } )$ , and measured for absorbance at $3 5 9 \mathrm { n m }$ . The basic calculations in adsorption can be found in previous study [32,54].

# 3.5. Degradation Study

The tetracycline degradation by $\mathrm { F e - C e / D I A , }$ , Fe-DIA, Ce-DIA, and DIA was investigated. Each sample $_ { ( 0 . 0 2 g ) }$ was dissolved in tetracycline $2 0 0 \mathrm { m L }$ , $5 0 \mathrm { m g / L }$ ), added with sodium persulfate $( 0 . 1 3 4 \ \mathrm { g } )$ to react in dark for $^ { 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ $( 3 7 \ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } ,$ $1 5 0 ~ \mathrm { r p m }$ ). The samples ${ \mathrm { 3 ~ m L , } }$ reacted for 0, 30, 60, 90, and $1 2 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ ) were processed and measured as described above.

The effect of visible light and ultraviolet on tetracycline degradation were investigated. Each sample $( 0 . 0 2 ~ \mathrm { g } )$ , $\mathrm { F e - C e / D I A } ,$ , Fe-DIA, Ce-DIA, and DIA, was dissolved in tetracycline $2 0 0 ~ \mathrm { m L } ,$ , $5 0 ~ \mathrm { m g / L } )$ separately, and then sodium persulfate $\left( 0 . 4 2 6 \mathrm { g } \right)$ was added to the system. The reaction was under Xe lamp (25 A, 500 W) and $\mathrm { H g }$ lamp (5.2 A, 350 W) with magnetic stirring at $6 5 0 \mathrm { r p m }$ .

A UPLC system coupled with a Bruker’s Campact high-resolution time-of-flight (TOF) mass spectrometer was applied to the UPLC-MS/MS analysis of samples to identify the structure of the degradation products of tetracycline.

Chromatographic separation was achieved using an C18 column $( 2 . 1 \ \mathrm { m m } \times 1 0 0 \ \mathrm { m m } , 1 . 7 \ \mu \mathrm { m } )$ ). The column temperature was maintained at $3 0 ^ { \circ } C ,$ and the mobile phase, at a flow rate of $0 . 2 \mathrm { m L } / \mathrm { m i n } ,$ consisted of solvent A ( $0 . 1 \%$ acetic acid in water) and mobile phase B (methanol). The gradient program of the mobile phase was as follows: $0 { - } 2 0 ~ \mathrm { m i n }$ , $1 5 \mathrm { - } 5 5 \%$ B. Mass spectrometry conditions: Electrospray interface (ESI), MS analysis in negative ion mode, mass-nucleus ratio scan range 100–800, collision energy is $1 2 \mathrm { e V } .$

# 3.6. Batch Study of Tetracycline Removal

The effect of the various parameters, including the dosage of composites (Fe-Ce/DIA, Fe-DIA, Ce-DIA, and DIA) and sodium persulfate, initial $\mathrm { p H }$ and tetracycline concentrations, were investigated in details. The reusability of the composites (Fe-Ce/DIA and Fe-DIA) were performed by placing

$0 . 0 0 2 { \mathrm g }$ Fe-Ce/DIA or Fe-DIA in $2 0 0 \mathrm { m L }$ of $5 0 \mathrm { m g / L }$ tetracycline solution and adding sodium persulfate of $0 . 4 7 6 \mathrm { g } ,$ respectively.

# 4. Conclusions

A series of composites (Fe-Ce/DIA, Fe-DIA, Ce-DIA) were synthesized to remove tetracycline from water. The average pore size of Fe-Ce/DIA was larger than DIA, which was beneficial to tetracycline adsorption. SEM and TEM images showed Fe and Ce were uniformly dispersed in DIA. The tetracycline removal efficiency was $8 0 \%$ at the catalytic oxidation system provided by Fe-Ce/DIA and persulfate. Ultraviolet light could promote the removal efficiency, and $8 6 \%$ tetracycline was removed by Fe-Ce/DIA activating persulfate under ultraviolet light. Eight main products were found in the tetracycline degradation process. A recommended parameter was of $\mathrm { F e - C e / D I A }$ dosage $0 . 0 2 { \mathrm { g } } ,$ persulfate $1 0 \mathrm { m M } ,$ and initial tetracycline solution $5 0 \mathrm { m g / L }$ . The iron dissolution of Fe-Ce/DIA was less than Fe-DIA in activating persulfate. Fe-Ce/DIA showed wide $\mathrm { p H }$ application and excellent reusability in tetracycline removal. The metal doped diatomite is a promising catalyst in antibiotics pollution control.
