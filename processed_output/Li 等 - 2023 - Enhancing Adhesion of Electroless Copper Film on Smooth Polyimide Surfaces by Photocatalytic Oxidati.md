# Enhancing Adhesion of Electroless Copper Film on Smooth Polyimide Surfaces by Photocatalytic Oxidation

Bingbing Li,1 Kamran Dastafkan,2 Yu Shen,1 Lu Wang,1 Yi Ma,1 Zenglin Wang, $\mathbf { 1 } , \mathbf { z } _ { \textcircled { 1 } }$ and Chuan Zhao 2,z

1 Key Laboratory of Applied Surface and Colloid Chemistry, Ministry of Education, and School of Chemistry and Chemical   
Engineering, Shaanxi Normal University, Xi’an 710062, People’s Republic of China   
2 School of Chemistry, The University of New South Wales, Sydney, NSW 2052, Australia

The application of advanced high-frequency (5 G) requires a low-profile $\mathrm { { C u } }$ foil to reduce the high-speed signal loss due to skin effect. However, the adhesion of electroless copper film on the smooth surface of polyimides (PI) remains a challenge. Here, a twostep composite modification method, consisting of $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ photocatalysis and $\mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ photooxidation treatment, is successfully applied to induce a high adhesion strength of electroless copper film on the smooth PI surface. To meet the requirements of the flexible electronics, the average surface roughness of the PI film is increased from 4.2 to $1 1 . 5 \mathrm { n m }$ through optimizing the modification. Whereas the surface contact angle decreases from the original $7 6 . 9 ^ { \circ }$ to $1 8 . 7 ^ { \circ }$ and the adhesion strength between the electroless copper film and PI film is increased from 0 to $0 . 9 0 \mathrm { K N m } ^ { = 1 }$ . Spectroscopic investigations reveal that the amide bonds on the surface PI get oxidized by radicals, while an abundant $- \mathrm { C O O H }$ and $\mathrm { - N H } _ { 2 }$ groups form. The high oxidation potential, strong stability of the sulfate anion radicals, as along with their increased concentration in the treatment solution are contributed to the extraordinary modification performance. Therefore, the $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ photocatalysis and $\mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ photooxidation composite treatment is an effective and environmentally friendly method for surface modification of PI films.

With the continuous development of thin-film materials in manufacturing processes, more attention is dedicated to the flexible electronics in developing future circuits due to their good flexibility, ductility, and structural characteristics.1 Polyimides (PI) are flexible materials and widely used in flexible batteries, organic light-emitting diode materials, and flexible printed circuit boards (FPCB), resulting from their excellent thermal stability, chemical stability, low dielectric constant, good electrical insulation, safety, and non-toxicity.2 However, because of the low surface energy and inertness, the adhesion strength between electroless copper and PI substrate is poor, hindering its application in flexible electronics.3

Numerous studies have been developed over the past several years, focusing on improving the adhesion of PI films. These efforts are mainly classified into two areas. Firstly, Cr, Al, Ti, W, or alloys are sputtered on PI films as a binding layer to enhance the adhesion strength by $2 { \sim } 3$ times.4,5 However, it is difficult to withstand further processing and results in cracking in the metal layer. Secondly, the surface modification includes alkaline hydrolysis,6,7 plasma treatment,8–10 grafting treatment,11–13 and ultraviolet (UV)/ozone treatment.14,15 Alkaline treatment enhances the surface roughness and adhesion strength between PI film and the metallization film, but also weakens the physical strength of the film. Plasma treatment by the gases, such as argon,8 helium,9 and oxygen,10 forms polar groups such as $\mathrm { C - O H }$ and COOH on the PI surface and boosts the surface hydrophilicity, however the modification performance is still insufficient. The surface grafting of functional groups, such as carboxylic acid,11 poly-ethyleneimine,12 and organosilane,1 3 can also improve the surface hydrophilicity and enhance the adhesion strength between PI film and electroless copper. However, the treatment process is complex and the cost is high. For the advanced high-frequency fifth-generation technology $( 5 \mathrm { G } )$ applications, PI film with smooth surface is required to reduce high-frequency signal loss due to the skin effect as a low-K substrate. So far, it has been an urgent need to obtain a high adhesion of the electroless copper film on the smooth PI surface.

The surface modification by $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ photocatalysis has received more attention in the recent years because of its high catalytic activity, high chemical stability, low cost, and environment-friendly system.16,17 Wang et al. modified ABS, PET, and PI films by photocatalytic $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ treatment and found that the surface hydrophilicity of the films could be improved significantly. Owing to the high redox potential, long life, and wide pH adaptability, the sulfate anion radicals produced by $\mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ photooxidation have also received extensive attention in the degradation of organic pollutants and the oxidation modification of plastics.18–20 Barnford et al.,18 Kubota et al.19 and Yang et al.20 investigated the surface modification of polyethene, polyamide, polypropylene, polystyrene, polyacrylonitrile, and polyurethane by persulfate ultrasound or photooxidation treatment. Though hydroxyl and carboxylic acid groups were formed on the surface after the photocatalysis or photooxidation modification, the adhesion strength between the electroless copper and the PI film was not enough for the FPCB. Further, it is noticed that the surface modification via $\mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ photooxidation or $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ photocatalysis is the radical oxidation occurred on the surface of the film, and the surface roughness of the modified film does not change much with the treatment process, which is conducive to meeting the requirement of FPCB for the smooth surface. Herein, to obtain a high surface hydrophily on the smooth PI film, a composite treatment method, $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ photocatalysis and $\mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ photo-oxidation, is used to explore the synergetic surface modification and an extraordinary result was obtained.

# Experimental

Pretreatment of the PI films.—PI films with a density of $1 . 4 2 \ \mathrm { g } { \cdot } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 3 }$ were procured from DuPont Chemical $\mathbf { \boldsymbol { C } _ { 0 } }$ ., Ltd., as Kapton HN in rolls with $4 0 \mathrm { m m }$ wide and $5 0 \mu \mathrm { m }$ thickness, were then cut into $4 0 \mathrm { m m } \times 2 5 \mathrm { m m }$ samples for all experiments. Prior to the surface modification, PI films were cleaned with acetone in an ultrasonic water bath for $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ at room temperature, then treated in a swelling solution (N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF), water, dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) at $4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 5 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ .

Surface modification of the PI films.— $\cdot \mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ powder (JR05) (purchased from Xuancheng Jingrui New Materials $\mathrm { C o }$ ., Ltd.) is pure anatase phase, and $0 . 5 \mathrm { g } { \cdot } \mathrm { l } ^ { - \Gamma } \mathrm { T i } \check { \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } }$ suspension and $\mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ solutions with different concentrations and $\mathsf { p H }$ were prepared. The PI films were then immersed into the solution. The UV irradiation experiment was carried out under a high-pressure Mercury lamp with maximum radiation at the wavelength of $3 6 5 \mathrm { n m }$ . The distance between the PI film and the Mercury lamp was ${ 8 \mathrm { c m } }$ . The effects of the treatment process, $\mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ content, $\mathsf { p H }$ , and treatment time on the modification performances were optimized. Afterwards, the PI films were rinsed with distilled water three times.

Electroless copper plating on PI films.—Before the activation process, the PI films were treated with a prepreg solution $( 2 5 0 \mathrm { g } 1 ^ { - 1 }$ OPC-SALT, Hangzhou Aoye) at room temperature for $2 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ . Then the activation process was proceeded in a solution $( 5 0 \mathrm { m l } \cdot \mathrm { l } ^ { - 1 }$ Catalyst OPC-80, $1 7 0 \mathrm { g } { \cdot } 1 ^ { - 1 }$ OPC-Salt, $3 0 \ m \mathrm { l } \mathrm { l } ^ { - 1 }$ , $3 5 \%$ HCl) at room temperature 6 min. Afterwards, the surface sensitization of the films was conducted by immersing the samples in a solution $( 1 0 0 \mathrm { m l } { \cdot } \mathrm { l } ^ { - 1 }$ accelerator OPC-35) at room temperature for $1 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ and washing the films with distilled water after each step. The chemical composition of the electroless copper plating solution was the same as reported before.21 The pH of the solution was adjusted to 12.5 using NaOH and the bath temperature was $7 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ . After electroless copper plating for $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ , the electroplating of copper was carried out at $\bar { 2 } 5 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ with a current density of $0 . 0 3 \mathrm { \ A } { \cdot } \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 2 }$ for $^ { 2 \mathrm { h } }$ , resulting in a copper thickness of $2 0 \mu \mathrm m$ .

Adhesion strength measurement.—The annealing of the prepared samples was carried out in an oven at $1 1 0 ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $1 2 0 \mathrm { { \bar { m i n } } }$ . Then, the copper-coated PI films were cut into $1 0 \times 4 0 \mathrm { m m }$ strips and the adhesion strength was measured by a $9 0 ^ { \circ }$ peel test at a peel rate of $2 5 \mathrm { { m m } \cdot \mathrm { { m i n } ^ { - 1 } } }$ . The experiment was repeated four times and the adhesion strength was reported as the average value of these four times.

Characterization of the PI films.—Philips-FEI quantum-200 electron microscope (SEM) was used to observe the surface morphology of the PI film. The surface roughness of the PI film was measured by dimension ICON scanning probe microscope (AFM-Bruck) and was reported as average roughness $\mathrm { ( R _ { a } ) }$ and root mean square roughness $( \mathbb { R } _ { q } )$ . The surface contact angle (SCA) of the PI film was measured by a video contact angle measuring instrument and the volume of the water drop was $2 \mu \mathrm { L }$ . The reflection IR spectra were measured by Brucher-EQUINX55 FTIR spectroscopy.

# Results and Discussion

Selection of surface modification method for PI film.—To obtain a favourable modification performance, $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ photocatalysis and $\mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ photooxidation are first used to modify the PI surface. The change of SCA with the treatment time is shown in Fig. 1a. Both SCAs of PI films first decreases with the treatment time and reaches the minimum value $( 3 7 . 3 ^ { \circ }$ for photocatalysis and $3 1 . 0 ^ { \circ }$ for photooxidation) when the treatment time is set to $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ . After that, SCA increases with the treatment time. It is noted that SCA of the PI film modified by photooxidation is lower than the one modified by photocatalysis, which is attributed to the high oxidation potential and stability of sulfate anion radical $\mathrm { ( S O _ { 4 } ) ^ { \bullet - } }$ ) generated by the UV irradiation persulfate solution. The oxidation potential and the steady time of $\mathrm { S O _ { 4 } ^ { \bullet \bullet } }$ are about $2 . 5 { \sim } 3 . 1 \mathrm { ~ V ~ }$ and $3 \sim 4 ^ { ^ { \bullet } } \times 1 0 ^ { - 5 } \mathrm { s }$ , respectively, much higher than the oxidation potential $( 1 . 9 \small { \sim } 2 . 7 \mathrm { V } )$ ) and the steady time $( 2 \mathrm { ~ \times ~ } 1 0 ^ { - 8 } \mathrm { s }$ ) of $\mathrm { O H } ^ { \bullet }$ generated by $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ photocatalysis.22–24 However, the modification performance neither by $\mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ photooxidation nor by $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ photo-catalysis is sufficient to meet the requirement of FPCB. Therefore, three treatment processes are designed to explore the appropriate modification approach. (i) $0 . 5 \ : \mathrm { g } { . } 1 ^ { - 1 } \ : \mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ and $0 . 1 \ \mathrm { g } . 1 ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { \hat { N } } \mathrm { a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { \hat { S } } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ mixed system by UV irradiation treatment; (ⅱ) $0 . 1 \ \mathrm { \bar { g } cdot l ^ { - 1 } \ N a _ { 2 } S _ { 2 } O _ { 8 } }$ photo-oxidation for $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ , then $0 . 5 \mathrm { g } { \cdot } 1 ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { \stackrel { . } { T i O } } _ { 2 }$ photocatalysis treatment; (ⅲ) $0 . 5 { \mathrm { g } } { \cdot } 1 ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ photocatalysis for $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ , then $0 . 1 \ \mathrm { g } . 1 ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ photooxidation treatment (note as composite system).

The changes of SCA for PI films with the treatment time and the treatment approach are shown in Fig. 1b. For the mixed system, although SCA decreases with the treatment time, it is higher than the one treated only with $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ photocatalysis or $\mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ photooxidation. This is due to the second-order kinetic rate constant between the hydroxyl radical and sulfate anion radical $( 1 . 0 ~ \times$ $1 0 ^ { 1 0 } \mathrm { M } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { S } ^ { - 1 } )$ being much higher than that of two hydroxyl radicals $( 5 . 5 \ \times \ 1 0 ^ { 9 } \mathrm { { \dot { M } ^ { - 1 } S ^ { - 1 } } ) }$ or for two sulfate anion radicals $( 4 . 0 ~ \times$ $1 0 ^ { 8 } \mathrm { M } ^ { - 1 } \mathrm { S } ^ { - 1 } )$ , which causes the low concentrations of the hydroxyl radical and sulfate anion radical in the mixed system.25–28 For system (ⅱ), after $0 . 1 \ \mathrm { g } . 1 ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ photooxidation treatment for $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ , the SCA of PI film experiences small change with $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ photocatalysis time. Only for the composite system, SCA decreases with the photooxidation time and then reaches a minimum value $( 1 8 . 7 ^ { \circ } )$ with the treatment time of $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ . This amount is much lower than those of recently reported treatments, such as $\mathbf { A r } / \mathbf { O } _ { 2 }$ plasma treatment $( 3 2 . 9 ^ { \circ } ) , ^ { 1 0 ^ { \circ } } 0 . 5 \mathrm { ~ \ r ~ M ~ \ } $ OH treatment $( 2 6 . 3 ^ { \circ } ) , ^ { 7 } \ \mathrm { U } \bar { \mathsf { V } }$ $( 2 5 4 \mathrm { n m } )$ irradiation $( 2 5 ^ { \circ } )$ ,15 grafting of carboxylic acid $( 4 9 ^ { \circ } )$ .11 We believe that the pretreatment, $0 . 5 \ \mathrm { { g } } . 1 ^ { - 1 } \ \mathrm { { T i O } } _ { 2 } ^ { \bullet }$ photocatalysis for $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ , is the key factor to achieve a low SCA. After photocatalysis treatment, some amide groups on the surface of the PI film were broken into carboxyl and amino groups. These defect structures and functional groups could act as electron transporters and activators to promote the breaking of O–O bond in the persulfate,29,30 which increases the concentration of sulfate anion radical in the solution and results in an excellent modification performance. Therefore, the treatment process (ⅲ) is used for the following investigations.

Effects of the modification conditions on the surface morphology and the surface roughness of $_ { P I }$ films.—The surface morphologies of PI films with the photooxidation time are acquired with SEM images (Fig. S1). Compared with the bare PI film, no obvious change was found on the surface. Then, the change of the surface roughness was investigated with AFM (Fig. 3 and Table I). After photocatalysis treatment for $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ , an uneven PI surface is formed. Some parts are high, and some parts are low, the whole looks like multiple island structures (Fig. 3a), $\mathrm { R _ { a } }$ and $\mathsf { R } _ { q }$ of the film are 4.2 and $6 . 8 \mathrm { n m }$ , respectively. With further photooxidation treatment, the island structure disappears slowly and a uniform acicular structure forms on the surface of PI films. The $\mathrm { R _ { a } }$ and $\mathsf { R } _ { q }$ of PI films increase with the photooxidation time and reach the maximum values (11.5 and $1 3 . 5 \mathrm { n m }$ ) at $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ for the photooxidation (Fig. 3d and Table I), which are much higher than those obtained only by photooxidation for $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ (4.8 and $6 . 6 \mathrm { n m }$ ). The trend of $\mathrm { R _ { a } }$ and $\mathrm { R } _ { q }$ with the photooxidation time is the same as the trend of surface hydrophilicity with the photooxidation time. However, the $\mathrm { R _ { a } }$ of the PI film after the photooxidation treatment is much lower than those of other treatment methods, such as alkaline $\mathrm { K M O _ { 4 } }$ treatment $( 1 0 \mathrm { n m } )$ ,21 $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ plasma $( 2 4 \mathrm { n m } )$ ),5 surface grafting $( 6 3 \mathrm { n m }$ ),12 UV irradiation $( 1 2 0 \mathrm { n m } )$ ),14 however they meet the requirements of FPCB for the smooth surface of thin materials.

Surface chemistry of PI films with the composite treatment.— The surface chemistry of the PI films is studied by FTIR and XPS spectroscopy. Figure $4 \mathrm { a }$ shows FTIR spectra of the PI films with different photooxidation times. The characteristic absorption peak at $1 7 2 0 \mathrm { c m } ^ { \hat { - } 1 }$ is attributed to the absorption vibration of the –COOH group. The absorption strength increases with the photooxidation time and reaches a maximum at the photooxidation for $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ . After that, the absorption strength decreases with a prolongation of the photooxidation time. The change of the –COOH absorption strength with the treatment time agrees with that of surface contact angle with the photooxidation time.

The change of carbon groups on the PI surface with the photooxidation time is shown in Table IV. The C–N group content decreases with the treatment time which is the opposite of the change in the C–OH and COOH group contents. These results further confirm that the $\mathrm { C - N }$ structure is destroyed with the photooxidation while COOH and C–OH groups form.

The O 1 s and $_ \mathrm { ~ \scriptsize ~ N ~ 1 ~ s ~ }$ XPS spectra of the PI films with the photooxidation time are shown in Fig. S4 and Table S1. The N 1 s peak is resolved into two peaks, at $4 0 0 . 1 \mathrm { e V }$ for the amide group $\scriptstyle { \bar { ( } \mathbf { N - C = } 0 ) }$ and at $4 0 1 . 1 \mathrm { e V }$ for the amine group (N–H). The O 1 s peak is resolved into the carbonyl group $\scriptstyle ( C = 0$ or $\mathrm { \bf N - C = O }$ ) at $5 3 2 \mathrm { e V }$ and the $\mathrm { C - O H }$ group at $5 3 3 . 3 \mathrm { e V }$ . The changing trend of the amine group content is the same as that of $\mathrm { C - O H }$ group, further demonstrating that the amide group is oxidized during the photooxidation treatment and that about one-fifth of the amide groups on the surface is converted to carboxyl and amino groups.

Effect of surface modification on the adhesion strength.—Two main approaches to obtain a high adhesion strength between the electroless copper film and the PI film include improving surface wettability and increasing surface roughness, causing a mechanical locking effect. Having an optimized composite treatment, the surface roughness of PI film is smaller than that achieved by other methods, but the SCA of the PI films is much lower than the previously reported values. The adhesion strength between the electroless copper film and the untreated PI film is zero. After $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ photocatalysis treatment for $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ or $\mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ photooxidation treatment for $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ , the adhesion strengths between the electroless copper film and the PI film becomes 0.60 or $0 . 6 8 \mathrm { K N } { \cdot } \mathrm { m } ^ { - 1 }$ , respectively, indicating that individual photocatalysis treatment and individual photooxidation treatments are resultful. After photocatalysis for $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ , the effects of the photooxidation time on the adhesion strength are investigated and the results are shown in Fig. 5a. The adhesion strength of the copper film and the PI film increases with the photooxidation time and reaches a maximum $( 0 . 9 0 \mathrm { K N } { \cdot } \mathrm { m } ^ { - 1 } )$ at the photooxidation time of $3 0 \mathrm { { m i n } }$ , which meets the requirements of FPCB for surface roughness and adhesion strength. The trend of the adhesion strength with the treatment time is exactly the same as the trend of the surface hydrophilicity with the treatment time. Further, the adhesion strength as the functions of the surface roughness and the surface contact angle are shown in Figs. 5b and 5c. The adhesion strength increases continuously with an increase of the surface roughness, and it increases with a decrease of the surface contact angle, but all the changes are not linear. These results indicate that the surface hydrophilicity and surface roughness play very important role in the improvement of the adhesion strength.

# Conclusions

A composite modification method, using $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ photocatalysis and $\mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ photo-oxidation, is applied to achieve a strong adhesion force for the electroless copper film on the smooth PI surface. The effects of $\mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ content, $\mathsf { p H }$ , and treatment time on SCA, surface roughness, surface chemistry, and the adhesion strength of the PI films are investigated by surface contact angle measurements, SEM observation, AFM, and XPS. With achieving an optimal composite treatment, the PI film keeps a smooth surface with a surface roughness of only $1 1 . 5 \mathrm { n m }$ . However, the surface wettability improves markedly and the corresponding SCA decreases from $7 6 . 9 ^ { \circ }$ to $1 8 . 7 ^ { \circ }$ . Also, the adhesion strength between the PI film and the electroless copper film reaches $0 . 9 0 \mathrm { K N } \mathrm { m } ^ { - 1 }$ . These results confirm that the composite treatment by $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ photocatalysis and $\mathrm { N a } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 8 }$ photooxidation is an effective and environmentally friendly method for surface etching of the PI film.
