# . Introduction

Levofloxacin (LFX) is an antibiotic widely used in oral and intravenous formulations due to its broad bacterial spectrum, and has been applied mainly in treatments of bacterial infections in humans, tuberculosis and more recently, in patients with asymptomatic HIV infection (Piscitelli et al., 1999; Ma et al., 2019). This compound has been continuously determined in an aquatic environment, due to its high solubility and accumulation, generating concern among environmentalists (Blokhina et al., 2016). The accumulation of LFX in the aquatic environment is mainly due to its partial metabolism in the human body $( 6 5 - 8 0 \% )$ , being discarded through the urine together with its metabolites, causing serious damage to the aquatic ecosystem, in addition to the increase in bacterial resistance. Due to its low biodegradability, LFX is not withdraw in conventional wastewater treatment plants (WwTPs) (Liu et al., 2017; Lu et al., 2019). Therefore, the development of a quick and effective treatment for removing LFX from the environment becomes a necessity.

Several studies have been carried out in recent years with the purpose of developing techniques capable of treating refractory organic compounds from wastewater, the majority of which are based on advanced oxidation processes (AOP) due to the formation of radicals with high oxidizing power, such as the radical ·OH (Ghauch and Tuqan, 2012; Ma et al., 2019). The formation of sulfate radicals $\left( { \mathrm { S O } } _ { 4 } { } ^ { \cdot - } \right)$ has also gained great prominence in AOP, since $S O _ { 4 } { ^ { \bullet } } ^ { - }$ exhibits a longer half-life, redox potential, selectivity and does not depend on the pH of the solution, compared to the radical ·OH, and may be more efficient in oxidizing organic pollutants (Gao and Zou, 2020; Zhong et al., 2020).

Heterogeneous photocatalysis has also stood out in the removal of organic compounds (Hu et al., 2019; Lu et al., 2019; Renda et al., 2021). In this process, the degradation of organic pollutants can occur directly, from the photodegradation of the molecule, and indirectly by the photo-generated charges and oxidants (Abukhadra et al., 2020; Lima et al., 2021). The combination of light incidence and application of electric potential during photoelectrocatalytic processes show excellent results in the treatment of recalcitrant compounds, with a quick and efficient removal. Generally the most used semiconductor materials as photoanodes are $\mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 }$ and ZnO (Xing et al., 2018; Qin et al., 2019; Raizada et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2020).

The Ti/MMO/ZnO photoanode showed excellent photocatalytic activity and high stability in the photoelectrolysis of antibiotics and herbicides. The great advantage of this electrode in photoelectrocatalytic processes is its photostability during oxidative processes under application of a wide range of external potential and light irradiation. This facilitates its use in electrochemical wastewater treatments under different conditions. In addition, Ti/MMO/ZnO presents $\mathrm { Z n O }$ nanostructures with a high surface area, due to the electrodeposition of $\mathrm { Z n O }$ on a titanium substrate covered with MMO film $\mathrm { ( R u _ { 0 . 3 } T i _ { 0 . 7 } O _ { 2 } ) }$ highly porous (Goulart et al., 2021a, 2021b). This nanostructured morphology associated with a high surface area can improve the light transport of the material, generating more load carriers, thus increasing the rates of degradation of organic pollutants (Cerrón-Calle et al., 2019).

Thus, the degradation of the LFX antibiotic in ${ \mathrm { N a } } _ { 2 } S { \mathrm { O } } _ { 4 }$ solution at different $\mathtt { p H }$ values using the Ti/MMO/ZnO as a photoanode was investigated in this work. The influence of light and applied potential, and the $\mathtt { p H }$ of the solution were also considered in the degradation efficiency of the antibiotic, in addition to the formation of oxidants and reaction intermediates species, such as carboxylic acids and ionic compounds. This study reports and discusses the operational conditions and the reasons for a good photoelectrocatalytic performance of Ti/MMO/ ZnO photoanode during the degradation of LFX. Because of the novelty of the electrode, instead of using real wastewater, sulfate solutions containing the antibiotic were used because in those solution the formation of oxidants (including radicals) is well known and helps to elucidate the mechanisms that explain the differences among the different tests (Cañizares et al., 2009; Groenen Serrano, 2021). This corresponds to a technology readiness level of 4, what it is important to be highlighted because higher TRL evaluations should only be carried out once these previous stages are fully understood (Lacasa et al., 2019). In addition, the application of semiconductor photoelectrodes in the degradation of antibiotics in water treatments has great advantages over the photocatalysis widely described, since the applied potential can improve the separation of the photogenerated charges increasing efficiency of the oxidative process (Tantis et al., 2015). The electrode polarization can also improve adsorption and promote the reaction of intractable recalcitrant contaminants by other processes. One of the great advantages of photoelectrocatalysis is the ease in recovering the treated solution without contamination with particulate catalyst, due to the use of a solid electrode as a catalyst (Cristino et al., 2019). Therefore, investigating the different variables that govern this system, such as the nature of the photocatalytic material, the polarization, the reaction medium, the oxidizing species formed and the degradation behavior of the antibiotic may facilitate the application of these processes in the industry in the future.

The synthesis and morphological characterization of Ti/MMO/ZnO electrodes were carried out according to the method previously described by Goulart et al. (Goulart et al., 2021a, 2021b). The electrolysis in the absence of light irradiation generates very low currents that are insufficient to promote the generation of reactive oxygen species with oxidizing power for direct degradation of the antibiotic. Thus, the rate degradation of LFX $( < 1 0 \% )$ and kinetic constants $( \leq 0 . 0 1 5 \mathrm { m i n } ^ { - 1 } )$ were relatively low in electrolysis experiments at all pH studied. On the other hand, experiments carried out with UvC light irradiation promoted the degradation of LFX at all studied pH values. In photolysis, about $1 3 . 5 \%$ of LFX was degraded at $\mathtt { p H } 5$ and $6 7 . 0 \%$ at $\mathtt { p H 7 }$ and 9. An increase in the rate of drug degradation with an increase in the pH of the solution was also observed in the photoelectrolysis experiments, where the degradations were $5 5 . 5 \%$ , $9 1 . 2 \%$ and $9 9 . 2 \%$ at pH 5, 7 and 9 respectively.

All rates constants of photoelectrolysis were higher than in the photolysis and electrolysis experiment, which shows the synergistic effect between UvC light and the applied potential at the Ti/MMO/ZnO electrode. The synergistic effect between UvC light irradiation on the electrode and the applied potential was 4.48, 2.22 and 3.91 for pH 5.0, 7.0 and 9.0, respectively, calculated as the ration between the kinetic constant of the coupled process and the addition of the kinetics constants of the single processes $( \mathrm { k _ { p h o t o e l e t r o l y s i s } } / ( \mathrm { k _ { p h o t o l y s i s } } + \mathrm { k _ { e l e c t r o l y s i s } } ) )$ , where it was proven the efficiency of photoelectrocatalysis compared to photolysis and electrolysis in the degradation of antibiotic. The degradation of LFX was facilitated in solutions with alkaline pH. The highest rates of mineralization were observed at $\tt p H 9 . 0$ , $6 \%$ for photolysis and $3 8 \%$ for photoelectrolysis, Fig. 3a.

The degradation of LFX in the presence of potential and/or UVC light in a sulfate medium at the different pH may be the result of numerous reaction processes. First, degradation can occur by direct photolysis from the absorption of UvC light radiation by the functional groups, aromatic rings and heteroatoms of the molecule; or indirect photolysis with the degradation of photosensitive species, Eq. (2).

At the presence of UVC light, the electrons of the $\mathrm { z n O }$ valence band (VB) are promoted to the conduction band (CB) forming the pair $\mathbf { e } _ { \mathrm { { C B } } } /$ $\mathrm { h } _ { \mathrm { V B } } ^ { + }$ , Eq (3). The high oxidative potential of the hole $( \mathrm { h } _ { \mathrm { V B } } ^ { + } )$ in the photoanode can directly oxidize LFX forming reactive intermediates, Eq. (4). The $\mathrm { h } _ { \mathrm { V B } } ^ { + }$ in the VB can oxidize adsorbed water or hydroxide ions to produce •OH, Eq. (5). The electron in the catalyst CB can reduce $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ to the superoxide anion, Eq. (6). The radical hydroxyl can attack LFX producing intermediates (Int), and these species can react with ·OH producing end products (P), Eq. $( 7 )$ (Mijin et al., 2009).

The $s 0 _ { 4 } \cdot \bar { }$ and ·OH radicals can react with aromatic compounds from electron transfer, they can also abstract hydrogen atoms and/or form radical adducts. According to the properties of functional groups of the molecule, electron donation/removal in the aromatic compound may occur by $s 0 _ { 4 } \cdot \bar { }$ species leading to the mineralization of the compound (Lee et al., 2020; Sánchez-Montes et al., 2020). The reactivity of $S O _ { 4 } { } ^ { \bullet }$ species is pH independent and at neutral pH, $S O _ { 4 } { ^ \bullet }$ is more reactive than •OH radical (Dewil et al., 2017). However, the formation of •OH in an alkaline medium is favored (Kaneco et al., 2004); increasing the reactive species in solution, leading to a more oxidizing environment.

Considering that the oxidizing species formed during the treatment may improve the degradation of organic compounds (Poblete et al., 2019; Bi et al., 2020), the final concentration of the oxidants in solution at different pH values, as a function on the processes studied, was evaluated, Fig. 3b. The concentration of oxidants presented corresponds to the remaining oxidants that did not react chemically with other compounds in the solution. Therefore, we cannot consider in this value, very reactive oxidants formed, with a short life span and that act before being detected. In acidic and neutral pH, the highest remaining concentration of oxidants was observed when compared to neutral pH for all processes. The high concentration of oxidants at these pH values may be related to the low consumption of these species during the degradation of LFX, which corroborates the lower rates of degradation and mineralization of the compound when compared to pH9, where greater efficiency of degradation was obtained. In electrolysis at pH5 and in photoelectrolysis at $\tt p H 7$ , a similar amount of oxidants was observed; however, there is a greater degradation of LFX during photoelectrolysis. This may be related to the activation of other oxidizing species in the presence of light, from electron/hole pair formed on the surface of the electrode during photoelectrolysis. In this process it is known that there is the decomposition of molecular oxidants forming short-lived and highly reactive radicals, which explains the higher rates of removal of the LFX during photoelectrolysis when compared to electrolysis.

The correlation of the effect of the pH on the photodegradation of LFX is complex, as several factors must be considered, such as the characteristics of the photocatalyst and LFX at different pH values, solvent molecules, electrostatic interactions between semiconductor surface, in addition to the formation of radicals oxidizers charged as ·OH (Eskandari et al., 2018).

At acidic pH, the excess of $\mathrm { H } ^ { + }$ ions in the solution can act as a ·OH radical scavenger, decreasing the degradation rate of organic compounds, Eq. (10) (Devi et al., 2013).

$$
\mathrm { H ^ { + } + \cdot O H + e ^ { - } \to H _ { 2 } O }
$$

In addition, the low efficiency of LFX degradation at acidic pH may be the result of the dissolution process of $\mathrm { Z n O }$ , Eq. (11) (Khataee and Zarei, 2011).

$$
\mathrm { Z n O } + 2 \mathrm { H ^ { + } }  \mathrm { Z n ^ { 2 + } } + \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }
$$

$\mathrm { Z n O }$ generally presents greater stability at alkaline $\mathrm { p H }$ , and its lower solubility is obtained at $\mathsf { p H }$ values close to 9 (Liu et al., 2018). The stability of the $\mathrm { Z n O }$ film at pH 9 may have directly influenced the rates of degradation and mineralization of LFX.

The electrostatic interaction between the photocatalyst surface and the studied molecule is generally considered to explain the degradation rates at different pH values (Kaur et al., 2017). Under acid and alkaline conditions, the ZnO surface can be protonated and deprotonated (Eskandari et al., 2018). LFX contains a basic group and an acid group in its structure and has two $\mathsf { p K } _ { \mathtt { a } }$ values (5.7 and 7.9). Within this pH range it is mainly as a zwitterionic form in solution. Under acid and alkaline conditions, LFX is in its cationic or anionic form (Hirano et al., 2006; Guo et al., 2010). Considering the electrostatic interactions between the Ti/MMO/ZnO surface and LFX, both at acidic pH $\mathrm { ( p H 5 . 0 ) }$ and alkaline pH $\left( \mathrm { p H 9 . 0 } \right)$ , could occurs the molecules repulsion by the catalyst surface. At $\mathtt { p H } 5 . 0$ , the low rate of degradation of LFX may be related to the repulsion between the electrode surface and the molecule in its cationic form. However, at $\tt p H 9 . 0$ , other variables may have a greater influence on the degradation of LFX than electrostatic interactions, since at this pH there was greater efficiency in the process. For example, in an alkaline medium the formation of the radical •·OH is favored and this radical can more easily attack LFX leading to its oxidation when compared to the acidic and neutral medium (Kaneco et al., 2004).

During the photolysis and photoelectrolysis experiments, two organic intermediates were formed at the three studied pH values, Fig. 4. The photoelectrolysis was more efficient in degrading these intermediates over time when compared to photolysis, where the concentration of these compounds remained constant or increased at the end of the analysis. Studies show that during the photodegradation of LFX with different semiconductor materials $\mathrm { ( S m _ { 6 } W O _ { 1 2 } / g  – C _ { 3 } N _ { 4 } }$ (Laskhmi et al., 2021) and $\mathrm { A g _ { 3 } V O _ { 4 } / A g _ { 2 } C O _ { 3 } }$ (Sun et al., 2020)) initially occurs demethylation and decarboxylation of the LFX methyl-morpholine group, followed by defluorination by the photogenerated hydroxyls and a degradation of the N-methyl piperazine ring. Abukhadra et al. initially reported the carboxylation/decarboxylation of LFX, followed by depiperazinylation and finally, demethylation processes when used clay nanotubes decorated with ZnO (ZnO/KNTs) as photocatalyst (Abukhadra et al., 2020). During the photoelectrolysis of LFX with $\mathbf { M o S _ { 2 } } / \mathrm { T i O _ { 2 } } ,$ the formation of intermediates occurred from the initial opening of the piperazine ring, followed by the oxidation of the amine, decarboxylation and demethylation. Finally, the intermediates of LFX defluoridation and organic acids and inorganic ions after mineralization were observed (Zeng et al., 2019). In our work, the formation of short chain organic acids was also observed at the end of processes with light.

During photolysis, the maleic and oxamic acids were formed and in LFX photoelectrolysis the oxamic, oxalic, maleic and acetic acids were formed at the different studied pH, Fig. 5. The formation of short-chain acids was facilitated at neutral, alkaline $\mathsf { p H }$ and during photoelectrolysis. This may be related to a higher rate of degradation and mineralization of LFX at these pH values, mainly in photoelectrolysis experiments.

Fig. 6 shows the concentration of ionic species formed at the photolysis, electrolysis and photoelectrolysis of LFX at different pH values. In all the studied processes, there is a slight decrease in the $s 0 _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - }$ ions concentration (from the electrolyte) with an increase in the pH of the solution. This behavior is more evident in photolysis and photoelectrolysis, where the consumption of these $S O _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - }$ ions may be due to the photogenerated holes in the Ti/MMO/ZnO during photoelectrolysis and the presence of $\mathrm { O H } ^ { - }$ in an alkaline medium, which leads to the formation of •·OH radicals which react with $S 0 _ { 4 } ^ { 2 - }$ forming sulfate radicals, according to Eqs. (8) and (9) (Hu et al., 2003).

The N-minerals of LFX degradation by different processes were also evaluated. As shown in Fig. 6b and c, only $\mathrm { N O } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ and $\mathrm { N H } _ { 4 } ^ { + }$ were detected at the LFX photolysis and photoelectrolysis. During electrolysis, there was no formation of N-minerals due to the low rate of degradation of LFX in this process. The concentrations of $\mathrm { N O } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ were higher at $\tt p H 9 . 0$ where the removal of organic matter was greater, both for photolysis and photoelectrolysis. However, it was observed a higher concentration of $\mathrm { N H } _ { 4 } ^ { + }$ in photolysis when compared to photoelectrolysis. The lower concentration of $\mathrm { N H } _ { 4 } ^ { + }$ during photoelectrolysis may be related to the greater conversion of this ion to nitrate during the analysis, due to the more oxidative environment in this process and the presence of photoelectrogenated free radicals (Meng et al., 2019). Rocha et al. also observed lower concentrations of $\mathrm { N H } _ { 4 } ^ { + }$ and higher concentrations of $\mathrm { N O } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ in the degradation processes of LFX where there was greater removal of organic charge, in the electro and photoelectro-Fenton processes (Rocha et al., 2017).

From the results obtained in this work it was found that the highest LFX removal rates were obtained during photoelectrocatalysis with Ti/ MMO/ZnO in sodium sulfate solution with $\mathtt { p H 9 . 0 }$ , under the potential of $_ { 0 . 2 \mathrm { ~ V ~ } }$ and UV irradiation. LFX presents acid/base characteristics which allowed investigating the interaction between its molecular structure and the photocatalytic properties of the electrode. Herein, the negative form of the LFX, predominant in basic pH, was the one with the highest rate of degradation. The Ti/MMO/ZnO electrode proved to be an excellent photocatalyst material in the degradation of the antibiotic LFX under the studied conditions. This electrode is a versatile material, being possible to apply it in solutions with different pH values with removal rates varying from 55 to $9 9 \%$ of the organic compound. In addition, it is possible to work with Ti/MMO/ZnO by applying low potentials (0.2 V), which sets it apart from other photocatalyst materials used in the LFX oxidation that generally require higher potentials and several hours for complete oxidation of the molecule (Cristino et al., 2019, 2020; Gao et al., 2021). Another advantage of the proposed photoelectrocatalytic process at water treatment containing LFX is the degradation of the organic intermediates formed during the oxidation, forming short chain acids and higher concentrations of inorganic ions such as $\mathrm { N O } _ { 3 } ^ { - }$ , which are characteristic of LFX mineralization.

# 4. Conclusions

From this work, the following conclusions can be drawn:

- The Ti/MMO/ZnO electrode stability and photoactivity were maintained under the incidence of light irradiation and potential application during photoelectrolysis.   
- It was found that light irradiation exercised a fundamental role in the removal of the drug, since the photolysis and photoelectrolysis experiments were the ones that presented the highest degradation rates, 67.0 and $9 9 . 2 \%$ ,respectively.

- In addition, the formation of intermediates, N-mineral species and the efficiency of TOC removal were dependent on the studied pH, obtaining the best results in alkaline $\mathsf { p H }$ and during photoelectrolysis. This may be related to the great stability of the Ti/ MMO/ZnO electrode, easier generation of radicals ·OH in alkaline medium and photoactivation of sulfate ions, which results in greater degradation efficiency.

- Ti/MMO/ZnO is a photocatalyst material that stands out due to its photostability in different reaction media and the use of low potentials during photocatalytic processes. In addition, the nanostructured surface of this electrode has a high surface area, which improves the transport of photogenerated charges, leading to higher rates of levofloxacin degradation.

- From the results, it is suggested that the Ti/MMO/ZnO can be used as a photo-anode in the efficient degradation of this antibiotic in effluents containing a high concentration of salts.
