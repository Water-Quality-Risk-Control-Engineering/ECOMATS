# Metal-Organic Frameworks and Their Derived Materials: Emerging Catalysts for a Sulfate Radicals-Based Advanced Oxidation Process in Water Purification

Chaohai Wang, Jeonghun Kim,\* Victor Malgras, Jongbeom Na, Jianjian Lin, Jungmok You, Ming Zhang, Jiansheng Li,\* and Yusuke Yamauchi\*

With the ever-growing environmental issues, sulfate radical $( \mathsf { S O } _ { 4 } ^ { \bullet - } )$ based advanced oxidation processes (SR-AOPs) have been attracting widespread attention due to their high selectivity and oxidative potential in water purification. Among various methods generating $s 0 _ { 4 } \mathrm { \mathrm { \cdots } }$ , employing heterogeneous catalysts for activation of peroxymonosulfate or persulfate has been demonstrated as an effective strategy. Therefore, the future advances of SR-AOPs depend on the development of adequate catalysts with high activity and stability. Metalorganic frameworks (MOFs) with large surface area, ultrahigh porosity, and diversity of material design have been extensively used in heterogeneous catalysts, and more recently, enormous effort has been made to utilize MOFs-based materials for SR-AOPs applications. In this work, the state-of-the-art research on pristine MOFs, MOFs composites, and their derivatives, such as oxides, metal/ carbon hybrids, and carbon materials for SR-AOPs, is summarized. The mechanisms, including radical and nonradical pathways, are also detailed ir the discussion. This work will hopefully promote the future development of MOFs-based materials toward SR-AOPs applications.

# 1. Introduction

Global environmental challenges, especially water pollution due to the rapid growth of economy and industrialization, have become one of the most critical threats humanity is currently facing.[1] Organic pollutants such as industrial dyes, pesticides, pharmaceuticals, and personal care products

$\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { ^ { \bullet - } }$ is generally produced by physical or chemical activation of peroxymonosulfate (PMS) or persulfate (PS).4) Since Anipsitakis and Dionysiou first reported Co/PMS system for the degradation of 2,4-dichlorophenol and atrazine in 2003,[5] tremendous scientific efforts have been made to develop SR-AOPs. To date, there are many methods for generating $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { \mathrm { ^ { - } } }$ , using direct energy (heat, UV, ultrasound, etc.) or through catalysis in alkaline media, transition metal ions $( \mathsf { C o } ^ { 2 + }$ , $\mathrm { C u } ^ { 2 + }$ , $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + }$ , etc.), metal oxides $\mathrm { ( C o _ { 3 } O _ { 4 } }$ , $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 }$ , $\mathrm { C u O }$ ,etc.), or metal-free materials (carbon-, boron-, and sulfur-based).[6] Employing catalysts to activate PMS or PS has demonstrated to be a more effective and less costly strategy.[7] The development of catalysts in terms of morphology and composition can be mainly divided into two stages: i) from homogeneous to heterogeneous catalysis; ii) from metallic system to metal-free system. Previous research shows that metal-based materials have a high efficiency for activating PMS or PS, however, most of them tend to leak toxic metal ions. Alternatively, despite the many exciting approaches using carbon materials (nanodiamonds, carbon nanotubes, graphene, etc.) for the activation of PMS, the catalytic performance of such metal-free catalysts remains insufficient[3b,8] Therefore, seeking better materials with adequate activity and stability is a persistent challenge for future advances of SR-AOPs.

Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) are a class of highly porous materials which consist of metal ions/clusters and organic ligands. Since it was first defined in 1995 by Yaghi and Li,9] more than 20 000 MOFs with different metal ions/ clusters or organic ligands have been discovered and applied to adsorption,[10] separation,[11]energy storage,[12]catalysis,[13] etc.[14] In addition, due to their components and structural characteristics, MOFs have been used as ideal sacrificial templates for fabricating oxides,[15] metal/carbon hybrids,[16] and carbon materials (especially doped with N, P, and S) via simple pyrolysis.[17] In particular, in the past decade, numerous exciting advances of MOFs have been made in energy storage and conversion,[18] photocatalysis,[9] Fenton-like catalysis, [20] and organocatalysis[i3a,21] because of their high porosity, low cost, excellent activity/performance, and modifiable structure/ composite, which are strongly required in recent energy, environment-, and chemical synthesis-related research fields.

More recently, MOFs-based materials as emerging catalysts have been intensively applied for SR-AOPs and many significant outcomes have been achieved. To the best of our knowledge, however, there is only one review about pristine MOFs for SR-AOPs.[20c] In this work, we first introduce and summarize the state-of-the-art research of pristine MOFs, MOFs composites, and their derivatives such as metal oxides, metal/carbon hybrids, and carbon materials applied to SR-AOPs (Figure 2). This report sets the basic knowledge necessary to understand in which direction should future scientific effort be aiming to exploit the full potential of advanced MOF-based materials toward improving environmental remediation.

In a recent study, Zhang's group reported a Materials of Institute Lavoisier (MIL)-53(Fe),[23] a Fe-containing MOF, used as catalyst to activate PS in order to degrade Acid Orange 7 (AO7, sodium 4-[(2E)-2-(2-oxonaphthalen-1-ylidene)hydrazinyl]benzenesulfonate) under visible light irradiation (LED). They found out that the introduction of a low concentration of PS can significantly enhance the degradation performance of MIL-53(Fe). This can be explained as PS is an electron acceptor and can prevent the fast recombination of electron $( \mathrm { e ^ { - } } )$ and hole $( \mathrm { h ^ { + } } )$ . Using photoluminescence and electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) analyses (Figure 3c), the authors concluded that the excitation of MIL-53(Fe) by the visible light, thus producing photoinduced $\mathrm { e ^ { - } }$ and $\mathrm { h ^ { + } }$ , is a likely mechanism (Figure 3d). Because PS can capture $\mathrm { e ^ { - } }$ to produce $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { ^ { * - } }$ radicals, it effectively facilitates charge separation. The strong oxidation capacity of both $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { \mathrm { ^ { - } } }$ and $\mathrm { h ^ { + } }$ can thereby accelerate the degradation of AO7. Later, Fang and co-workers reported the fabrication of a bimetallic $\mathrm { F e / T i  – M O F  – N H } _ { 2 }$ for removal of Orange II.[24] Due to the existence of $\mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + }$ which increases the formation of hydroxyl radicals, the degradation efficiency could be significantly enhanced. Their study also showed that Fe/Ti-MOF. $\mathrm { N H } _ { 2 }$ exhibited the highest activity when $\mathrm { F e } / \mathrm { T i } = 3 { : } 1$ (Figure 3e). This efficiency can be imputed to the synergetic effect between Fe${ \mathrm { M O F } } { \cdot } { \mathrm { N H } } _ { 2 }$ and Ti-MOF. $\mathrm { N H } _ { 2 }$ : $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + } / \mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + }$ redox cycle of the MOFs can activate PS to produce $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { \mathrm { ^ { - } } }$ ,while $\mathrm { T i } ^ { 4 + } / \mathrm { T i } ^ { 3 + }$ enhances the formation of $\cdot _ { \mathrm { O H } }$ .Both $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { \mathrm { ^ { - } } }$ and ·OH possess powerful oxidizing potential to degrade organic pollutants molecules. The results under different light conditions clearly highlight this synergy (Figure 3f). In addition, other bimetallic MOFs such as $[ \mathrm { F e } ( \mathrm { C N } ) _ { 6 } ]$ and $[ \mathrm { C o } ( \mathrm { C N } ) _ { 6 } ]$ based Prussian blue analogues (PBAs) have also been investigated and it was found that Co promotes PMS activation significantly compared to other metal ions.[25] Past works on MOF catalysts employed to activate PS or PMS for the degradation of organic pollutants are summarized in Table 1. The existing research mainly focused on the Fe-based or Co-related MOFs, however other MOFs should be further studied, especially bimetallic or multimetallic systems which can potentially benefit from synergistic effect.

# 3. MOFs Composites

The MOFs combined with other catalysts having only high surface area and thermal stability cannot achieve significantly enhanced catalytic performance and stability. A recent research by Cai's group devoted to designing of yolk-shell $\mathrm { C o } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 } @$ MOFs nanoreactor for improved environmental remediation.[26] As shown in Figure 4a, they synthesized a yolk-shell $\mathrm { C o } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 } @$ MOFs nanoreactor via simple solvothermal method. The transmission electron microscopy (TEM) image of the $\mathrm { C o } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 } @ \mathrm { M O F s }$ clearly revealed the yolk—shell structure $\mathrm { C o } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 }$ as the core and MOF-5 as the shell) (Figure 4b). Nearly $100 \%$ of 4-chlorophenol (4-CP) was degraded within the first $6 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ using the $\mathrm { C o } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 } @$ MOFs as catalyst, whereas only $5 9 . 6 \%$ efficiency was reached for bare ${ \mathrm { C o } } _ { 3 } { \mathrm { O } } _ { 4 }$ NPs. Furthermore, inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) measurements of Co leaching could determine that the MOFs shell act as an armor, keeping the ${ \mathrm { C o } } _ { 3 } { \mathrm { O } } _ { 4 }$ core safe from erosion (Figure 4c). The mechanisms responsible for this performance can be described as follows: i) the MOF shell has a large surface area and offers efficient adsorption of enriched 4-CP, resulting in a high concentration of reactants within the nanoreactor; ii) in the interstitial hollow spaces, the microenvironment can increase the opportunity of collisions between $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { ^ { * - } }$ and 4-CP, which provides a driving force to promote the oxidation degradation of 4-CP; iii) the MOFs shell would effectively prevent the aggregation of magnetic ${ \mathrm { C o } } _ { 3 } { \mathrm { O } } _ { 4 }$ NPs (Figure 4d). A similar study has been reported for the removal of AO7 by PS catalyzed with $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 } @$ MIL-101.[27]

Although MOFs and MOFs-based nanoreactors are efficient catalysts for advanced SR-AOPs, the clogging of the pipes and recycling problems largely thwart the practical use of powdery

MOFs materials. In 2017, our group first developed an effective strategy to solve these issues by electrospinning MOFs into nanofibers. We have successfully prepared ZIF-67/PAN composite nanofibers and used them for removal of Acid Yellow (AY), tetracycline (TC), and Bisphenol A (BPA) (Figure 5a).[28] The activity of the nanofibers remained high with the benefit of fast recovery. Due to their 1D flexible character, ZIF-67/polyacrylonitrile (PAN) nanofibers were facilely processed into a thin film and deposited on a glass sand funnel (Figure 5b). The dynamic catalytic experiment demonstrated the superiority of the ZIF-67/PAN nanofibers and the great potential for practical applications. Furthermore, the effect of the initial pH and temperature on AY removal was also investigated. As shown in Figure 5c, the results demonstrated that ZIF-67/PAN possesses higher activity under neutral conditions. Under acidic conditions, $\mathrm { H ^ { + } }$ ions may inhibit the production of $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { \mathrm { ^ { - } } }$ radicals; while PMS may suffer from self-decomposition without generating sulfate radicals under alkaline conditions. Figure 5d shows that decolorization efficiency of AY improves significantly at higher temperature. From the radical scavenger experiments (Figure 5e), it can be clearly seen that $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { \mathrm { ^ { - } } }$ plays a important role in the degradation of AY. Moreover, after 5 cycles, ZIF-67/PAN retains high regeneration efficiency (more than $9 8 \%$ (Figure 5f). This reusable, flexible, and highly effective catalytic membrane showcases great promises for industrial applications. Similar MOFs loaded with polymers have been reported for SR-AOPs, including shaped bodies[29] and polymer beads[30] (Table 1). As shown in Figure 6, Yao et al. developed a method to load ZIF-9 and ZIF-12 on cellulose aerogel. These hybrid aerogels exhibit high performance for activating PMS to degrade organic pollutants, such as RhB, tetracycline hydrochloride (TC), and p-nitrophenol (PNP) (Figure 6g-i). They also studied the underlying mechanisms by EPR and radical scavenger experiments (Figure 6j1l), confirming that this pathway is $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { \mathrm { ^ { - } } }$ -dominated. Later, Lin and coworkers reported ZIF-67 supported on macroscale resin to form beads $( \mathrm { Z I F } @ \mathrm { R } )$ (Figure 7a). During RhB removal, $\mathrm { Z I F @ R }$ demonstrated high performance, decomposing the target pollutants within $2 0 \mathrm { m i n }$ (Figure 7b). The effects of $\mathrm { Z I F } @ \mathrm { R }$ dosage, oxone dosage, and oxone activation temperature were also investigated (Figure 7c—e). This determined that the decolorization kinetics was significantly enhanced at higher dosages and temperatures.

# 4. MOFs Derivatives

As described above, pristine MOFs and MOFs composites can work as activators for PS/PMS with excellent catalytic performance. More interestingly, apart from direct use purposes, MOFs consisting of ions/clusters and organic linkers can also be transformed into nanoporous metal oxides, metal/ carbon hybrids, and carbon materials by calcination and pyrolysis under suitable conditions.[32] For instance, the pyrolysis of MOFs under an inert atmosphere $( \mathrm { N } _ { 2 } ,$ rcan generate nanoporous metal/carbons composites. Further etching the residual metal species off can produce carbon materials. [33] On the other hand, if the direct heat treatment of MOFs is carried out in air, porous metal oxides can be obtained.[15] In 2012, our group directly used for the first time an Al-based

MOF as a self-sacrificial template for the production of nanoporous carbons (NPC).[34] Since then, extensive efforts have been performed to develop MOFs-derived materials. In a previous reviews, we discussed the features of MOFs-derived nanostructured materials (controllable porous architectures, high pore volumes, large surface areas, etc.) in detail.[35] Here, we highlight the recent progress made in the field, of nanostructured materials as emerging catalysts showing promising performance in SR-AOPs applications.

# 4.2. Metal/Carbon Hybrids

Although transition-metal-oxides (TMOs) are considered as the dominant and best catalysts for promoting SR-AOPs, yet the leaching of toxic metal ions impedes their further applications in environmental remediation. Recently, transition-metalnitrogen carbon materials (TMNCs) are emerging alternatives with low leaching and high efficiency, originating from the N coordination anchoring metal atoms and reducing their adsorption energy.43-45] MOFs with unique structures and compositions are the desired precursors to synthesize TMNCs. In fact, some impressive results have been achieved recently (Table 3). Wang and co-workers directly carbonized the typical ZIF-67 to prepare $\mathrm { C o } @ \mathrm { N . C }$ . From the high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) image (Figure 13a),[43] it can be clearly observed that the Co nanoparticles are encapsulated inside N-doped carbon layers, which protect it from corrosion and leaching during the reaction. After $2 4 \mathrm { { h } }$ reaction, the cobalt leaching of $\mathrm { C o } @ \mathrm { N . C }$ was as low as $0 . 1 1 \mathrm { \ m g \ L ^ { - 1 } }$ . More interestingly, they found that only singlet oxygen is involved in the BPA degradation as illuminated in the $\mathrm { C o } @ \mathrm { N . C / P M S }$ system (Figure 13b). However, for TMO catalysts, $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { \mathrm { ^ { - } } }$ plays the dominant role. Li et al. synthesized a series of graphene-encapsulated $\mathrm { F e } _ { x } \mathrm { M n } _ { 6 - x } \mathrm { C o } _ { 4 } \mathrm { - N } @ { \mathrm { C } }$ with a well-controlled morphology by using PBAs as templates (Figure 13c).[44] The catalytic performance of $\mathrm { F e } _ { x } \mathrm { M n } _ { 6 - x } \mathrm { C o } _ { 4 } \mathrm { - N } @ { \mathrm { C } }$ was considerably enhanced with increasing Mn content (Figure 13d,e). Density functional theory (DFT) calculations revealed that the excellent activity attributed to $\mathrm { M n } _ { 4 } \mathrm { N }$ could largely reduce adsorption energy and facilitate electron transfer.

In recent years, single-atom catalyst (SAC) with maximum atom-utilization efficiency and exposed active sites has attracted the attention.[46,47] Currently, Li et al. first demonstrated single cobalt atoms anchored on porous N-doped graphene with high activity and stability for BPA removal.[46] The single cobalt atoms catalyst was prepared by pyrolyzing Fe-Co-containing PBA in $\mathrm { N } _ { 2 }$ and then treating with 1 M $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { S O } _ { 4 }$ at $8 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 4 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ as illustrated in Figure 14a. # 4.3. Carbon Materials

Carbon materials (CMs), especially porous CMs, as emerging heterogeneous catalysts exhibit promising performance in the field of AOPs because they are metal-free and have good chemical stability[8,48] Since Wang and co-workers first reported that reduced graphene oxide (rGO) could effectively catalyze PMS in 2012,[49] various CMs as green catalysts such as nanodiamonds (0D),[50] nanotubes (1D),[51] GO (2D),[49,52] hexagonally ordered mesoporous carbon $( 3 \mathrm { D } ) ^ { [ 5 3 ] }$ have been developed for wastewater remediation. Furthermore, heteroatom (B, N, S, P, etc.)-doped CMs could further enhance the catalytic performance by modifying the surface properties and by generating point defects.[3b,54] Due to their inherent adjustable structure and composition, MOFs are excellent precursors for derivation and particular heteroatom doping of CMs. Therefore, reviewing the progress of MOF-derived carbon materials would be valuable to understand recent developments in SR-AOPs.

Recently, ZIF-8, a Zn-based MOFs, has been widely used as template to prepare N-doped CMs due to its facile synthesis methods, high N-containing, and large surface area.[16] Furthermore, Zn content can be readily vaporized during the pyrolysis process.[18b] Quan and co-workers used ZIF-8-derived N-doped porous carbons (NPCs) as a catalyst for phenol degradation[55] (Figure 15a). In order to investigate the relationship between the catalytic efficiency and nitrogen content or species, two other MOFs, $\mathrm { N H } _ { 2 } { \cdot } \mathrm { M I L } { \cdot } 5 3$ and IRMOF-3, were selected to prepare NPCs. Interestingly, the catalytic performance was significantly enhanced with increasing the graphitic N content (Figure 15b,c). Moreover, they also modulated the content of graphitic N by changing the calcination temperature, highlighting how graphitic N-assisted electron transfer plays a crucial role during PMS activation. Their study offered a new insight to prepare metal-free catalysts with high efficiency for SR-AOPs.

Taking into consideration graphitic N-driven activation of PMS, Wang et al. employed $Z \mathrm { n - C o }$ PBAs $( \mathrm { Z n } _ { 3 } [ \mathrm { C o } ( \mathrm { C N } ) _ { 6 } ] _ { 2 } \cdot 6 \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } )$ as template to prepare porous nitrogen-doped carbon (PNC) with a high graphitic N content, largely promoted by the presence of Co, to improve the catalytic performance.[56] Compared with ZIF-8, $- \mathrm { C } \equiv \mathrm { N }$ units in PBAs have a richer nitrogen content. During the carbonization process, Zn tends to vaporize, thus the yielding carbon with a rich porosity.

As shown in Figure 15d, the as-synthesized PNC had abundant porosity and high graphitization degree. They also studied the effect of temperature on graphitization. The catalytic activities demonstrated that PNC heated at $8 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ displayed a better PMS activation performance compared with other carbon materials such as GO, carbon nanotubes (CNTs), and ZIFs-derived (Figure 15e,f). More interestingly, the authors identified the presence of nonradical-dominated degradation of methylene blue (MB) in the PNC/PMS system. Previous studies have demonstrated that coupling is an effective strategy to enhance the performance of catalysts. Gong et al. reported a ZIF-8-derived N-doped carbon (ZIF-NC) with graphitic carbon nitride $\left( { \mathrm { g } } { \mathrm { - C } } _ { 3 } { \mathrm { N } } _ { 4 } \right)$ for catalysis of SR-AOPs [57] ZIF-NC was produced by a facile pyrolysis, after which ZIF-NC and $\mathrm { g } – \mathrm { C } _ { 3 } \mathrm { N } _ { 4 }$ were mixed in an appropriate mass ratio in methanol solution. The ZIF $\mathrm { \cdot N C } / \mathrm { g } { \cdot } \mathrm { C } _ { 3 } \mathrm { N } _ { 4 }$ composite was formed after heating the above mixture at $3 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ under Ar atmosphere. The resulting $\mathrm { Z I F - N C / g - C _ { 3 } N _ { 4 } }$ exhibited a high performance for the removal of BPA by catalyzed PMS under visible light irradiation. This considerable increase of catalytic performance could be attributed to the unique properties of Z $\mathrm { I F } { \cdot } \mathrm { N C } / \mathrm { g } { \cdot } \mathrm { C } _ { 3 } \mathrm { N } _ { 4 }$ such as, high surface area and porosity, superior conductivity, and abundant exposed active sites, which resulted in enhancing light harvesting and interfacial charge separation efficiency. Apart from ZIF-8 and PBAs, some other MOFs have also been used as templates to prepare heteroatom doped CMs, such as MIL-100, MIL-101, ZIF-67, etc. (Table 4). These interesting works open up a new way to prepare metal-free catalysts for environmental remediation.

# 5. Possible Mechanism

The mechanisms responsible for activating PS/PMS toward the removal of organic pollutants in MOFs-based materials include radical and nonradical pathways. As shown in Scheme 1, in pristine MOFs, MOFs composites, and MOFs-derived metaloxides, the catalytic degradation is $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { \mathrm { ^ { - } } }$ -dominated, while MOFsderived metal/carbon hybrids and carbon materials are governed by nonradical-dominated pathways. Up to now, there are several methods used for investigating these mechanisms, such as EPR analyses, radical quenching experiments, and theoretical calculation. For the radical pathway, metal ions from the catalysts can react with PS/PMS to generate $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { \mathrm { ^ { - } } }$ with powerful oxidizing potential, which can degrade and further decompose the organic pollutants molecules into $\mathrm { C O } _ { 2 }$ and $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }$ [22,28]Due to the limited mineralization capacity of $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { \mathrm { ^ { - } } }$ , pollutants can only partially oxidize into $\mathrm { C O } _ { 2 }$ and $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O }$ , the remaining translating into small molecule intermediates. On the other hand, some research groups found that carbon materials or metal/ carbon hybrids, especially N-doped carbon materials, can induce a unique nonradical oxidation process to activate PMS: graphitic $_ \mathrm { N }$ in carbon frameworks can effectively break the chemical inertness of the $\mathrm { s p } ^ { 2 }$ -hybridized carbon configuration, inducing electron transfer from neighboring carbon atoms to graphitic nitrogen atoms, therefore generating positively charged sites. These active sites effectively improve the adsorption capability of PMS and produce electron transfer intermediates which can act as a favorable platform for electrons to directly transfer from organic molecules toward PMS, instead of generating $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { ^ { \bullet - } }$ as the reactive species through a redox reaction.[56] However, the efficiency of nonradical oxidation processes depends on the conductivity, graphitic degree of the carbocatalyst, and N-doping. If the carbocatalyst has poor crystallinity (e.g., amorphous carbon), the transferred electron from organic molecules cannot be rapidly spread out, and the accumulated charges will induce the cleavage of the peroxide bond $( - \mathrm { O - O - } )$ of PMS to generate $\mathrm { S O } _ { 4 } \mathrm { ^ { * - } }$ , resulting in a radical oxidation process. Additional research showed that sometimes, the nonradical pathway can be ${ } ^ { 1 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ dominated.[43,46] Because of their complexity, nonradical pathways have not yet revealed all their secrets and more attention should be paid to these underlying mechanisms in order to exploit the full potential of these properties.

Compared with transitional catalysts, MOFs have many inherent advantages, such as large surface area, highly ordered structures, and various compositions. Therefore, MOFs-related materials have been considered as potential candidates for SR-AOPS. In addition, MOFs consisting of metal ions and organic ligands have attracted a great interest as outstanding templates/precursors for the preparation of metal oxides, metal/carbon hybrids, and carbon materials due to their porous structures and tailored compositions. Not to mention that their derivatives also showcase excellent catalytic performance. In this work, the recent development on MOFs and MOFs-derived materials for SR-AOPs have been summarized.

# 6. Conclusion and Prospects

In conclusion, the development of new technologies mainly depends on the advances of novel functional materials. MOFs, as emerging porous materials, have brought a revolutionary change to the field of AOPs in the past years.

Up to now, great progress has been achieved on the research of MOF-based materials for SR-AOPs which keeps moving rapidly forward. Scientific efforts have focused on controlling chemical composition by using different metal centers and organic ligands. However, the water sensitivity and insulating nature of most MOFs inhibit their application to SR-AOPs as a catalyst.

MOF-derived nanostructured materials for SR-AOPs may open up new ways toward their suitable usage. In fact, typical MOFs such as PBAs, ZIFs, MILs, etc., have been widely used as sacrificial templates to produce metal oxides, metal/carbon hybrids, carbon materials, and their composites with high performances for PMS activation in the degradation of organic pollutants. Among the derivatives, metal/carbon hybrids and carbon materials seem more environmentally friendly and have better chemical stability. However, issues related to the recycling of powdery materials largely thwart their application at the industrial scale. In addition, the catalytic degradation of MOFs-derived metal/carbon hybrids and carbon materials is governed by nonradical-dominated process. Because of their complexity, further study is necessary to fully exploit the potential of these properties. Although the optimization and implementation of MOFrelated materials for SR-AOPs have been widely studied, there are still many challenges to overcome, as illustrated in Scheme 2:

(a) Large-scale preparation: Only a few MOFs (ZIF-8, ZIF67, Hong Kong University of Science and Technology-1 (HKUST-1), etc.) can be synthesized at room temperature. Many rely on solvothermal methods, which impede the potential for large-scale preparation due to high time and energy consumption.   
(b) Water stability: The stability is essential for catalysts, however, most of MOFs are unstable in water and may decompose in highly oxidative environment, which limits their operation conditions. Therefore, future studies should target new strategies, such as the design of stable MOFs, modified MOFs, and armored MOFs (e.g. $\mathrm { M O F s @ S i O _ { 2 } }$ , $\mathrm { M O F s } @ \mathrm { T i O } _ { 2 } )$ .   
(c) Pyrolysis mechanism: Despite a variety of MOFs-derived nanostructures have been gained, controlling the fine structures is still a great challenge due to the pyrolytic transformation mechanisms remaining unclear. In reality, MOF-derived materials generally exhibit increase or decrease of surface area and disordered features, which are critical for catalytic performance. Therefore, further study should aim at understanding these processes.   
(d) Emerging contaminants: The present researches have been focused on removing dyes, phenols, and BPA. Other emerging contaminants (pesticides, PPCPs, perfluorinated compounds, etc.) need to be given more attention in the future.   
(e) Industrial application: The current studies have been carried out at lab-scale. More efforts on production cost and benefit should be devoted to the industrialization. Taking into consideration the eco-friendly and cost aspects, Fe and Zn-based MOFs, as well as their derivatives are likely to find industrial applications in the future.

Overall, MOFs and their derivatives, as young but rapidly growing functional materials, have demonstrated many new opportunities for preparing efficient catalysis in SR-AOPs. Although utilizing MOF-related materials as catalysts for environmental remediation has still a long way to go, the increasing interest of the scientific community to this field will likely allow an acceleration of research outcomes.
