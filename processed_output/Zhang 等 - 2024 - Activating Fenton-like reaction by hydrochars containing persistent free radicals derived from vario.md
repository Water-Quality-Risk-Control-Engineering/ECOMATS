# Activating Fenton-like reaction by hydrochars containing persistent free radicals derived from various pomelo peel components

Chaoyang Zhang1,2, Zili Jiang1,2, Wanxue Sun1,2, Yuyuan Tang1,2, Zhanying Zhang3,4, Changrong $\mathsf { S h i } ^ { 3 , 4 }$ and Xiuxiu Ruan1,2\*

# Abstract

To reveal the infuence of the diversity of precursors on the formation of environmental persistent free radicals (EPFRs), pomelo peel (PP) and its physically divided portion, pomelo cuticle (PC), and white fber (WF) were used as precursors to prepare six hydrochars: PPH-Fe, PCH-Fe, WFH-Fe, PPH, PCH, and WFH with and without Fe(III) addition during hydrothermal carbonization (HTC). PPH-Fe and WFH-Fe had higher EPFRs content $( 9 . 1 1 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 8 }$ and $8 . 2 5 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 8 }$ spins·g−1) compared to PPH and WFH $( 3 . 3 3 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 8 }$ and $2 . 9 6 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 8 } \mathsf { s p i n s cdot g } ^ { - 1 } )$ , indicating that iron-doping favored EPFRs formation. However, PCH-Fe had lower EPFRs content $( 2 . 7 8 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 8 } \mathsf { s p i n s { \cdot } g } ^ { - 1 } )$ than PCH $( 7 . 9 5 \times 1 0 ^ { \bar { 1 } 8 } \mathsf { s p i n s . g } ^ { - 1 } )$ , possibly due to excessive iron leading to the consumption of the generated EPFRs. For another reason, the required Fe(III) amount for EPFRs formation might vary among diferent precursors. PC has a lower concentration of phenolic compounds but $6 8 – 9 7 \%$ fatty acids, while WF and PP are rich in cellulose and lignin. In the Fenton-like reaction, oxygen-centered radicals of hydrochar played a signifcant role in activating ${ \sf H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ and efciently degrading bisphenol A (BPA). Mechanisms of reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation in hydrochar ${ \mathrm { \bar { H } } } _ { 2 } { \mathrm { O } } _ { 2 }$ system were proposed. EPFRs on hydrochar activate ${ \sf H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ via electron transfer, creating ·OH and $^ { 1 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ,$ leading to BPA degradation. More importantly, the embedded EPFRs on the hydrochar’s inner surface contributed to the prolonged Fenton-like reactivity of PPH-Fe stored for 45 days. This study demonstrates that by optimizing precursor selection and iron doping, hydrochars can be engineered to maximize their EPFRs content and reactivity, providing a cost-efective solution for the degradation of hazardous pollutants.

# Article Highlights

To favor g3-type EPFRs formation, the quantity of Fe(III) and aromatic compounds of the biomass should be matched.

# 1 Introduction

Environmental persistent free radicals (EPFRs) refer to a type of free radical substance that can exist in ambient air for hours to months (Balakrishna et  al. 2009; Borrowman et al. 2016; Dellinger et al. 2007; Liao et al. 2014; Truong et  al. 2010). Te formation of EPFRs involves the transfer of an unpaired electron to a stable molecule, forming a stable radical species. Transition metals and substituted aromatics are widely recognized as key factors for the formation of EPFRs in the environment matrices (Alfei and Pandoli 2024; HawrylukSidoruk et  al. 2024; Lomnicki et  al. 2008; Ruan et  al. 2019). EPFRs on biochar exhibit a high degree of reactivity and can activate $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ or persulfate (PS) to generate reactive oxygen species (ROS) (·OH, $\cdot S { \mathrm { O _ { 4 } } } ^ { - }$ and $^ 1 \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } )$ for the efective degradation of organic contaminants (Fang et  al. 2014; Huang et  al. 2023; Jin et  al. 2024; Yang et al. 2016; Zhang et al. 2021b). Interestingly, due to their abundant persistent free radicals (Fang et  al. 2015a; Zhang et  al. 2021a; Zhao et  al. 2018), carbonbased materials can also directly or indirectly mediate the transformation of refractory organic pollutants. However, the formation mechanisms of EPFRs in carbon materials are not fully understood yet, mainly due to the variety of precursors and the infuence of synthesis conditions.

Hydrothermal carbonization (HTC) is widely employed for producing hydrochar. Compared with pyrochar, hydrochar possesses more oxygen-containing functional groups and exhibits higher aromatization (Xu et  al. 2014). Both HTC processing conditions and the biomass precursors, along with the presence of transition metals in the precursors, signifcantly impact the formation of EPFRs on biochar. Previous research indicated that metal and phenolic compounds increased the concentration of EPFRs in the biochar and altered the type of EPFRs formed (Fang et al. 2015a). Te highest amount of EPFRs was formed when phenol and monophlorophenol were used as the carbon precursors, and $\mathrm { { C u O } }$ was added at concentrations between $1 \%$ and $3 \%$ (Kiruri et al. 2014). Te presence of functional groups and aromatization structure in biomass favors the formation of EPFRs (Pan et  al. 2019; Qin et  al. 2018; Tao et  al. 2022). Terefore, biochar derived from diferent biomasses such as pine needles, wheat straws, and maize straw (Fang et al. 2014) as well as diferent components of biomass like lignin and cellulose (Ruan et  al. 2018), exhibited signifcant diferences in EPFR content. Te content of EPFRs in biochars prepared from algae with diferent habitats also varied due to their infuence on the elemental composition (Huang et al. 2020).

Pomelo peels are common biological wastes and a rich renewable resource (Chen et al. 2020), which makes them one of the most ideal biomass precursors for carbon production. Structurally, the pomelo peel consists of the outer layer, known as the cuticle, which contains ketones, fats, favonoids, lipids, and polysaccharides, as well as the inner layer, referred to as the white fber, which is abundant in cellulose and a small portion of lignin and other polysaccharides (Tocmo et al. 2020). Te cuticle is generally exposed to the environment, resulting in a relatively higher content of mineral elements due to air pollution (Zhuang et  al. 2019). Recent studies have reported the characteristics and application of the biochar derived from pomelo peel in the form of magnetic $\mathrm { M g F e _ { 2 } O _ { 4 } }$ /biochar (Yao et al. 2022) and nitrogen-doped biochar (Wang and Chen 2022).

To the best of our knowledge, no single study has investigated the impact of the various components presented in pomelo peel on the characteristics of EPFRs formed in the corresponding hydrochars, as well as their relevant Fenton-like reactivity towards organic contaminates. Hence, there is a need for a thorough understanding of the mechanisms involved in EPFRs formation and their properties when producing hydrochars from diferent components of pomelo peel, including the incorporation of minerals (e.g., iron).

In this study, pomelo peel (PP) was physically divided into white fbers (WF) and cuticles (PC) and then used as precursors for the production of hydrochars through HTC. Both iron-doped and non-doped hydrochars were prepared. Te properties of the hydrochars were characterized through elemental composition analysis, infrared spectrometry, Boehm  titration, scanning electron microscopy, and electron paramagnetic resonance analysis. Te role of biomass precursor functionality and iron doping in the formation of EPFRs on the hydrochars was examined. Furthermore, the Fenton-like reactivity of the hydrochars containing EPFRs, derived from PP, was explored for the degradation of bisphenol A (BPA). Pollution with polycarbonate plastic has been increasing in recent years, and BPA may leach into the soil when plastic waste is landflled. BPA, as it is an Endocrine disrupting chemical (EDC) compound, is urgent to be eliminated (Cleveland et  al. 2014; Kim et  al. 2015). Terefore, BPA was selected as the target contaminant in this study. Te infuences of storage time on the EPFRs in hydrochars and their Fenton-like reactivity were also investigated. Tis work provides valuable insights into the formation of EPFRs in hydrochars derived from diferent components of PP and their associated properties.

# 2 Materials and methods

# 2.1 Materials

Pomelo peel was collected from the market. Chemicals such as ferric chloride $\left( \mathrm { F e C l } _ { 3 } \right)$ , oxalic acid, ammonium oxalate, ethanol, methanol (HPLC), hydrogen peroxide $\mathrm { ( H _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } ) }$ , sodium hydroxide, sodium bicarbonate, sodium carbonate, hydrogen chloride, phenolphthalein, potassium acid phthalate, tert-Butyl alcohol (TBA), and bisphenol A (BPA) were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China). All reagents used were of chemical reagent grade. Deionized water was used for all pomelo peel treatment and HTC processes.

# 2.2 Hydrochar preparation and characterization

Tree components, PP, PC, and WF were used for hydrochar preparation via HTC at $2 4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 4 \mathrm { h } }$ . Te prepared hydrochar was designated as PPH, PCH, and WFH, respectively. Further details regarding the materials and hydrochar preparation can be found in Text S1. To assess the impact of  iron on the hydrochar properties, $1 0 \ { \mathrm { g } }$ of pomelo peel components (PP, PC, and WF) were mixed with $0 . 5 \ { \tt g }$ of $\mathrm { F e C l } _ { 3 }$ and $5 0 ~ \mathrm { m L }$ of deionized water before HTC. Te hydrochars resulting from this process were denoted as PPH-Fe, PCH-Fe, and WFH-Fe, respectively. Additionally, hydrochars with diferent storage times were represented as PPH-Fe-X $\mathrm { X } { = } 5$ , 9, 18, 45).

Te elemental composition $( \mathrm { C } , \mathrm { H } , \mathrm { N } , \mathrm { S } )$ of the samples was determined using elemental analysis (EA) through an EA3000 (Euro Vector, Italian). Te sample was dried in air at $1 0 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ and then burnt at $6 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ . Te residue obtained is the ash. Te oxygen (O) content was estimated by subtracting the content of other identifed elements and ash from $1 0 0 \%$ . Te Fe content was determined by an inductively coupled plasma emission spectroscopy (ICP-AES, Prodigy of Leeman, USA) after digestion. Te leaching of Fe from hydrochars was detected using the Phenanthroline spectrophotometric method (Feng et  al. 2021). Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectra were acquired using a NICOLET 380 spectrometer (Termo Fisher Scientifc, USA). Te content of three oxygen-containing functional groups (carboxylic, lactonic, and phenolic) was determined by the Boehm titration method (Kang et al. 2012). Te surface morphology of biomass and microstructure of hydrochar were observed using scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM-6700F, Japan). Te signal and content of EPFRs in hydrochar were measured using an electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectroscopy analyzer (Bruker Elexsys 58, USA). Further details regarding EPR spectrum parameters can be found in Text S2. Peak ftting of free radicals’ signal on the hydrochar was performed using PeakFit $\mathbf { v } 4 . 1 2$ (SeaSolve Software Inc.), and the results are presented in Figure S1. Te $\mathbb { R } ^ { 2 }$ values were all above 0.99, indicating a high level of accuracy in the ft. Te radical signals were identifed by EPR using 5,5-dimethyl-1-pyrroline $N \cdot$ -oxide (DMPO) and 2,2,6,6-tetramethylpiperidine (TEMP) as trapping agents. Detailed quantitative methods for measuring $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ and ·OH are described in Text S2.

# 2.3 Fenton‑like reaction and analytical methods

Firstly, $2 5 0 ~ \mathrm { m L }$ of $5 0 ~ \mathrm { m g { \cdot } L ^ { - 1 } }$ BPA, $0 . 2 5 \ \mathrm { g }$ of hydrochar, and $1 \ \mathrm { m L }$ of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ were added into a beaker. As a control experiment, another reaction without the addition of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ was performed under the same conditions to assess the sorption efciency. Te $\mathrm { p H }$ of the reaction system was measured before and during the reaction, as shown in Figure S2a, with an initial $\mathrm { p H }$ of $\sim 7 . 2$ for all reaction systems. Troughout the reaction duration, the $\mathrm { p H }$ decreased to 6.0. No signifcant $\mathsf { p H }$ variation was observed among the diferent systems, ensuring comparability of the results between the diferent hydrochars. As shown in Figure S2b, the adsorption efciency of PPH-Fe at diferent initial pH (6.0–7.0) was similar. Terefore, the impact of initial $\mathsf { p H }$ on the adsorption of BPA by hydrochar can be ignored.

Te reaction was carried out in a water bath at $2 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 6 \mathrm { h } }$ . At regular intervals, $5 \mathrm { m L }$ of the reaction solution was collected as subsamples and fltered through $0 . 2 2 \mu \mathrm { m }$ membranes. To quench the reaction, $1 \ \mathrm { m L }$ ethanol was added to the fltered sample. Te concentration of the residual BPA in the reaction solution was determined using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC, Shimadzu LC-20A). Te mobile phase was methanol/ water $\left( 7 0 { : } 3 0 , \mathbf { v } / \mathbf { v } \right)$ . All of the analytical wavelength was $2 7 8 ~ \mathrm { n m }$ , and the fow rate was $1 \ \mathrm { m L } \cdot \mathrm { m i n } ^ { - 1 }$ . Detection of intermediates was performed using an LC (Agilent Technologies 1260, Santa Clara, CA, USA) coupled with a triple quadrupole mass spectrometer (Agilent Technologies G6400). To assess the efect of $\mathsf { p H }$ of the initial solution on the degradation performance of BPA, the $\mathrm { p H }$ of the

BPA solution was adjusted with $0 . 1 \ \mathrm { m o l { \cdot } L ^ { - 1 } }$ NaOH or $\mathrm { H N O } _ { 3 }$ solution.

To investigate the efect of leached Fe on BPA degradation, the fltrate of $\mathrm { P P H + B P A }$ , after stirring for $^ { 1 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ was collected. Subsequently, $1 \ \mathrm { m L }$ - $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ was added to examine the removal of BPA. Te reaction was carried out in a water bath at $2 5 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ for $^ { 6 \mathrm { h } }$ . Te Fenton efect of ferrous and ferric ions, equivalent to the amount of Fe leached within $^ { 1 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ , on BPA degradation was investigated. All experiments were performed in triplicate under the same experimental conditions.

To investigate the efect of reactive species on BPA degradation, experiments were performed under identical conditions, except for the addition of $4 0 \ \mathrm { \ m m o l {cdot } L ^ { - 1 } }$ TBA, which is commonly used as ·OH scavenger (Yu et  al. 2019). Te concentration of TBA was calculated from BPA-OH radical rate constant $( \mathrm { k B P A } { = } 6 . 9 { \times } 1 0 ^ { 9 } \quad \mathrm { m o l . L ^ { - 1 } } \quad \mathrm { s ^ { - 1 } } )$ ), BPA concentration $( 2 . 2 \times 1 0 ^ { - 4 } ~ \mathrm { m o l { \cdot } L ^ { - 1 } } )$ , and TBA-OH radical reaction rate constant $( \mathrm { k T B A } { = } 5 { \times } 1 0 ^ { 8 } \ \mathrm { \ m o l { \cdot } L ^ { - 1 } } \ s ^ { - 1 } )$ . Tese experiments were also performed in triplicate. Unless otherwise stated, all experiments were performed without control of $\mathrm { p H }$ . Error bars (Standard Deviation, SD, $\mathrm { n } = 3$ ) were provided on all fgures.

Among the raw materials, the PC had the highest C and H content, while the O content was the lowest. A similar trend was observed with PCH when compared to other hydrochars. Tis can be attributed to the signifcant presence of lipids in PC, which were converted into longchain fatty acids during HTC. # 3.2 Surface functional groups

FTIR analysis was performed to examine the functionality of pomelo peel components and the corresponding hydrochars. Te spectra are presented  in Fig.  1. In Fig. 1a, b, the functional groups of the PP and WF, as well as their respective hydrochars are shown. Te wide band observed at $1 1 2 0 { - } 1 0 3 0 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ in the PP and WF  is associated with the methoxy group or ether bond in lignin (Ojha et  al. 2017). Te intensity of this bond decreased greatly in the hydrochars. Te characteristic absorption peak at $1 6 9 5 ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ indicates the presence of carbonyl groups, suggesting the formation of carbonyl groups in raw cellulose materials after HTC. Tese carbonyl groups may be re-formed by dehydration of a hydroxyl group in the HTC precursors (Yu et al. 2019). Te band at around $3 2 2 8 ~ \mathrm { c m ^ { - 1 } }$ in PP and WF is related to the hydroxyl groups, predominantly phenolic hydroxyl and carboxyl groups (Dutta et  al. 2015). Te content of hydroxyl groups in the hydrochars was greatly reduced compared to the raw materials, indicating dehydration and decarboxylation reactions during HTC. Te band at around $2 9 1 \dot { 6 } ~ \mathrm { c m } ^ { - 1 }$ corresponds to the asymmetric stretching vibration peak of methyl, methylene, and methylidyne in the PP and WF. Tis band slightly decreased after HTC, indicating enhanced aromaticity, which is consistent with the decreased $\mathrm { H } / \mathrm { C }$ ratio as observed in Table 1. Te band at $8 7 0 ~ \mathrm { c m ^ { - 1 } }$ observed with the PP and WF is associated with the $\beta$ -glycoside bond (Yongbin et al. 2008), and this signal persisted in the corresponding hydrochars, indicating that a small portion of cellulose remained in hydrochars.

Te morphology of the raw materials and hydrochars, with and without iron doping, was determined using SEM, and the corresponding images are presented in  Fig.  2. In Fig.  2a, b, the surface morphology of PC exhibited a honeycomb-like net structure. PCH-Fe showed a fufy cotton-like structure. # 3.4 Efects of biomass components on EPFRs

The spin-value (content) and $\mathbf { g }$ -factor of EPFRs in hydrochars were determined using EPR  and analyzed using PeakFit v4.12 (SeaSolve Software Inc.), with results presented in Figure S1 and Table 2. According to the previous studies, three types of EPFRs have been identified: carbon-centered radicals $( \mathbf { g } 1 < 2 . 0 0 3 0 )$ , carbon-centered radicals with an adjacent oxygen atom ( $2 . 0 0 3 0 < \mathrm { g } 2 < 2 . 0 0 4 0 )$ , and oxygen-centered radicals $( \mathbf { g } 3 > 2 . 0 0 4 0 )$ (Jia et  al. 2017). In  Table  2, the content of EPFRs on PPH-Fe and WFH-Fe $( 9 . 1 1 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 8 }$ and $8 . 2 5 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 8 }$ spins $\mathbf { g } ^ { - 1 } .$ ) was higher than that on PPH and WFH $( 3 . 3 3 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 8 }$ and $2 . 9 6 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 8 }$ spins $\mathbf { g } ^ { - 1 }$ ). Transition metals are necessary for the formation of EPFRs as they accept electrons from aromatic compounds (Fang et  al. 2014). The content of $\mathbf { g } 3$ type of EPFRs in PPH-Fe and WFH-Fe $( 4 . 9 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 8 }$ and $4 . 2 0 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 8 }$ spins $\mathbf { g } ^ { - 1 }$ ) was higher than that in PPH and WFH (0 and 0 spins $\mathbf { g } ^ { - 1 }$ ). This indicates that $\mathrm { F e ( I I I ) }$ promotes the formation of EPFRs, particularly oxygen-centered radicals. As indicated by previous studies, Fe(III) enhanced dehydration and decarboxylation, increasing the content of oxygen-containing functional groups, which could subsequently form EPFRs with the chelated ferric ions (Ruan et  al. 2019; Sun et  al. 2014; Thompson et al. 2015). However, the content of EPFRs on PCH-Fe $( 2 . 7 8 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 8 }$ spins $\mathbf { g } ^ { - 1 }$ ) was lower than that on PCH $( 7 . 9 5 \times 1 0 ^ { 1 8 }$ spins· $\mathbf { g } ^ { - 1 }$ ). Compared to WF, PC is the outer layer of pomelo peel and is more exposed to the environment and, as a result, more susceptible to heavy metal pollution of the atmosphere (Zhuang et al. 2019). PC endogenously has the highest content of Fe $( 0 . 0 6 6 \% )$ , Mn $( 0 . 1 3 0 \text{‰}$ , Cu $( 0 . 0 3 7 \text{‰}$ , and other transition metals among three biomass feedstocks (Table S1), which may contribute to EPFRs formation.

# 3.5 Possible formation mechanisms of EPFRs

Te proposed formation mechanisms of EPFRs on different hydrochars are shown in Scheme  1. During the HTC process white fber, cellulose, and lignin were transformed into phenolic compounds such as hydroxybenzene. In the cuticle, condensed tannins and proanthocyanidins (Mattonai et  al. 2017) decompose into 1,3,5-hydroxybenzene and other phenolic compounds containing multiple hydroxyl groups, while lipid components decompose into fatty acids. Tese phenolic compounds adsorb Fe(III) through the elimination of water or hydrogen chloride, followed by the transfer of single electrons from substituted aromatics to Fe(III), resulting in the reduction of Fe(III) and the formation of EPFRs (Ruan et al. 2019). When an excess amount of transition metal is added, EPFRs could be consumed through further reduction of Fe(III) (Wang et al. 2022).

# 3.6 Adsorption and Fenton‑like reaction

Te Fenton-like reaction was employed to investigate the reactivity of hydrochar in activating $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ for the degradation of BPA, and the results are shown in Fig.  3. Te Fenton-like performances of PPH-Fe hydrochars prepared at diferent temperatures (200, 240, 260, 280, and $3 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C } )$ and with diferent iron additions $( 1 \% , 3 \%$ , $5 \%$ $1 0 \% )$ are compared in Fig. 3a, b. Te PPH-Fe prepared at $2 4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ with $5 \%$ iron addition exhibited the highest Fenton-like reactivity, resulting in the maximum efciency of BPA degradation. Terefore, PPH-Fe-240 $^ \circ \mathrm { C }$ was chosen for further investigation. Te BPA removal rate by PPH-Fe- $2 0 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ was $\sim 4 0 \%$ . However, when the HTC temperature was further increased beyond $2 4 0 ~ ^ { \circ } \mathrm { C }$ , the oxygen-containing functional groups, such as phenolic hydroxyl group, on the surface of PPH-Fe decreased, resulting in a reduction in EPFRs content, which was insufcient for fully activating $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ (Ruan et al. 2018).

With the increase in Fe loading, the BPA removal efciency initially increased and reached a peak at $5 \%$ before declining. Iron loadings of $1 \%$ and $3 \%$ were insufcient for the formation of EPFRs in PPH, while $1 0 \%$ iron loading was excessive, as discussed in Sect. 3.2. A similar phenomenon has been observed in our previous work (Ruan et al. 2018).

Te adsorption and Fenton-like performances of hydrochars derived from PC, WF, and PP were compared, and the results are shown in Fig.  3c–f. Te efciency of the Fenton-like reactions was much higher than that of the sorption processes. As shown in Fig.  3c, d, WFH and PPH-Fe were the most efective hydrochars for BPA adsorption, with a maximum efciency of only about $4 0 \%$ . Other hydrochars adsorbed BPA at less than $1 0 \%$ Figure  3e depicts the BPA removal efciency through the Fenton-like reaction, where the use of PCH showed the highest BPA removal efciency compared with other hydrochars, which is positively correlated with the intensity of EPFRs as shown in Table  2. A similar trend was also observed with the iron-doped hydrochars as shown in Fig.  3f. PPH-Fe and WFH-Fe remarkably increased the efciency of the Fenton-like reaction compared to PPH and WFH. Te BPA removal efciency in the Fenton-like reaction of PPH-Fe and WFH-Fe reached $\sim 9 9 \%$ within $^ { 3 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ , which was competitive with other materials, while the removal efciency of the BPA Fenton-like reaction for PPH and WFH was only about $1 6 . 3 \%$ and $5 9 \%$ , respectively. Based on Table 2 and Figure S3, PPH-Fe and WFH-Fe contained more EPFRs than PPH and WFH, especially oxygen-centered radicals, which were positively correlated with BPA degradation, thereby enhancing the Fenton-like reaction and achieving a higher BPA removal rate. On the contrary, the BPA removal efciency in the Fenton-like reaction of PCH (about $9 9 \%$ in $^ \textrm { \scriptsize 1 h }$ ) was better than that of PCH-Fe $( 5 0 \% )$ . Excessive Fe(III) hindered the formation of EPFRs on PPH-Fe, which was unfavorable for the Fenton-like reaction. Terefore, the BPA removal efciency in the Fenton-like reaction is dependent on the content of EPFRs.

Te efect of $\mathsf { p H }$ of the initial solution on the removal of BPA was investigated, and the results are shown in Figure S2c. As the initial $\mathsf { p H }$ decreased, the degradation of BPA in hydrochar $/ \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ system became more and more efective. BPA was efectively degraded under acidic and neutral conditions. Te BPA removal rate dropped dramatically when the $\mathrm { p H }$ of the initial solution was 11 and only about $1 0 \%$ of the BPA was removed. Tis might be because the high pH may lead to the formation of ferric hydroxide complexes, which would prevent the production of ·OH (Yi et al. 2021). Te hydrochar $/ \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ system system also showed good degradation of methyl orange and oxytetracycline, which proved the wide application of this system (Figure S9).

# 3.8 Possible activation mechanisms of ${ \sf H } _ { 2 } { \sf O } _ { 2 }$ 3.8.1 Role of leached Fe in the Fenton‑like reaction

To elucidate the role of leached Fe in the Fenton-like reaction, the Fe released to the aqueous solution from PPH-Fe was analyzed by using the Phenanthroline spectrophotometric method, and the $\mathrm { p H }$ was also measured as it impacts the leaching of Fe into the aqueous solution (Fig. 5a). Some of the iron ions present in the hydrochar would be released into the solution as the $\mathrm { p H }$ gradually decreases during the Fenton-like reaction. In the PPH$\mathrm { F e / H _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } }$ system, the highest degradation of BPA was observed within $^ { 1 \mathrm { h } }$ . Terefore, the suspension of PPH-Fe after $^ { 1 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ , the addition of $\mathrm { F e ( I I I ) }$ , and $\mathrm { F e ( I I ) }$ $( 0 . 2 5 ~ \mathrm { m g { \cdot } L ^ { - 1 } }$ according to Fig.  5a) were examined for their impact on the removal of BPA (Fig.  5b). It was found that the leached Fe contributed little to the activation of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ since the fltered suspension of PPH-Fe, the addition of Fe(III), and $\mathrm { F e ( I I ) }$ only resulted in a small amount of BPA removal in the presence of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ at $\mathrm { p H } ~ 6 { - } 7$ . Tis was further supported by the results shown in Fig.  5c, d, where the Fe content in the aqueous solution of PPHFe decreased signifcantly after multiple washing cycles. However, this reduction did not inhibit the removal of BPA. Terefore, it is concluded that the role of leached Fe in the Fenton-like reaction is limited. Some articles difer in their view that iron ions on biochar play a major role in the Fenton-like reaction, while EPFRs play a secondary role (Yi et al. 2021). Te reason is that pyrochar has a higher Fe content than the hydrochar used in this study.

3.8.2 Reactive species generated in hydrochar $H _ { 2 } O _ { 2 }$ system To identify reactive species (ROS) generated in the Fenton-like reaction system, TBA was utilized as scavengers for ·OH, with a  reaction rate constant of $6 { \times } 1 0 ^ { 8 } \ \mathrm { \ m o l { \cdot } L ^ { - 1 } } \ s ^ { - 1 }$ (Rong et  al. 2019). Te removal efciency of BPA was found to be $9 9 \%$ without the addition of a scavenger, whereas the removal efciency of BPA was signifcantly reduced in the presence of TBA in the reaction systems, as depicted in Fig. 6a. Tis observation suggests that ·OH was present in the system and played a major role in the degradation of BPA. Te decomposition of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ and the accumulation of ·OH in the system were quantifed, as depicted in Figure S5a, c. Te ftting plot of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ decomposition and ·OH accumulation versus BPA degradation are presented in Figure S5b, d. Te high level of correlation observed between the degradation of BPA and the decomposition of $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ,$ as well as the production of ·OH, suggests that these factors are closely interrelated.

To further confrm the existence of other reactive species in the reaction system, EPR analysis using DMPO and TEMP as the trapping agent was performed. A typical DMPO-·OH signal was identifed in the Fenton-like reaction system. Importantly, the EPR signal of $\mathrm { D M P O + W F H - F e + H _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } }$ was observed to be signifcantly stronger than that of $\mathrm { D M P O + H _ { 2 } O _ { 2 } } ,$ confrming the necessity of hydrochar in this process (Fig. 6b). Some studies have reported diferent shapes of DMPO$\mathrm { \Omega } \mathrm { O _ { 2 } } ^ { - }$ signals, presenting as four peaks with a 1:1:1:1 intensity ratio (Li et al. 2022). # 4 Conclusions

In conclusion, among all hydrochars, those containing higher levels of persistent radicals exhibited superior Fenton-like activity. Te biomass precursors and the additive amount of Fe(III) are two crucial factors infuencing the presence, content, and type of EPFRs. To favor EPFR formation, the quantity of Fe(III) and aromatic compounds in the biomass should be matched. EPFRs on hydrochar activate $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ via electron transfer, generating ·OH and $^ 1 \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } ,$ leading to BPA degradation. Among the three types of EPFRs, oxygen-centered radicals showed the highest Fenton-like reaction activity. EPFRs on the hydrochars gradually decreased over time, especially for the oxygen-centered free radicals. EPFRs on the external surface of hydrochars were easily consumed, while those on the internal surface persisted during long storage. Tus, PPH-Fe-45 still exhibited high Fenton-like reactivity due to the gradual release of embedded EPFRs. Tis study provides new insights into EPFRs formation and consumption on hydrochar.
