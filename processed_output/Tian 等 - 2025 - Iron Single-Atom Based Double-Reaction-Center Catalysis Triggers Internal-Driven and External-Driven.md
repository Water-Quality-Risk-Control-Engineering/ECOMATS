# Iron Single-Atom Based Double-Reaction-Center Catalysis Triggers Internal-Driven and External-Driven Pathways for Green Fenton-Like Chemistry

Qingbai Tian, Jiale Chang, Xiaoming Peng, Longlong Geng, Baoyu Gao, Qian Li, Yue Gao, and Xing Xu\*

Abstract: Double-reaction-centers (DRCs) Fenton-like chemistry with low or zero oxidant addition has garnered increasing attentions due to their alignment with the principles of green and sustainable development. However, the regulation of such processes remains a significant challenge, primarily due to deficiencies in the microscopic interpretation of their electron migration mechanisms operating with low or zero oxidant addition. In this work, iron single-atom DRCs catalyst (Fe/N-SAC) was prepared for internal-driven system (zero oxidant addition) and external-driven system (low peroxymonosulfate [PMS] addition). Results indicated the absence of dissolved oxygen activation in the PMS-zreo $\mathrm { F e / N _ { \mathrm { - } } }$ SAC system, and the iron single atoms in the Fe/N-SAC acted as the predominate electron acceptors to extract the electrons from the electron-donating pollutants with iron valence decreasing from $+ 2 . 3 7$ to $+ 2 . 0 7$ and they could also be recovered under $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ atmosphere. In contrast, the electrons from the pollutants could be transferred to both PMS and iron atoms in the external-driven Fe/N-SAC/PMS system involving both predominant electron transfer process (ETP) and iron internal-driven. Furthermore, two experimental devices based on core mechanisms of internal-driven and external-driven systems were designed to achieve long-term operation. These studies will complement the core catalytic mechanisms and module applications of internal-driven and external-driven DRCs systems.

# Introduction

By constructing dual-reaction-centers (DRCs) comprising electron-rich and electron-deficient microregions on the surface of heterogeneous catalysts, the interfacial electron transfer in Fenton-like reactions can be precisely controlled.[19] This approach effectively addresses the rate-limiting steps in traditional Fenton reactions, optimizing the utilization of pollutant energy and achieving both low energy consumption and high efficiency[1-5] The core mechanism of DRCs catalysis for the Fenton-like system is primarily based on: i) organic pollutants as an electron donor interacting with the electron-deficient centers on the catalyst surface through the $\pi - \pi$ structure and transferring electrons[1,3,10]; ii) Electrons transferring to the electron-rich centers via the $_ { \mathrm { C - O - M } }$ (metal species) bridge on the catalyst surface, thereby activating the adsorbed oxidants (hydrogen peroxide $\left( \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } _ { 2 } \right)$ , peroxymonosulfate (PMs)) and dissolved oxygen in electron-rich centers to generate reactive oxygen species (ROS).[1,3,4,10,11]

Recent studies have further demonstrated that by reinforcing the micro-electric field on the surface of the DRCs catalysts, the internal energy of sewage constituents such as pollutants, water, and oxygen can be effectively utilized, and zero chemical addition and zero external energy assistance can be achieved in the water treatment process (It is often masked by adsorption).[1,1114] This significantly reduces the cost of the catalytic system and resolves one of the core bottlenecks of the traditional Fenton system. This also poses a new challenge to the catalytic mechanisms and pathways of the DRCs-based catalytic systems. Hu et al. proposed that salinity-mediated DRCs surface could induce the distortion of the bond network of water molecules, thereby strengthening the water self-purification process.[1,2,1] It has also been reported that dissolved oxygen, as the main electron acceptor, realized the efficient degradation of pollutants in the zeroagent addition system.[15] Nevertheless, Lu et al. reported that the DRCs system could achieve over $9 5 \%$ pollutant removal under ${ \bf N } _ { 2 }$ atmosphere without oxidant addition.[13] This observation prompts the consideration of alternative oxidation mechanisms in the zero-oxidant system, beyond the role of dissolved oxygen in electron-rich regions.

To solve the mystery of this core mechanism, we prepared an iron single-atom DRCs catalyst (Fe/N-SAC) and applied in internal-driven system (zero-oxidant addition) and external-driven system (low-oxidant (PMS) addition). The metal single-atom catalyst (SAC) can disperse the metalbased electron-rich center more effectively,[16-18] which is conducive to enhancing the catalytic performance of the DRCs catalyst. One may be concerned about the difference between the removal of contaminants by an internal-driven Fe/N-SAC system and the adsorption process, which was also distinguished by the analysis of degradation intermediates, the change in iron value of $\mathrm { F e / N  – S A C }$ ,and electrochemical measurements. Our results indicated that the role of dissolved oxygen in the internal-driven system with PMS-zero addition was nearly negligible. In contrast, the iron single atom in the Fe/N-SAC would absorb the electrons from the pollutants with iron valence of the iron single atom decreasing from $+ 2 . 3 7$ to $+ 2 . 0 7$ in the internal-driven DRCs system (they could also be recovered under $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ atmosphere), and the electron transfer capacity was closely related to the electrondonating ability of pollutants. Conversely, the catalytic mechanism in the low-PMS-driven DRCs system was based on the complex pathways. As a matter of fact, previous numerous studies on the adsorption (or considered to be adsorption) of pollutants by the similar catalysts do not rule out the existence of such an internal-driven mechanism because of the absence of product analysis and metal valence analysis. Therefore, this work provides a new research idea for the future study on the pathways of adsorption/catalysis of pollutants by such DRCs-based catalysts.

# Catalytic Performances of Internal-Driven and External-Driven Systems

Internal-driven Fe/N-SAC and external-driven Fe/NSAC/PMS systems exhibited significantly difference in the PCM removal. The removal of PCM by the PMS-zero Fe/N-SAC $\left( 0 . 1 \ { \ g } / \mathrm { L } \right)$ was only $4 5 . 9 \%$ with the $K _ { \mathrm { o b s } }$ of 0.0416 $\operatorname* { m i n } ^ { - 1 }$ , and it was significantly increased to $0 . 3 4 5 ~ \mathrm { \ m i n ^ { - 1 } }$ at Fe/N-SAC usage of $0 . 5 \ \mathrm { ~ g ~ L ^ { - 1 } }$ (Figures 2a and S13). Conversely, external-driven Fe/N-SAC/PMS system could attain the similar degradation performance $( 0 . \ 4 0 1 \ \mathrm { \ m i n ^ { - 1 } }$ using very low Fe/N-SAC dosage $( 0 . 1 \ \mathrm { g } \ L ^ { - 1 } )$ with PMS usage of $0 . 2 5 \mathrm { \ m M }$ , and the catalytic performances towards PCM could be further enhanced with increasing Fe/N-SAC dosage (Figures 2b, S14, and S15). In addition, PCM removal by Fe/N-SAC in both PMS-zero and PMS-addition conditions were significantly higher than those of other catalysts, such as $\mathrm { F e } ( 0 )$ , Fe $\mathrm { N P s / N - C }$ , $\mathrm { F e } _ { 3 } \mathrm { O } _ { 4 }$ , N-C (Figure 2c). Although numerous previous studies have attributed the high removal efficiency under PMS-zero conditions to the adsorption process,[19-21] both N-C and Fe $\mathrm { N P s / N \mathrm { - } C }$ with similar carbon frameworks demonstrated very low PCM removal at high catalyst dosage (Figure S16). This suggested a non-adsorption mechanism with internal-driven process probably involved in the PMS-free Fe/N-SAC system mentioned subsequently.

Stability tests indicated that the Fe/N-SAC could keep stable in both internal-driven and external-driven systems (Figures 2d and S17), and the nitrogen/carbon composition of the Fe/N-SAC were also unchanged after used in the two systems (Figures S18, S19 and Table S5). The degradation of PCM could also keep stable in different $\mathrm { p H }$ conditions (Figures 2e and S20). Furthermore, PCM removal with PMSzero system was significantly enhanced in the presence of co-existing humic acid (HA) and anions (Figures 2f), S21, and S22 which contrasted with the inhibition of adsorption observed in the presence of these substances.[2,23] This is a typical DRCs-based phenomenon, that is, salinity mediates the distortion of the bond network of water molecules on the surface of DRCs-based catalysts, thus enhancing the water self-purification process.[1,12] As a result, the removal of PCM by the PMS-free Fe/N-SAC was most likely mediated through the internal-driven pathway, as detailed in subsequent sections. In contrast, the removal of PCM by the Fe/N-SAC/PMS system was adversely impacted by the presence of co-existing anions and HA, particularly as their concentrations rose (Figures S21 and S22), owing to the interference of these substances exerting on the PMS activation process.[18,24,25] In addition, comparison of normalized $K _ { \mathrm { o b s } }$ of PCM indicated that both PMS-zero-driven and low-PMS-driven systems could achieve the superior PCM removal performances than most reported metal or metal-free catalytic systems (Figure $2 \mathrm { g }$ and Table S6).[26,27]

Removal of various pollutants by both PMS-free Fe/NSAC and Fe/N-SAC/PMS systems demonstrated high selectivity, characterized by three distinct rate gradients (Figures 2h and S23). Typical electron-donating pollutants (i.e., PCM, and BPA) can be completely removed by the PMS-free $\mathrm { F e / N } .$ SAC $( 0 . 5 \mathrm { ~ g ~ L ^ { - 1 } } )$ system and Fe/N-SAC $( 0 . 2 \mathrm { ~ g ~ L ~ } ^ { - 1 }$ )/PMS $( 0 . 1 ~ \mathrm { \ m M } )$ system within $1 5 ~ \mathrm { \ m i n }$ . Conversely, electronwithdrawing pollutants, such as MND and NBA were difficult to be oxidized in both Fe/N-SAC system and Fe/N-SAC/PMS system. The removal performances of these pollutants in both PMS-zero-driven and low-PMS-driven systems could be further quantitatively evaluated using a series of parameters derived from the structural characteristics of the pollutants, including electrophilicity index, nucleophilicity index, and half-wave potential $\left( \varphi _ { 1 / 2 } \right)$ values (Table S7). Pollutants with different electrophilicity/nucleophilicity indexes could be divided into three regions (electron-accepting region, transition region and electron-donating region) according to their group characteristics (Figures 2h and S24).[2830] Quantitative structure-activity relationship (QSAR) analysis exhibited that the electrophilicity index and nucleophilicity index were strongly correlated with the $\mathrm { l n } K _ { \mathrm { o b s } }$ data in both systems, with $R ^ { 2 }$ values ranging from 0.89 to 0.99 (Figure 2i1). These findings suggested a significant correlation between the electron-donating properties of pollutants and their degradation characteristics in both internal-driven Fe/NSAC system and external-driven Fe/N-SAC/PMS system. The $\varphi _ { 1 / 2 }$ value is an index of the redox potentials of various pollutants.[29-31] The $\underline { { \varphi } } _ { 1 / 2 }$ values of different pollutants with three regions (low $\varphi _ { 1 / 2 }$ region, moderate $\varphi _ { 1 / 2 }$ region, and high $\varphi _ { 1 / 2 }$ region) represented their redox characteristics, which also corresponded with their degradation performances in above resulting systems (Figure S25). Although a superior correlation with the $\mathrm { l n } K _ { \mathrm { o b s } }$ data $R ^ { 2 } = 0 . 9 8$ can be observed, they can only be used for the pollutants with $\varphi _ { 1 / 2 }$ values lower than $1 . 2 \mathrm { V }$ (Figures S25-S27).

# Catalytic Mechanisms of Internal-Driven and External-Driven Systems

After introducing ${ \bf N } _ { 2 }$ into the degradation system, unlike some previous setups where dissolved oxygen served as an electron acceptor for pollutant degradation,13,32] there was no significant decrease in PCM removal in the Fe/N-SAC system and Fe/N-SAC/PMS system (Figure 3a and b), suggesting that the impact of dissolved oxygen on PCM degradation was minimal. As a result, the electron acceptors for both Fe/N-SAC system and Fe/N-SAC/PMS system (especially the internaldriven Fe/N-SAC system with typical DRCs characteristics) should be well clarified. The role of singlet oxygen $\left( ^ { 1 } \mathbf { O } _ { 2 } \right)$ in the PCM degradation was assessed using the $\mathbf { D } _ { 2 } \mathbf { O }$ as probe considering that the life time of ${ } ^ { 1 } { \bf O } _ { 2 }$ in $\mathbf { D } _ { 2 } \mathbf { O }$ was an order of magnitude (50 vs. $4 ~ \mu \mathrm { s } \dot$ higher than that in $\mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } .$ [33,34] The nonradical ${ } ^ { 1 } { \bf O } _ { 2 }$ pathway could be excluded in both internaldriven and external-driven systems due to the similar $K _ { \mathrm { o b s } }$ values in $\mathbf { D } _ { 2 } \mathbf { O }$ and $_ { \mathrm { H } _ { 2 } \mathrm { O } }$ (Figure 3a and b). Quenching tests using $1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { m M }$ of EtOH and TBA demonstrated a negligible inhibitory effect in the internal-driven Fe/N-SAC system (Figures 3c and S28). In contrast, a slight quenching effect was observed in the external-driven Fe/N-SAC/PMS system, with approximately $1 3 \%$ of radical oxidation contributing to the PCM degradation (Figure 3d). These results were also confirmed by the existence of OH'-signals in the EPR of Fe/NSAC/PMS system (Figure S29). Furthermore, the EPR signals of $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } \mathrm { ^ { * - } }$ were absent in both Fe/N-SAC and Fe/N-SAC/PMS system (Figure S30). Since the $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 } \mathrm { ^ { * } }$ was always based on the DRCs reaction derived from the dissolved oxygen[1,35]; this result also confirmed the absence of dissolved oxygen activation for PCM degradation.

To identify the role of iron single atoms in both Fe/N-SAC system and Fe/N-SAC/PMS system for PCM degradation, XAFS analysis of used Fe/N-SAC in PMS-free and PMSaddition systems was assessed. The configurations of iron single atom after used in both Fe/N-SAC and Fe/N-SAC/PMS systems were unaffected (Figures S31 and S32), while the iron valence of used Fe/N-SAC was decreased compared to that of pristine Fe/N-SAC (Figures $3 \mathrm { e - g }$ and S33). A more significant decline in iron valence from $+ 2 . 3 7$ to $+ 2 . 0 7$ was observed in the used Fe/N-SAC (PMS-free) compared to $+ 2 . 2 0$ in the used $\mathrm { F e / N { \mathrm { - } } S A C }$ (with PMS), as shown in Figure 3f and g. These results indicated that the iron single atoms would be used as the electron acceptors to receive electron from PCM with $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 3 + }$ reduction to $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + }$ , and the internal-driven $\mathrm { F e / N { \cdot } S A C }$ system almost exclusively utilized iron single atoms as the electron acceptors (dissolved oxygen as electron acceptor has been excluded). As a result, the iron single atoms in internal-driven system were reduced to a lower valence. Furthermore, the iron valence of regenerated Fe/N-SAC under $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ atmosphere could be recovered from 2.07 to 2.29, which indicated that the iron single atom could also be regenerated by $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ for continuous cycles (Figure 3e-g).

In addition, the internal-driven Fe/N-SAC system excluded high-valent iron-oxo due to the absence of trans-stilbene oxidation (Figure 3h),[16,3,36] while the external-driven Fe/N-SAC/PMS system exhibited trace peaks of trans-stilbene oxide, representing the presence of weak high-valent iron-oxo species, although the role of high-valent iron-oxo could be negligible (Figure 3h). The results showed that there were two opposite trends for iron atoms in external-driven Fe/N-SAC/PMS system: PMS would trigger the iron atoms to form a small amounts of high-valent iron-oxo species,[36,37] while electrons were also donated from PCM for iron atoms reduction. Conversely, only one trend (iron atoms reduction) was observed in the internaldriven Fe/N-SAC system (Figure 3i). Notably, few reported DRCs systems have considered the valence decline of metals induced by electron transfer from pollutants in zero-oxidant systems, which might be essential to the catalytic reaction if the role of dissolved oxygen was nondominant.

# DFT Analysis of the Fe/N-SAC as Well as the Electron Transfer Pathways

The coordination structure of Fe/N-SAC was further verified by DFT calculation, and the results were displayed in Figure 5a. The graphitized cavity carbon carriers were considered as an ideal carrier for immobilizing the metal single atom as they could offer numerous cavities with a high density of $\mathbf { N }$ atoms for coordinating the metal atoms. The formation energies of the three coordination configurations of $\mathrm { F e - N _ { 4 } }$ were calculated as $- 1 6 . 0 8 \ \mathrm { e V }$ for $2 \mathrm { F e } { \mathrm { - N } } _ { 4 }$ $- 1 0 . 4 3 \mathrm { e V }$ for $\mathrm { F e } _ { 2 } { \ - } { \bf N } _ { 6 }$ , and $- 9 . 3 5 \ \mathrm { ~ e V }$ for $\mathrm { F e - N _ { 4 } }$ , respectively (Figure 5a). The appropriate formation energy outcomes indicated the universality and stability of the three Fe $\mathbf { \cdot N _ { 4 } }$ configurations existed in graphitized cavity carbon carriers. The electron localization function (ELF) of three coordination configurations $\mathrm { ( 2 F e - N _ { 4 } }$ , $\mathrm { F e } _ { 2 } { \ - } { \bf N } _ { 6 }$ , and $\mathrm { F e - N _ { 4 } }$ was calculated, and the lower ELF value represented the greater electron localization tendency (Figure 5b). The results indicated that the electron distribution of $2 \mathrm { F e } { \mathrm { - N } } _ { 4 }$ , $\mathrm { F e } _ { 2 } { \cdot } \mathrm { N } _ { 6 }$ , and $\mathrm { F e - N _ { 4 } }$ configurations could be significantly altered by doping iron atoms, and the electron delocalization was greatly enhanced around iron atoms. The work function data of Fe$\mathrm { N } _ { 4 }$ in different configurations were reduced from $4 . 2 6 \ \mathrm { e V }$ to $4 . 1 3 { \mathrm { - } } 4 . 2 2 { \mathrm { ~ e V } }$ by comparison with the iron-free graphitized carbon substrate (Figures 5c and S38), indicating that electron transfer was more likely to occur within the three $\mathrm { F e - N _ { 4 } }$ configurations.[41,42] The binding of PMS onto different Fe$\mathrm { N } _ { 4 }$ configurations exhibited different adsorption energies $\left( E _ { \mathrm { a d s } } \right)$ and binding lengths although all configurations were inclined to binding with the PMS molecule (Figure S39). PMS binding with $\mathrm { F e } _ { 2 } { \ - } { \bf N } _ { 6 }$ configuration showed that its $l _ { \mathrm { F e - O } }$ was $1 . 9 4 \mathrm { ~ \AA ~ }$ , which was lower than those (2.002.01 Å) of $\mathrm { P M S } + 2 \mathrm { F e } { \mathrm { - } } \mathrm { N } _ { 4 }$ and $\mathrm { P M S } + \mathrm { F e } { \cdot } \mathrm { N } _ { 4 }$ configurations. # Catalytic Devices for Internal-Driven and External-Driven Systems

Two catalytic devices have been designed for the internaldriven Fe/N-SAC and external-driven Fe/N-SAC/PMS systems according to their essential catalytic pathways. The core feature of the internal-driven system lies in its ability to conduct electrons from pollutants to the catalyst without the need for an external oxidant, relying instead on single-atom iron as the electron acceptor. Therefore, the device design for internal-driven system can utilize an adsorption column style, in which Fe/N-SAC was immobilized on felt and then secured within a packed column to achieve continuous contaminant degradation without the addition of PMS (Figure 6a). In contrast, the external-driven system primarily focuses on oxidation processes mediated by PMS, which facilitates pollutant degradation through ETP pathway (Fe/N-SAC-based internal-driven mechanism was also involved). To prevent sulfate pollution from PMS in the water body, PMS can be placed in four separate chambers isolated from the main treatment solution, allowing pollutant degradation in the water body via the ETP facilitated by a salt bridge (Figure 6a).

A physical image of the internal-driven-based column with loaded $\mathrm { F e / N { \mathrm { - } } S A C }$ was shown in Figure 6b. The $\mathrm { F e / N } .$ SAC loaded carbon felt was packed with $1 8 \ \mathrm { c m }$ in height and $4 \ \mathrm { c m }$ in diameter. The SEM image showed that the felt consists of densely packed carbon structures resembling fiber bundles, with Fe/N-SAC catalysts uniformly distributing across its surface (Figures 6c and S63). The Fe/N-SAC laden felt maintained nearly $1 0 0 \%$ of PM removal efficiency for over $^ { 3 6 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ , whereas the bare felt exhibited significantly lower PCM removal (Figure 6d). Moreover, in two actual scenarios involving waste water treatment plant (WWTP)

effluents (Table S10), the continuous internal-driven Fe/NSAC system achieved satisfactory performance for PCM removal (Figure 6e). As for the external-driven Fe/NSAC/PMS system, a $^ \textrm { \scriptsize 3 L }$ of amplifying ETP reactor with eight electrodes connecting four PMS chambers was designed (Figures 6f and $\mathbf { g }$ and S64), which could avoid the sulfate into the treated water. Contact angle experiments showed that the Fe/N-SAC laden electrode has superior hydrophilicity compared to the bare electrode (Figure 6h), which would facilitate the interaction between the laden Fe/N-SAC and pollutants/PMS. Results showed that PCM could not be removed by the bare electrode in the amplifying reactor (Figure S65), while more than $5 0 \%$ and $9 6 \%$ of PCM could be oxidized within $^ { 1 2 \mathrm { ~ h ~ } }$ and $4 8 \mathrm { ~ h ~ }$ via ETP pathway by the amplifying ETP reactor (Figure 6i). The removal of PCM could also be improved by increasing the electrode numbers (Figure S66). In addition, only $4 0 \%$ of PMS was left in the PMS containing chambers due to the consumption of PMS as electron acceptors. What's more, PCM $( 2 0 0 ~ \mu \varrho / \mathrm { L } )$ in actual WWTP effluents could be almost completely removed by the amplifying ETP reactor within $5 4 \mathrm { ~ h ~ }$ without the pollution of sulfate in conventional PMS-activation systems (Figure 6j).

Life cycle assessment (LCA) is an indispensable tool for evaluating environmental trade-offs, enabling a comprehensive understanding of the environmental impacts associated with a product or process across its entire life cycle or specific stages thereof.[48-50] The environmental significance and feasibility of both internal-driven Fe/N-SAC and external-driven Fe/N-SAC/PMS systems can be further quantitatively compared with the benchmark homogeneous $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { P M S }$ system and heterogeneous $\mathrm { F e _ { 3 } O _ { 4 } / P M S }$ system via the LCA, so as to assess the superior sustainable technology compared to the alternative or comparable equivalent options.[51,52] The input materials and energy required to treat 1 ton of PCM wastewater through different systems were given in Table S11. All detailed LCA analysis data were presented in Table S12S19. Based on the impact scores of various descriptors in LCA, it is evident that both the internal-driven Fe/N-SAC and external-driven Fe/N-SAC/PMS systems demonstrated superior environmental friendliness compared to conventional homogeneous $\mathrm { F e } ^ { 2 + } / \mathrm { P M S }$ system and heterogeneous $\mathrm { F e _ { 3 } O _ { 4 } / P M S }$ system (Figure 6k). This is due to the lower catalyst and PMS usage requirements of both systems, as well as the lower energy consumption required. However, the internal-driven Fe/N-SAC exhibited the higher impact scores for almost all descriptors. This was due to the 2.5-fold of Fe/NSAC usage in the internal-driven Fe/N-SAC system compared to the external-driven Fe/N-SAC/PMS system. For future research, the catalyst usage as well as the materials for catalyst preparation in internal-driven system should be reduced so as to make it more environmental significance and feasibility.

# Conclusion

DRCs-based Fenton-like systems with low or zero oxidant addition have increasingly attracted scholars' attention due to their alignment with the green and sustainable development trend. However, i) there are shortcomings in the microscopic interpretation of the electron migration mechanism of DRCs oxidation systems with low or zero oxidant addition; and ii) in many similar catalyst systems, the degradation mechanism based on zero oxidant system is often masked by the adsorption process. In this work, Fe/N-SAC with DRCs characteristics was prepared and applied in internal-driven system (zero oxidant addition) and external-driven system (low PMS addition). Results indicated that the internal-driven Fe/N-SAC system almost exclusively utilized iron single atoms as the electron acceptors (iron valence declining from $+ 2 . 3 7$ to $+ 2 . 0 7$ ), and the iron single atoms in the PMS-free system would accept a greater number of electrons compared to the scenario where PMS was added and received a portion of the transferred electrons. We also observed that the iron valence of regenerated Fe/N-SAC under $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ atmosphere could be recovered from 2.07 to 2.29, which indicated that the iron atoms could also be regenerated by $\mathrm { O } _ { 2 }$ for continuous cycles. Based on the key mechanisms of the two systems, internaldriven-based column and external-driven-based amplifying ETP reactor have been designed for the long-term treatment, which could also be used for the treatment of actual WWTP effluents. These studies will replenish a new understanding on the core catalytic mechanism in the DRCs systems considering the electron transfer from pollutants to the metal species in DRCs catalysts.
